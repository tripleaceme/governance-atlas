European Union · Regulation

The GDPR — the global gold standard

The General Data Protection Regulation serves as the global "gold standard" for privacy. It emphasises Privacy by Design and grants individuals extensive rights, such as the Right to be Forgotten and Data Portability, placing a heavy burden of accountability on organisations regardless of their location.

Nigeria · National law

The NDPA 2023

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 represents a significant shift towards formalising data rights in Africa. It establishes the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) and mandates that data controllers and processors conduct regular audits and appoint Data Protection Officers.

North America · Sectoral model

A sectoral patchwork

Unlike the unified approach of the EU, the United States employs a sectoral model: federal laws like HIPAA for healthcare and GLBA for finance, alongside rigorous state-level statutes such as the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) — creating a complex compliance map for businesses.

Global · Mechanism

Cross-border data transfers

In a globalised economy, data frequently moves across borders. Frameworks must address adequacy decisions, where one nation recognises another's laws as sufficient, and Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) to ensure data remains protected when leaving its country of origin.

Principles · Ethics

Ethical governance

Beyond mere legal compliance, ethical governance involves transparency, fairness, and purpose limitation. It requires organisations to consider the societal impact of data use — ensuring that algorithms do not perpetuate bias or infringe upon human dignity.

Knowledge check · Cross-border transfers

One question, straight from a compliance meeting.

Which mechanism is primarily used to ensure legal compliance when transferring personal data from a GDPR-compliant region to a country lacking an adequacy decision?

Correct — Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are the workhorse of international transfers: pre-approved contract terms that bind the receiving party to GDPR-level protections. Adequacy decisions remove the need for them, but where none exists, SCCs are the primary mechanism.
