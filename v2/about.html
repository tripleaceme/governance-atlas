The open reference for data protection law

Privacy law shouldn't require a law degree to understand. Governance Atlas makes data protection laws easier to explore, compare, and understand.

Why this exists

If you're expanding a product into Ghana, you shouldn't have to start by reading a 120-page law. You need a clear summary of the rules, the key obligations, links to the official sources, and a way to compare them with the countries where you already operate.

Governance Atlas exists to make that information easier to find and understand, especially across Africa, where data protection laws have developed rapidly but are often harder to navigate than better-known regimes such as the GDPR.

It is for the startup founder exploring a new market, the data professional trying to understand their organisation's responsibilities, the privacy or data protection professional working across multiple jurisdictions, and anyone who wants to understand how different countries regulate personal data.

What this is not

Governance Atlas is not legal advice. The information here is intended to help you understand and explore data protection laws, not to make legal or compliance decisions.

Laws change, interpretations differ, and the rules that apply depend on your organisation and its activities. Always check the official sources linked on each country page, and seek qualified legal advice when you need to make a decision that depends on the law.

Who builds it

Governance Atlas is built by Ayoade Adegbite, a data engineer working across analytics engineering and data governance.

Contributions are welcome including corrections, country information, translations, and additional resources.

The project is open source and available on GitHub.
