About the atlas

An open reference for data protection law, built so that a non-lawyer can get oriented in minutes and a professional can verify every claim.

Why this exists

If you are expanding a product into Ghana tomorrow, you do not want to read a 120-page act. You want a trustworthy summary, the key obligations, links to the official law, and a way to compare it with the countries you already operate in. That resource did not exist — especially for Africa, where a wave of new data protection laws arrived in the last decade with far less coverage than the GDPR gets.

Governance Atlas is for the founder scoping a new market, the analytics engineer asked "are we compliant?", the DPO managing five jurisdictions, and the student trying to understand how the world regulates data.

How the data is maintained

Every country is a structured record in an open repository — the law, the regulator, the obligations, the sources, and a confidence level. The site is generated from those records, so the country pages, comparison tables and statistics can never drift apart.

Current pages are compiled from public and official sources and are pending legal review. Every page shows its confidence level and links to primary sources so you can verify before acting.

What this is not

Governance Atlas is not legal advice. Summaries are written for orientation, not for compliance decisions. Laws change, interpretations differ, and your situation is specific — always verify against the official sources linked on every page, and consult a qualified lawyer for decisions that matter.

Who builds it
