The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Guyana's Data Protection Act 2023 (No. 18 of 2023) was passed by the National Assembly on 9 August 2023 and assented by President Ali on 16 August 2023 — but the recorded status of 'in force' is wrong: the commencement order has never been issued, so the Act is not operational. The gap became conspicuous in 2026 when the Digital Identity Card Act was commenced (31 March 2026) even though the Commissioner who administers the digital ID registry is a creature of the still-dormant Data Protection Act; the EU Election Observer Mission and local commentators have pressed the government to activate the law, citing misuse of 'cash grant' personal data.

Institutional groundwork is finally moving: Aneal Giddings, a former senior GECOM official, was appointed Data Protection Commissioner in February 2026, and as of May 2026 was formally establishing the Data Protection Office. The Act itself is a modern, broadly GDPR-aligned framework: data protection principles, controller/processor duties, security obligations, 72-hour breach notification to the Commissioner (and notification of data subjects), cross-border transfer conditions based on adequacy and safeguards, and tiered administrative penalties reaching GYD 20 million for serious violations, with parallel criminal offences.

Practical read: treat Guyana as 'imminent regime'. A Commissioner exists and the state is building enforcement infrastructure around the digital ID rollout, so commencement could come quickly; organisations in Guyana's fast-growing economy should map processing and prepare breach and transfer procedures now.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Security of processing (pending) — Controllers and processors must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to secure personal data once the Act commences.
  • 02
    72-hour breach notification (pending) — Controllers must notify the Data Protection Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach, and inform affected data subjects.
  • 03
    Cross-border transfer safeguards (pending) — Transfers outside Guyana require adequacy assessment of the destination, appropriate safeguards, approvals and documentation.
  • 04
    Documentation and accountability (pending) — Failure to maintain required processing documentation attracts penalties up to GYD 5 million; unauthorized processing up to GYD 10 million; serious breaches up to GYD 20 million.
