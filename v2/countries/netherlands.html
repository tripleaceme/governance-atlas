The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Netherlands is governed directly by the EU GDPR, supplemented by the national GDPR Implementation Act (Uitvoeringswet AVG, or UAVG), both effective 25 May 2018. The UAVG fills in the areas where the GDPR leaves member states discretion — for example retaining the default age of 16 for children's online consent and setting rules for processing national identification numbers (BSN), which may only be used where a statute specifically permits it. The framework applies to any organisation established in the Netherlands and to foreign organisations targeting or monitoring people there.

The regulator, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP) in The Hague, is one of Europe's more active authorities. It has issued some of the EU's largest fines — including a €290 million penalty against Uber in 2024 over unlawful transfers of driver data to the US — and publishes extensive Dutch-language guidance on cookies, algorithms and AI. The AP also serves as one of the Netherlands' coordinating regulators for the EU AI Act, making it a key interlocutor for companies deploying automated decision-making.

Practically, Dutch compliance is standard GDPR compliance: lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs for high-risk processing, and DPO appointment where Art. 37 thresholds are met. The main Dutch-specific traps are strict BSN rules, the AP's assertive position on tracking cookies and legitimate-interest advertising, and active private litigation including collective actions under Dutch class-action law.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and accountability — Establish and document a valid GDPR legal basis for every processing purpose and be able to demonstrate compliance (Art. 5–6 GDPR).
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach, and affected individuals when there is high risk.
  • 03
    Records of processing — Maintain Art. 30 records of processing activities; no registration with the AP is required.
  • 04
    Restricted use of BSN — The Dutch citizen service number (BSN) may only be processed where a specific law authorises it — a stricter rule than the GDPR baseline.
  • 05
    DPIA and DPO where required — Carry out data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing and appoint a DPO if Art. 37 GDPR criteria are met.
