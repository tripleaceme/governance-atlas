The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Costa Rica's Law No. 8968 on the Protection of the Person regarding the Processing of their Personal Data (2011), implemented by Executive Decree 37554-JP, was a Central American pioneer. It is grounded in the constitutional right of informational self-determination and applies to automated or manual databases, public or private, used for distribution, disclosure or commercialization of personal data. Processing rests heavily on express informed consent, with strengthened rules for sensitive data.

The Agencia de Protección de Datos de los Habitantes (PRODHAB), operating since March 2013 under the Ministry of Justice and Peace, enforces the law. Distinctive pre-GDPR features include mandatory registration of commercial databases with PRODHAB before processing, a five-working-day duty to report security vulnerabilities/breaches to the agency and affected persons, and comparatively modest fines of 5-30 base salaries (about US$4,000-24,000 at the top end). There is no DPO mandate and no adequacy-based transfer regime — cross-border transfers essentially ride on consent.

Modernization is underway: Bill No. 23097 would repeal Law 8968 entirely and enact a GDPR-aligned statute with legitimate-interest processing, DPIAs, DPO requirements, a strengthened and better-funded PRODHAB, and higher fines. The Science and Technology Commission reported on the bill in January 2025 and it was readied for plenary debate, but as of mid-2026 passage has not been confirmed — so Law 8968 remains the operative regime.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Obtain express informed consent — Processing generally requires the data subject's express consent, given freely and with clear information about purposes; consent can be withdrawn.
  • 02
    Register qualifying databases with PRODHAB — Any person or entity maintaining databases for distribution, disclosure, or commercialization of personal data must register them with PRODHAB before processing begins.
  • 03
    Report security incidents within 5 days — Security vulnerabilities or breaches affecting personal data must be reported to PRODHAB and the affected data subjects within five working days.
  • 04
    Honor access, rectification and deletion rights — Data subjects may access their data and demand rectification or deletion; controllers must respond within the statutory deadlines under the regulation.
  • 05
    Maintain confidentiality and security — Adopt technical and organizational measures to guarantee the security and confidentiality of personal data, especially sensitive and restricted-access data.
