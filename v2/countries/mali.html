The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Mali's Law No. 2013-015 of 21 May 2013 on the Protection of Personal Data is a comprehensive statute in the Francophone mold, establishing the legal bases on which personal data may be collected and processed in Mali and protecting privacy as a fundamental right. It was amended by Law No. 2017-070 of December 2017, which adjusted the functioning of the supervisory authority.

The Autorité de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (APDP), launched in 2016, is among the more visibly active authorities in the Sahel: it receives declarations and authorization requests, issues warnings, formal notices and injunctions, can withdraw approvals, and can impose monetary fines ranging from XOF 2.5 million to XOF 20 million; criminal sanctions including imprisonment also back the law.

The regime predates GDPR-era concepts, so there is no general DPO mandate or 72-hour breach clock; compliance in practice centers on completing prior formalities with the APDP and responding to its supervision. Mali's political instability has not dissolved the authority, but cross-border and enforcement details should be confirmed with the APDP directly for current practice.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Declare processing to the APDP — File declarations before processing personal data and obtain authorization for sensitive categories of processing.
  • 02
    Establish a lawful basis for processing — Collect and process personal data only on the legal bases recognized by Law 2013-015, respecting purpose limitation.
  • 03
    Comply with APDP supervision — Respond to warnings, formal notices and injunctions; non-compliance can lead to withdrawal of approval and fines up to XOF 20 million.
  • 04
    Respect data subject rights — Honor rights of access, rectification and objection for individuals whose data is processed in Mali.
