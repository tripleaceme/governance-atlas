The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Sierra Leone still has no comprehensive data protection law, but the legislative process is well advanced. The Ministry of Information and Civic Education completed the final national validation of the Data Protection and Right to Access Information Bill, 2025 in November 2025, after nationwide consultations involving Parliament, local councils, security agencies, the Electoral Commission, civil society, media, academia and the private sector.

In April 2026, Cabinet approved the country's first National Data Protection Policy and authorized drafting and enactment of a combined Data Protection and Right to Access Information Act — a distinctive design that would merge data protection and access-to-information functions into a single strengthened authority (building on the existing Right to Access Information Commission). The Ministry has been directed to work with the Attorney-General's office to finalize and table the Bill in Parliament.

As of August 2026 the Bill has not been enacted. The forthcoming law is expected to impose stricter protocols on banks, mobile network operators and government agencies over citizens' financial, digital, health and tax records, and to align Sierra Leone with international data standards for trade. In the meantime, protections rest on constitutional privacy rights and sectoral rules such as the Cyber Security and Crime Act, 2021.
