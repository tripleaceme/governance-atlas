The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Somalia enacted its first comprehensive privacy statute, the Data Protection Act (Law No. 005 of 2023), signed by President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud on 21 March 2023 and effective from 23 March 2023. Modeled in significant part on modern African frameworks such as Nigeria's NDPA, it regulates collection, storage, processing and transfer of personal data across all sectors, and scales obligations by imposing the heaviest duties on 'controllers and processors of major importance'.

The Somali Data Protection Authority (DPA) was officially launched in February 2024 and is unusually active for a young regulator — publishing guidance, implementing regulations and e-services. Key obligations include 72-hour breach notification to the Authority, DPO appointment for government bodies and larger or sensitive-data processors, adequacy-or-safeguards rules for cross-border transfers, and parental consent for data of children under 18.

Penalties can reach a percentage (commentators cite 2–4%) of annual turnover, though enforcement is still building and implementing regulations continue to flesh out the regime. Somalia has no EU adequacy decision; the practical compliance picture depends heavily on DPA guidance.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers must notify the DPA within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach risking data subjects' rights and freedoms; extensions are narrowly permitted but must themselves be flagged within the 72-hour window.
  • 02
    Appoint a DPO if of major importance — Government bodies, entities processing data of over 1,000 individuals annually, or those regularly handling sensitive data as core activity must appoint a data protection officer within six months of commencing business.
  • 03
    Register with the DPA — Controllers and processors of major importance must register with the Authority and comply with its implementing regulations.
  • 04
    Safeguard cross-border transfers — Transfers require an adequate destination, appropriate safeguards (BCRs, contractual clauses), or the data subject's informed, unwithdrawn consent after disclosure of risks (Articles 30–31).
  • 05
    Protect children's data — Processing the data of anyone under 18 requires consent from a parent or legal representative.
