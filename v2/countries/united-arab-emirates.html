The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The UAE's first federal data protection law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (PDPL), has been in force since 2 January 2022. It applies to controllers and processors in the UAE (outside the financial free zones) and to those abroad processing data of UAE residents, and it follows a broadly GDPR-like architecture: lawful bases centered on consent with exceptions, data subject rights, DPO requirements for high-risk processing, breach notification to a federal Data Office, and adequacy-or-safeguards rules for international transfers. The DIFC, ADGM and Dubai Healthcare City free zones each retain their own separate data protection laws and regulators.

The regime's defining feature is its unfinished state. The Executive Regulations that must operationalize the PDPL — and the Cabinet decision fixing administrative penalties — had still not been issued as of the Chambers Data Protection & Privacy 2026 guide, which describes them as 'a work in progress,' and the UAE Data Office (created by Federal Decree-Law No. 44 of 2021) is not yet fully operational, with the TDRA and Digital Government providing transitional support. Organizations will get six months from publication of the regulations to come into compliance.

Enforcement reality is therefore limited: compliance is driven by internal risk management, sector regulators (Central Bank, health authorities, TDRA) and the active free-zone regimes rather than federal PDPL enforcement. Note that a number of 2026 marketing-oriented sources claim the Executive Regulations have now been issued and enforcement activated; this could not be verified against authoritative legal sources and should be independently confirmed.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and consent management — Process personal data only with the data subject's consent or under statutory exceptions (contract necessity, legal obligation, public interest), and be able to demonstrate and honor withdrawal of consent.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Report personal data breaches likely to pose risk to the Data Office immediately upon becoming aware, including the breach's nature, DPO contact, likely consequences and mitigation; notify affected individuals where risk to them is high.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO for high-risk processing — Controllers and processors using new large-scale technologies, systematically evaluating sensitive data (including profiling), or processing large volumes of sensitive data must appoint a Data Protection Officer.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer safeguards — Transfer personal data abroad only to adequate jurisdictions or under appropriate safeguards/exceptions; sectoral rules keep health data and payment data localized absent specific authorization.
  • 05
    Records, security and data subject rights — Maintain records of processing, implement appropriate technical and organizational measures, conduct DPIAs for high-risk processing, and honor access, correction, deletion, restriction and portability rights.
