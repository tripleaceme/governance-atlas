The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Contrary to the recorded status, Comoros does have a comprehensive data protection law: the Union of the Comoros enacted its Law on the Protection of Personal Data on 29 June 2021. The text is closely modelled on the GDPR — it applies to automated and manual processing, has extraterritorial reach over foreign entities offering goods and services to, or monitoring, people in Comoros, and requires explicit consent or another legal basis for processing.

The law establishes a National Authority for the Protection of Personal Data as an independent public body with powers to issue warnings and administrative sanctions, and requires DPOs for public authorities and for large-scale monitoring or sensitive-data processing. Controllers must notify breaches to the Authority within 72 hours, and cross-border transfers follow a three-tier adequacy/safeguards/derogations structure.

The catch is enforcement: the Authority has never been formally constituted or made operational, so the law exists on paper with no active regulator, no adequacy decisions, no registration machinery and no published sanctions. Practically, organisations face a compliance framework without a supervisor — a common pattern in smaller African jurisdictions.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and explicit consent — Personal data must be collected and processed with the data subject's explicit consent unless another legal ground applies.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers must notify the National Authority of personal data breaches within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to pose a risk to individuals.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public authorities and entities engaged in large-scale regular monitoring or sensitive-data processing must appoint a data protection officer.
  • 04
    Use approved transfer mechanisms — Cross-border transfers require an adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards such as standard contractual clauses, or a recognised derogation.
