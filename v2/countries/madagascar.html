The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Madagascar's Law No. 2014-038, adopted in December 2014 and promulgated on 9 January 2015, is a pre-GDPR, French-inspired data protection statute. It applies to automated and manual processing carried out wholly or partly on Malagasy territory, imposes classic principles (lawfulness, purpose limitation, accuracy, security, confidentiality) and relies on prior formalities — declarations and authorisations — rather than the accountability model of newer laws. It has no general breach-notification duty and limited children-specific rules.

The big development is institutional: for nearly a decade the law was a dead letter because its supervisor, the Commission Malagasy de l'Informatique et des Libertés (CMIL), did not exist. The CMIL was created by Decree 2023-1541 (December 2023), its members were sworn in before the Supreme Court on 4 August 2025, and it adopted its procedures manual in December 2025. It joined the francophone association of DPAs (AFAPDP) as a full member in November 2025, with operationalisation support from the OIF and AFAPDP.

Enforcement reality: the CMIL declares itself fully operational and holds both regulatory and sanctioning powers under the 2014 law, but it is a brand-new regulator with no enforcement track record, and the underlying statute is dated relative to GDPR-era peers. Expect guidance, registration mechanics and possibly a modernised law to develop over the next few years.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Complete prior formalities with the CMIL — Processing operations are subject to French-style prior declaration or authorisation requirements overseen by the CMIL.
  • 02
    Process data lawfully and securely — Controllers must respect lawfulness, purpose limitation, accuracy, retention and security/confidentiality obligations under the 2014 law.
  • 03
    Respect data subject rights — Individuals have rights of information, access, rectification and opposition that controllers must honour.
  • 04
    Restrict transfers to adequate countries — Personal data may only be transferred to countries ensuring an adequate level of protection, under CMIL oversight.
