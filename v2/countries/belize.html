The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Belize's Data Protection Act, 2021 (Act No. 45 of 2021), assented to on 29 November 2021, is a GDPR-modeled statute covering controllers and processors, with lawful-basis processing, data subject rights, breach notification, data protection impact assessments, and transfer restrictions. It creates a Data Protection Commissioner to administer the Act — notably structured as a public officer within the ministry responsible for digital transformation rather than an independent agency, a design choice that drew comment when the bill was introduced.

Key operational duties: notify the Commissioner of personal data breaches without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours (with reasons required for late notice, and communication to data subjects where risk is high); designate a 'data privacy officer' for public bodies and for large-scale monitoring or large-scale sensitive processing; and only transfer data outside Belize where an adequate level of protection or safeguards exist (including binding corporate rules). Children's provisions set the child threshold at under 16, with self-consent possible from over age 13. Unusually for the region, enforcement is largely criminal-offence based, with fines on summary conviction ranging from BZD 5,000–20,000 for most offences up to BZD 500,000 and/or three years' imprisonment for the most serious.

Enforcement reality is nascent: the Commissioner's office has limited public presence (no dedicated website located), no published penalty practice, and regional commentators note implementation is still maturing. The statute itself, however, is comprehensive and in force, so businesses handling Belizean data should treat the 72-hour breach clock and transfer rules as live obligations.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Process on a lawful basis — Establish consent or another lawful basis before processing; sensitive personal data carries stricter conditions, and children under 16 need parent/guardian consent unless over 13.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report personal data breaches to the Commissioner without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours, and inform affected data subjects when the breach poses high risk.
  • 03
    Designate a data privacy officer where required — Public authorities and organizations doing large-scale monitoring or large-scale sensitive-data processing must appoint a data privacy officer.
  • 04
    Restrict transfers outside Belize — Transfer personal data abroad only where the destination ensures an adequate level of protection or appropriate safeguards such as binding corporate rules are in place.
  • 05
    Bind processors by contract — Use written contracts requiring processors to act only on controller instructions and meet equivalent security obligations — non-compliance is an offence carrying fines up to BZD 500,000.
