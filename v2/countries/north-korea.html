The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

North Korea (DPRK) has no data protection or privacy law in any recognizable sense, and no supervisory authority. The state operates one of the world's most pervasive surveillance systems: personal information is collected and used by the state for control purposes, and there are no independent bodies, judicial remedies or legal mechanisms through which citizens could challenge the handling of their data.

The legislation that does touch information is oriented toward state control rather than individual rights — for example, the Law on Protection of State Secrets adopted in 2023, which criminalizes leakage of classified information and further restricts citizens' access to and dissemination of information. Enforcement is carried out by the Ministry of State Security without oversight, with penalties that can include imprisonment, forced labour or execution.

For any organization, the question of DPRK data compliance is academic: foreign businesses do not process North Korean personal data under local law, and comprehensive international sanctions bar most commercial activity. There is no constitutional or statutory privacy protection with practical effect, no DPO, registration, breach or transfer rules, and no realistic prospect of data protection legislation.
