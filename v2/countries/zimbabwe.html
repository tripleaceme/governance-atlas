The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Zimbabwe's Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07], enacted in December 2021 and effective from 11 March 2022, is a hybrid statute combining cybercrime offences with a data protection regime. The Postal and Telecommunications Regulatory Authority of Zimbabwe (POTRAZ) doubles as the Data Protection Authority. The Act covers lawful processing, data security, sensitive data, breach notification and cross-border transfers, though its data-subject rights are narrower than the GDPR's.

The regime hardened considerably with Statutory Instrument 155 of 2024 (Cyber and Data Protection — Licensing of Data Controllers and Appointment of Data Protection Officers — Regulations), promulgated 13 September 2024. Every data controller must obtain a data protection licence from POTRAZ (applications were due within six months, by 12 March 2025) and appoint a POTRAZ-certified DPO. Breach notification is unusually tight: 24 hours to POTRAZ, with high-risk breaches communicated to affected individuals within 72 hours.

Enforcement reality: POTRAZ has been actively driving licensing and DPO certification since 2024–25, making Zimbabwe one of the more operationally demanding regimes in the region. Penalties are criminal (fines up to level 11 on the standard scale and/or up to seven years' imprisonment for serious offences). Civil-society observers have raised concerns that the Act's cybercrime provisions can be used against journalists and critics, so the law cuts both ways politically.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Obtain a POTRAZ data protection licence — Under SI 155 of 2024, all data controllers must apply to POTRAZ for a data protection licence (Form DP1); the initial application deadline was 12 March 2025.
  • 02
    Appoint a certified DPO — Every data controller must appoint a Data Protection Officer certified through POTRAZ-recognised training.
  • 03
    Report breaches within 24 hours — Security breaches must be notified to POTRAZ within 24 hours of awareness, and to affected individuals within 72 hours where there is high risk.
  • 04
    Clear transfers abroad — Controllers must notify POTRAZ and ensure adequate protection before transferring personal data outside Zimbabwe, including to foreign cloud providers, or rely on explicit consent/contractual necessity.
  • 05
    Secure and lawfully process data — Controllers must process data lawfully and implement security measures, with criminal liability for unlawful processing and disclosure.
