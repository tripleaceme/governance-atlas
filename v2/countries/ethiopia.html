The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Ethiopia's first comprehensive privacy law, the Personal Data Protection Proclamation No. 1321/2024, was approved by Parliament on 4 April 2024 and entered into force in mid-2024. It is broadly GDPR-inspired — lawful bases, data-subject rights, DPOs, 72-hour breach notification — but with distinctly Ethiopian features: a data-localisation requirement obliging controllers to store locally collected personal data on servers or data centres in Ethiopia, and prior regulator approval for cross-border transfers of sensitive data.

Supervision was assigned to the Ethiopian Communications Authority (ECA) rather than a standalone privacy regulator. The ECA maintains the register of controllers and processors, investigates complaints, issues enforcement notices and can impose administrative fines; the Proclamation also carries graduated criminal penalties, rising to five-to-ten years' imprisonment and fines of ETB 200,000–600,000 for unlawful international transfers. Minors are defined as under 16, and their data requires parental or guardian consent.

Enforcement reality lags the text: a year after entry into force, reporting described a slow path to operationalisation, with implementing directives, registration machinery and ECA capacity still being built. Companies serving the Ethiopian market should nonetheless plan for registration, DPO appointment, the localisation rule and the 72-hour breach clock, as the ECA has been holding implementation workshops and is expected to ramp up.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Register with the ECA — Data controllers and processors must register with the Ethiopian Communications Authority, which maintains the national register and oversees compliance.
  • 02
    Store local data in Ethiopia — Personal data collected or obtained in Ethiopia must be stored on servers or data centres located in the country; cross-border transfer of sensitive data requires prior ECA approval.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Data breaches must be reported to the ECA within 72 hours of the controller becoming aware of them.
  • 04
    Appoint a Data Protection Officer — Controllers and processors must appoint a DPO, except small businesses that do not engage in large-scale processing or handle sensitive data.
  • 05
    Protect minors' data — Processing data of children under 16 requires parental or guardian consent and must prioritise the child's rights, including age-verification expectations.
