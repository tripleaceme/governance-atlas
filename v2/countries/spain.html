The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Spain applies the GDPR directly, supplemented by Organic Law 3/2018 (LOPDGDD), which goes further than most member-state laws: beyond filling GDPR opening clauses it adds a catalogue of "digital rights" — digital disconnection at work, device privacy for employees, digital education, and rights concerning deceased persons' data. The digital consent age is set at 14, and Article 34 makes DPO appointment mandatory for 16 listed sectors (including banks, insurers, health centres, schools, telecoms and gambling operators) regardless of size, with appointments notified to the AEPD within 10 days.

The regulator is the AEPD, consistently among the most active supervisory authorities in Europe by volume of resolutions and fines, with major penalties against banks, telecoms and utilities. Spain also uses statutory presumptions (e.g. blocklist rules, video surveillance and workplace monitoring provisions in the LOPDGDD) that give enforcement a distinctly codified flavour compared with other member states.

Compliance is standard GDPR — lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs, Chapter V transfers — plus the Spanish extras: check whether your sector triggers mandatory DPO designation, honour employees' digital rights, and apply the age-14 rule for minors' consent.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Appoint and notify a DPO if in a listed sector — LOPDGDD Art. 34 mandates DPOs for 16 sectors regardless of size; appointments and removals must be notified to the AEPD within 10 days.
  • 02
    Lawful basis and transparency — All processing needs a GDPR Art. 6 basis with clear privacy information; LOPDGDD codifies rules for credit blocklists, video surveillance and advertising exclusion lists.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report personal data breaches to the AEPD within 72 hours; inform affected individuals when risk is high.
  • 04
    Respect digital rights at work — Employers must honour statutory rights to digital disconnection and privacy in workplace devices, geolocation and surveillance.
  • 05
    Apply the age-14 consent rule — Minors can consent to data processing from 14; younger children require consent of parental authority holders.
