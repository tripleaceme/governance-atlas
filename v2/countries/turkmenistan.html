The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Turkmenistan's data protection framework rests on Law No. 519-V 'On Information about Private Life and its Protection', adopted on 20 March 2017 and in force since 1 July 2017. It is the country's only law dedicated to the collection and processing of personal data, and it partially mirrors principles familiar from the GDPR — lawfulness, purpose limitation, consent as the main processing basis, and confidentiality/security duties on operators of personal data databases.

The most distinctive feature is what is missing: there is no dedicated supervisory authority. Oversight sits generally with the Cabinet of Ministers, with the Prosecutor General's Office responsible for supervision of legality. There is no database registration requirement, no data protection officer requirement, and no breach notification regime. Cross-border transfers are contemplated where the receiving state ensures protection of the data.

In practice, enforcement is essentially invisible: no public enforcement actions, guidance, or case law are available, and the state itself operates pervasive surveillance with tight information controls. Companies dealing with Turkmenistan should treat the law as a formal baseline with little practical regulatory activity, and rely on consent and contractual protections.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Consent-based processing — Collection and processing of information about private life generally requires the data subject's consent, with limited statutory exceptions.
  • 02
    Confidentiality and security — Operators and third parties must keep personal data confidential and apply legal, organizational and technical measures against unauthorized access, alteration or destruction.
  • 03
    Purpose limitation — Data may only be collected for defined, lawful purposes and must not be used in ways incompatible with those purposes.
  • 04
    Cross-border care — Transfers abroad are contemplated only where the receiving state ensures protection of personal data; otherwise transfers can be restricted.
