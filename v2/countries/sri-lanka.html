The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 made it the first South Asian country to enact comprehensive, GDPR-style privacy legislation. It applies to controllers and processors in Sri Lanka and to those abroad offering goods or services to, or monitoring, people in Sri Lanka. The Act creates familiar duties — lawful bases for processing, data subject rights, DPO appointment for higher-risk processing, breach notification, and controlled cross-border transfers — overseen by a dedicated Data Protection Authority (DPA), established in 2023.

The critical caveat is timing: the Act is being commenced in phases. The institutional parts (establishing the DPA and its administration) are in force, but the substantive obligations in Parts I–III were postponed — the original 18 March 2025 commencement was repealed, and the Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act No. 22 of 2025 pushed the timeline back while the DPA completes staffing, rules and guidance. As of mid-2026, no Gazette Order has yet brought the core obligations fully into operation, so day-to-day enforcement against controllers has not really begun.

For a meeting, the practical framing is: the law exists and the regulator is real and issuing circulars (e.g. on DPOs), but compliance deadlines are still crystallizing, with full effect expected during 2026. Penalties, once live, run to LKR 10 million per directive breach, doubling for repeat offences.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Process on a lawful basis — Personal data may only be processed on grounds set out in the Act (consent, contract, legal obligation, legitimate interests, etc.), with stricter conditions for special categories.
  • 02
    Honor data subject rights — Controllers must give effect to rights of access, rectification, erasure, withdrawal of consent and objection, and respond within prescribed timeframes once the relevant Parts are in operation.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Controllers and processors meeting prescribed conditions must appoint a Data Protection Officer to advise on compliance and impact assessments.
  • 04
    Report breaches to the DPA — Personal data breaches must be notified to the DPA and affected data subjects in accordance with rules the Authority is finalizing.
  • 05
    Safeguard cross-border transfers — Transfers outside Sri Lanka require an adequacy designation, appropriate safeguards, or another statutory ground, with added flexibility introduced by the 2025 amendments.
