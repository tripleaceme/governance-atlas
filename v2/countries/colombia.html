The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Colombia has a well-established, actively enforced data protection regime rooted in the constitutional right of habeas data (Art. 15). Statutory Law 1581 of 2012, with implementing Decree 1377 of 2013 (and Law 1266 of 2008 for financial/credit data), governs processing of personal data by public and private controllers and processors in Colombia and, in some cases, processing abroad of Colombians' data. It is consent-centric: prior, express and informed authorization is the default basis, with special protection for sensitive data and children's data.

The supervisory authority is the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio (SIC) through its Delegatura for Personal Data Protection — one of Latin America's most active DPAs, with a steady record of investigations and fines against banks, retailers, telecoms and platforms. Distinctive compliance features include the National Database Registry (RNBD), where companies above an asset threshold (100,000 UVT) and non-profits must register their databases, and a duty to report security incidents to the SIC within 15 business days. Fines reach 2,000 current monthly minimum wages (several billion pesos), and the SIC can also suspend or shut down processing operations. Cross-border transfers require an adequate destination per SIC Circular 005 of 2017, data subject consent, or a declaration of conformity.

Enforcement is real and routine — Colombia is not a paper regime. Individuals can also enforce habeas data directly through tutela actions in the courts. Companies familiar with GDPR should note the differences: consent-first logic, the RNBD registration and incident-reporting mechanics, and the local accountability figure requirement.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Obtain prior, express, informed authorization — Consent is the default lawful basis; controllers must be able to prove authorization for each processing purpose and maintain a compliant privacy policy and notice.
  • 02
    Register databases in the RNBD — Private companies with total assets above 100,000 UVT and non-profits must register their databases with the SIC's National Database Registry and keep entries updated.
  • 03
    Report security incidents within 15 business days — Controllers and processors must report data security incidents to the SIC within 15 business days of detection, via the RNBD or the SIC's channels.
  • 04
    Honour habeas data rights — Individuals may know, access, update, rectify and delete their data and revoke authorization; controllers must run complaint/petition procedures with statutory response times.
  • 05
    Restrict international transfers — Transfers are permitted only to adequate jurisdictions per SIC Circular 005/2017, with data subject consent, or under a declaration of conformity or other legal exception.
