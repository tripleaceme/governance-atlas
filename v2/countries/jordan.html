The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Jordan's Personal Data Protection Law No. 24 of 2023 is the kingdom's first comprehensive data protection statute. Published in September 2023, it became effective on 17 March 2024, with a one-year transition ending 17 March 2025 — so all controllers and processors, public and private, are now expected to be fully compliant. The law is broadly GDPR-inspired: consent-centric processing, data subject rights (access, correction, erasure, objection), sensitive-data safeguards, and accountability duties.

Institutionally, Jordan chose a ministry-anchored model rather than a fully independent regulator: a Personal Data Protection Council chaired by the Minister of Digital Economy and Entrepreneurship sets policy, while a Data Protection Unit within MoDEE handles complaints, compliance inspections, and a registry of controllers and processors. DPO appointment is mandatory for a fairly wide band of organizations — those whose core activities involve personal or sensitive data, data of incapacitated persons, financial data, or transfers abroad.

Enforcement combines administrative fines (JOD 1,000–10,000, plus daily fines for continuing breaches capped at 3% of annual revenue) with judicial penalties that double for repeat offenses. Cross-border transfers are restrictive on paper — generally requiring an adequate level of protection or informed consent. As the regime only recently exited its transition period, published enforcement practice is still limited; expect the Unit's early activity to focus on registration and complaint handling.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and explicit consent — Processing requires prior, documented, specific consent or another statutory ground; sensitive data (including children's and financial data) demands heightened protection.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the Data Protection Unit within 72 hours of a qualifying breach and affected individuals within 24 hours where significant harm is likely.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where triggered — Controllers whose core activities involve personal/sensitive data, incapacitated persons' data, financial data, or cross-border transfers must appoint a data protection officer.
  • 04
    Register with the Unit — Controllers and processors are recorded in the registry maintained by MoDEE's Data Protection Unit.
  • 05
    Restrict cross-border transfers — Do not transfer personal data outside Jordan without an adequate protection level, informed consent, or a narrow statutory exception.
