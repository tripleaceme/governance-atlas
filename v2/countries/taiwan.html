The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) is one of Asia's older comprehensive privacy laws — enacted in 1995 as the Computer-Processed Personal Data Protection Act, renamed and broadened in 2010, and in force since 2012. It applies to both government and non-government entities processing personal data of natural persons, covers all sectors, and grants data subjects rights of access, correction, deletion and objection. Historically its biggest weakness was fragmented enforcement: each sector ministry policed the Act within its own domain, with no central regulator.

That changed after a 2022 Constitutional Court ruling required an independent supervisory mechanism. A 2023 amendment raised fines (to NT$15 million for repeated security failures) and created a Preparatory Office of the Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC), which has operated since December 2023. Major amendments promulgated on 11 November 2025 formally establish the PDPC as an independent, centralized authority with interpretive, inspection and enforcement powers, mandate DPOs for government agencies, introduce breach reporting to the authority (a 72-hour regime), and move cross-border transfer restriction powers to the PDPC. The effective date of the 2025 amendments will be set by the Executive Yuan.

For businesses, Taiwan is a moderate-risk but tightening jurisdiction: fines were traditionally low, but the new PDPC, higher penalties, and mandatory breach reporting signal a shift toward GDPR-style centralized enforcement. Cross-border transfers remain generally free unless specifically restricted.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Notice and consent or legal basis — Collect and process personal data only for specified purposes with notice to the data subject, based on consent or another statutory basis; sensitive data (medical, genetic, criminal records, etc.) is subject to stricter conditions.
  • 02
    Security safeguards — Adopt appropriate technical and organizational security measures to prevent personal data from being stolen, altered, damaged, destroyed or disclosed; repeated failure to remedy can draw fines up to NT$15 million.
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify affected data subjects of breaches; under the 2025 amendments, entities must also report incidents to the supervisory authority within 72 hours once the new rules take effect.
  • 04
    Honor data subject rights — Provide access, copies, correction, cessation of processing and deletion on request; these rights cannot be waived in advance.
  • 05
    Government-agency DPOs — Government agencies must appoint Data Protection Officers to supervise compliance and act as contact points for the PDPC and data subjects (2025 amendments).
