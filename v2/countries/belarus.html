The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Belarus's first comprehensive privacy statute, Law No. 99-Z of 7 May 2021 on Personal Data Protection (PDPL), entered into force on 15 November 2021. Broadly inspired by the GDPR and Russian-style data laws, it applies to state bodies, legal entities and individuals ('operators') processing personal data by automated or structured manual means, establishes consent as the primary legal basis with a closed list of exceptions, grants data subjects rights of access, correction, deletion and withdrawal of consent, and requires operators to adopt legal, organisational and technical protection measures, including a designated internal-control person or unit, staff training, and a published processing policy.

Supervision belongs to the National Personal Data Protection Center (NPDPC), created by Presidential Edict No. 422 of 28 October 2021 and operational since 15 November 2021. The NPDPC handles complaints, conducts inspections, approves cross-border transfers to countries lacking adequate protection (adequacy is generally recognised for Convention 108 parties and EAEU members), and issues clarifications. Breaches of protection systems must be reported to the NPDPC immediately and no later than three business days.

Enforcement runs through the Administrative Offences Code (Arts. 23.7, 24.11), with fines that are modest by GDPR standards — up to around 200 base units — plus criminal liability under Arts. 203-1 and 203-2 of the Criminal Code carrying up to three years' imprisonment for intentional unlawful collection or dissemination. Compensation for moral harm is available without proof of material loss. Belarus holds no EU adequacy decision, so exports of EU personal data to Belarus require SCCs or another GDPR transfer mechanism.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Obtain valid consent — Consent is the default legal basis and must be informed, specific and documented; a closed statutory list of exceptions allows processing without consent.
  • 02
    Implement protection measures and internal control — Adopt legal, organisational and technical measures, appoint a person or unit responsible for internal control of processing, train staff, and publish a processing policy (Art. 17).
  • 03
    Report breaches within 3 business days — Notify the NPDPC of personal data protection system breaches immediately and no later than three business days after discovery.
  • 04
    Clear cross-border transfers — Transfers to countries without adequate protection are prohibited unless the NPDPC issues a permit, the data subject consents, or another statutory exception applies.
  • 05
    Honour data subject rights — Respond to access, correction, deletion and consent-withdrawal requests within statutory deadlines (generally 15 days for information requests).
