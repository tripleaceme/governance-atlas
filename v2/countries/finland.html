The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Finland is governed directly by the GDPR, specified and supplemented by the national Data Protection Act (1050/2018), which entered into force on 1 January 2019 and replaced the earlier Personal Data Act. The Act exercises GDPR opening clauses on matters such as the processing of personal identity codes, journalistic and academic exemptions, and sets the digital age of consent at 13.

Supervision follows an ombudsman model: the Data Protection Ombudsman and their Office (tietosuoja.fi) act as the national supervisory authority. A distinctive Finnish feature is that administrative fines are not imposed by the Ombudsman alone but by a collegial sanctions board within the office, and public-sector bodies are exempt from administrative fines altogether — a notable divergence from many EU peers.

In practice, compliance in Finland is GDPR compliance: lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs and Chapter V transfer safeguards, with additional care needed around Finnish personal identity codes and employee data (governed by the separate Act on the Protection of Privacy in Working Life).

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Comply with full GDPR obligations — Lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, records of processing, DPIAs and accountability apply directly under the GDPR as specified by the Data Protection Act (1050/2018).
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches posing a risk to individuals must be reported to the Data Protection Ombudsman within 72 hours.
  • 03
    Handle personal identity codes lawfully — Finnish personal identity codes may only be processed on the specific grounds set out in the Data Protection Act, such as consent or where unambiguous identification is important.
  • 04
    Appoint a DPO where required — Mandatory for public authorities and organisations with large-scale monitoring or special-category processing as core activities.
  • 05
    Obtain parental consent under 13 — Online services relying on consent must obtain parental authorisation for children under 13.
