The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Jamaica's Data Protection Act, 2020 (DPA) is a comprehensive, GDPR-inspired law passed in June 2020 and fully in effect since 1 December 2023 after a two-year transition. It applies to public and private data controllers established in Jamaica or using equipment there, and is built around eight data protection standards covering fair and lawful processing, purpose limitation, accuracy, retention, data subject rights, security, and restrictions on overseas transfers.

The Office of the Information Commissioner (OIC), operational since December 2021, supervises the regime. Distinctive features include mandatory registration of data controllers with the OIC (with annual fees), a strict 72-hour breach notification duty, and criminal — not just administrative — liability: individuals face fines up to JMD 5 million and up to 10 years' imprisonment, while corporate offenders can be fined up to 4% of annual gross worldwide turnover, one of the toughest sanction ceilings in the Caribbean.

Enforcement is in its early phase: the OIC has focused on registration drives, guidance, and breach-response supervision since the compliance deadline. Organizations doing business with Jamaica should confirm registration status, appoint a data protection officer where required, and have a tested 72-hour breach-response process.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Register with the OIC — Data controllers must register with the Office of the Information Commissioner and renew annually; processing personal data without registration is a criminal offence.
  • 02
    Comply with the eight data protection standards — Process personal data fairly and lawfully, for specified purposes, keeping it accurate, adequate, secure and no longer than necessary, and respecting data subject rights.
  • 03
    Report breaches within 72 hours — Notify the Information Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of a security breach and inform affected data subjects; failure to report is an offence under the Act.
  • 04
    Appoint a data protection officer where required — Public authorities and controllers processing sensitive or criminal-conviction data or processing on a large scale must designate a DPO to oversee compliance.
  • 05
    Restrict overseas transfers — Do not transfer personal data outside Jamaica unless the destination ensures an adequate level of protection or a statutory exception applies.
