The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Poland is governed by the EU GDPR, supplemented by the Act of 10 May 2018 on the Protection of Personal Data, which took effect on 25 May 2018 and replaced the 1997 act. The 2018 Act contains few substantive derogations from the GDPR: its main role is establishing the supervisory authority (UODO, replacing the former GIODO), setting procedural rules for inspections and administrative proceedings, capping fines on public bodies at PLN 100,000, and retaining the GDPR default age of 16 for children's online consent. A companion 'sectoral implementation' act of 2019 amended over 160 other statutes, including significant employer rules on employee monitoring and recruitment data.

The President of UODO is appointed by the Sejm for a four-year term and runs an active enforcement programme: annual inspection plans, published decisions, and fines against both private companies and public institutions. Decisions can be appealed to the administrative courts, which have overturned several UODO fines, so enforcement is genuinely contested.

In practice, compliance in Poland is standard GDPR work — lawful basis, transparency in Polish, 72-hour breach reporting, DPIAs against UODO's published high-risk list — with local specifics around employee monitoring notice requirements and the obligation to notify DPO appointments to UODO electronically.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    GDPR compliance baseline — Apply the GDPR in full: lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, and accountability documentation.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Report personal data breaches to UODO within 72 hours; UODO provides an online notification channel.
  • 03
    Notify DPO appointments — Where a DPO is required under Art. 37 GDPR, notify the appointment and contact details to UODO within 14 days.
  • 04
    Employee monitoring rules — CCTV and email monitoring of employees is permitted only under Labour Code conditions with prior notice to staff.
  • 05
    DPIA against UODO's list — Conduct data protection impact assessments for processing types on UODO's published high-risk list.
