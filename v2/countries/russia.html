The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Russia's Federal Law No. 152-FZ 'On Personal Data', enacted in 2006, is a comprehensive consent-centric regime that has been amended repeatedly and now anchors one of the world's most restrictive data governance systems. It applies to any 'operator' processing personal data of Russian citizens, and its most distinctive features are mandatory data localization (databases with Russians' personal data must be located in Russia — a rule tightened from 1 July 2025 to cover processors as well), mandatory notification of Roskomnadzor before processing begins, and a separate prior notification before any cross-border transfer, with transfers to some jurisdictions subject to restriction.

Enforcement has hardened sharply since 2022. Breaches must be reported to Roskomnadzor within 24 hours (with a 72-hour detailed follow-up). Federal Law 420-FZ, in force since 30 May 2025, introduced turnover-based fines: data leaks now attract fines of RUB 3–15 million depending on the number of affected individuals, and repeat leak violations can cost up to 3% of annual turnover, capped at RUB 500 million. Parallel Federal Law 421-FZ created criminal liability — up to four years' imprisonment — for illegal collection, use or transfer of personal data, with harsher sentences involving minors' or biometric data.

Roskomnadzor is an assertive regulator with website-blocking powers (LinkedIn has been blocked since 2016 over localization). Russia holds no EU adequacy decision, so GDPR-covered transfers into Russia require safeguards, while outbound compliance for foreign companies serving Russian users is dominated by localization, notification and consent formalities.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Localize Russian citizens' data — Personal data of Russian citizens must be recorded, stored and processed in databases located in Russia; from 1 July 2025 this obligation extends to processors acting for operators.
  • 02
    Notify Roskomnadzor before processing — Operators must file a processing notification with Roskomnadzor before starting to process personal data; RKN maintains a public register of operators.
  • 03
    Notify cross-border transfers in advance — A separate notification to Roskomnadzor is required before commencing cross-border transfers, and transfers to certain countries may be restricted or refused.
  • 04
    Report breaches within 24/72 hours — Data leaks must be reported to Roskomnadzor within 24 hours, with a detailed follow-up report within 72 hours; failure carries fines of RUB 1–3m for legal entities.
  • 05
    Obtain specific, documented consent — Processing generally requires specific, informed consent — with separate, stand-alone consent for publicly disseminated data and strict rules for biometric data.
