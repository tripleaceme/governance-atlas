The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Italy applies the GDPR directly, supplemented by the Personal Data Protection Code (Legislative Decree 196/2003, the "Codice Privacy"), which was overhauled by Legislative Decree 101/2018 to align with the GDPR rather than being repealed. The Code sets the digital consent age at 14, keeps criminal offences for unlawful processing and fraudulent acquisition of data, preserves sector rules (employment, health, journalism, electronic communications), and keeps in force many of the Garante's pre-GDPR general authorisations and codes of conduct.

The regulator, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, is one of Europe's most assertive: it issued Europe's early landmark actions on generative AI (temporary limitation and fine against OpenAI/ChatGPT), fined Clearview AI, runs aggressive cookie and telemarketing enforcement, and treats age verification as a substantive compliance duty for online and AI services. Telemarketing abuses have drawn some of Italy's largest fines.

Compliance is standard GDPR — lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs, DPOs where triggered — with Italian distinctives: the age-14 rule, criminal exposure under the Code, strict Garante positions on employee monitoring and metadata (e.g. email retention), and detailed telemarketing/cookie rules.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and transparency — All processing needs a GDPR Art. 6 basis with clear privacy information; Garante measures and codes of conduct supplement the rules in key sectors.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report personal data breaches to the Garante within 72 hours; inform affected individuals when risk is high.
  • 03
    Apply the age-14 consent rule and verify age — Minors can consent to information-society services from 14; the Garante increasingly expects effective age verification, including for AI services.
  • 04
    Mind criminal liability — The Privacy Code retains criminal offences for unlawful data processing and fraudulent data acquisition, on top of administrative fines.
  • 05
    Follow strict marketing and workplace rules — Telemarketing requires clean opt-in consent chains, and employee monitoring is constrained by the Code and the Workers' Statute.
