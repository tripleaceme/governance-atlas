The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Guinea-Bissau has no data protection law and no supervisory authority, making it one of the very few ECOWAS member states without such legislation. It also lacks adjacent frameworks such as comprehensive cybercrime or electronic transactions laws, so there is no operative statutory regime governing how organisations collect or process personal data.

The main protections are constitutional and regional: the Constitution enshrines rights to privacy and, by extension, personal data protection, but without implementing legislation the direct applicability of these rights to data processing disputes is unclear. As an ECOWAS member, Guinea-Bissau is also covered in principle by the 2010 ECOWAS Supplementary Act on Personal Data Protection, though this has no practical domestic enforcement machinery.

Drafting of a national data protection law has reportedly been initiated, aiming to regulate processing and establish a legal and institutional framework, but as of mid-2026 no bill has advanced to enactment. Political instability — including the 2025 coup — makes near-term legislative progress uncertain. Businesses operating there typically apply regional or contractual standards voluntarily.
