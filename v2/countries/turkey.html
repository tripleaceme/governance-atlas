The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Turkey's Law No. 6698 on the Protection of Personal Data (KVKK), in force since 2016, was modelled on the pre-GDPR EU Directive but was substantially modernised by the March 2024 amendments (Law 7499, the '8th Judicial Reform Package'), effective June 2024. The law applies to anyone processing personal data of individuals in Turkey and is enforced by the Personal Data Protection Authority and its decision-making Board in Ankara.

Turkey's most distinctive feature is VERBIS, the mandatory public registry of data controllers: Turkish controllers above employee/turnover thresholds and all foreign controllers processing Turkish personal data must register and appoint a contact person (Turkey does not mandate a GDPR-style DPO). The 2024 reform overhauled the previously restrictive cross-border transfer regime — which had effectively required explicit consent or Board permission — replacing it with a GDPR-like structure of adequacy decisions, Turkish Standard Contractual Clauses (which must be filed with the Authority within five business days of signing), and Binding Corporate Rules. It also expanded the legal bases for processing sensitive data, notably for employment purposes.

Enforcement is vigorous and fines are indexed annually for inflation: 2026 amounts reach TRY 17,092,242 per violation, with VERBIS failures alone drawing fines from TRY 341,809 upward. Breach notification to the Board is due within 72 hours under Board decision 2019/10, plus notification to affected individuals. Multinationals should treat Turkey as a distinct compliance regime — GDPR compliance helps but does not cover VERBIS registration, Turkish-language SCC filing, or local sensitive-data rules.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    VERBIS registration — Register with the VERBIS data controllers' registry and appoint a contact person before processing, if you exceed the thresholds or are a foreign controller processing Turkish data.
  • 02
    Explicit consent or legal basis — Process personal data only with explicit consent or another enumerated legal basis; sensitive data has its own stricter basis list expanded in 2024.
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify the Personal Data Protection Board within 72 hours of learning of a breach and inform affected data subjects.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer compliance — Transfer data abroad only via adequacy, Turkish SCCs (filed with the Authority within 5 business days), BCRs, or statutory exceptions under the June 2024 regime.
  • 05
    Disclosure (aydınlatma) obligation — Inform data subjects at collection about the controller's identity, purposes, recipients, and their rights, in line with the Authority's communiqué.
