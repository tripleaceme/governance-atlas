The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Sudan has no comprehensive data protection law and no data protection authority. A Draft Data Protection Bill was proposed in 2018 to set out rules for the collection, storage, processing and sharing of personal information, but it never advanced to enactment, and the legislative process has been effectively frozen — first by the 2021 coup and then by the civil war that broke out in April 2023 and was still ongoing as of 2026.

What exists instead is a patchwork of sectoral instruments: the Electronic Transactions Act 2007, the Cybercrime Act 2007 and the Cybercrime Prevention (Amendment) Act 2020, plus informational-crimes provisions. These laws criminalize certain misuse of computer systems and communications but do not create data-subject rights, controller obligations, or a supervisory regime. Constitutional privacy guarantees under the 2019 Constitutional Document are of limited practical effect given the collapse of transitional institutions.

For any organization handling Sudanese personal data, the practical position is that there are no registration, DPO, breach-notification or transfer rules to comply with domestically — but also no legal certainty. Expect no near-term movement on a data protection statute while the conflict continues.
