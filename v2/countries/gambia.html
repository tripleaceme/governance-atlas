The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Gambia enacted its first comprehensive privacy statute in 2025: the Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act was passed unanimously by the National Assembly on 29 September 2025 and assented to by President Adama Barrow on 7 November 2025 — so the country has moved from 'bill in progress' to having a law in force. The Act was developed with Council of Europe support and aligns with GDPR-style international practice: lawful bases, data-subject rights, 72-hour breach notification, DPOs, and turnover-based fines up to 4% of annual global turnover.

Rather than creating a brand-new agency, the Act vests supervision in the existing Information Commission, established under the Access to Information Act 2021, giving it a dual access-to-information and data protection mandate with powers to investigate complaints, issue guidelines and impose administrative fines. Criminal sanctions range from GMD 500,000 for unlawful processing up to GMD 10 million and 10 years' imprisonment for aggravated offences such as unlawful data sales.

Distinctive points: children are defined as under 18 with parental consent generally required, cross-border transfers need adequacy or Commission-adopted safeguards with documented transfer assessments, and DPOs are mandatory for public authorities and large-scale monitoring or sensitive processing. Enforcement is nascent — the Commission is still standing up its data protection function — but the compliance clock has started for organisations processing Gambian data.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers must notify the Information Commission within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach and inform affected individuals without undue delay where there is substantial risk.
  • 02
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public authorities and organisations engaged in large-scale monitoring or sensitive-data processing must appoint a Data Protection Officer.
  • 03
    Justify cross-border transfers — Transfers require adequacy in the destination or appropriate safeguards under instruments adopted by the Commission, and controllers must document their transfer assessments.
  • 04
    Obtain parental consent for children — Persons under 18 are children under the Act; processing generally requires parental or guardian consent, and information addressed to minors must be in clear, plain language.
  • 05
    Process lawfully and transparently — Processing requires a lawful basis and respect for data-subject rights, backed by administrative fines up to 4% of global annual turnover for serious violations.
