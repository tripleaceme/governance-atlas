The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

As an EU member state, Sweden is governed directly by the GDPR, supplemented by the Data Protection Act (2018:218) — 'the Act with supplementary provisions to the EU GDPR' (dataskyddslagen) — and the Data Protection Ordinance (2018:219), both in force since 25 May 2018. The national law exercises GDPR opening clauses, setting the digital age of consent at 13, regulating personal identity numbers, and preserving Sweden's strong constitutional protections for freedom of expression and press, which carve journalistic and constitutionally protected publishing out of much of the regime.

The supervisory authority is IMY (Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten, the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection), renamed from the Data Inspection Board (Datainspektionen) in January 2021. Sweden has one of the world's oldest data protection traditions — its first national data act dates to 1973 — and IMY actively audits, handles complaints and issues administrative fines, with a distinctive national cap of SEK 10 million for fines against public authorities.

Compliance for organisations operating in Sweden is essentially GDPR compliance: lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs, and Chapter V safeguards for transfers outside the EU/EEA.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Comply with full GDPR obligations — Lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, records of processing, DPIAs and accountability apply directly under the GDPR as supplemented by dataskyddslagen.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches posing a risk to individuals must be reported to IMY within 72 hours.
  • 03
    Handle national identity numbers carefully — Swedish personal identity numbers (personnummer) may only be processed with consent or where clearly justified by the purpose, per the Data Protection Act.
  • 04
    Appoint a DPO where required — Mandatory for public authorities and organisations with large-scale monitoring or special-category processing as core activities.
  • 05
    Obtain parental consent under 13 — Online services relying on consent must obtain parental authorisation for children under 13.
