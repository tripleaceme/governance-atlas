The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Portugal applies the GDPR directly, executed nationally by Law No. 58/2019 of 8 August. The national law sets the digital consent age at 13 — the lowest the GDPR permits — and requires that parental consent for younger children be collected through secure authentication means such as the Citizen's Card. It also contains Portuguese specifics on employee data, health data, deceased persons' data, and video surveillance, and structures fines into national bands within GDPR ceilings.

Supervision belongs to the CNPD (Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados). A notable quirk: shortly after Law 58/2019 passed, the CNPD publicly deliberated that it would disapply several of its provisions it considered incompatible with the GDPR's primacy — so practitioners rely on the GDPR text over the national law where they conflict. The law also allowed public entities a temporary waiver from fines, reflecting a historically softer enforcement posture toward the public sector.

Enforcement has grown steadily, with the CNPD issuing fines on video surveillance, municipalities and data-subject-rights failures, and intervening prominently on international transfers (notably ordering suspension of transfers in the Census 2021 Cloudflare case). Day-to-day compliance is standard GDPR with the age-13 rule and secure parental authentication as Portuguese distinctives.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and transparency — All processing needs a GDPR Art. 6 basis with clear privacy information; where Law 58/2019 conflicts with the GDPR, the GDPR prevails.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report personal data breaches to the CNPD within 72 hours; inform affected individuals when risk is high.
  • 03
    Verify parental consent securely — Consent-based services for under-13s require parental consent gathered via secure authentication (e.g. Citizen's Card, Digital Mobile Key).
  • 04
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public bodies and organisations doing large-scale monitoring or special-category processing must designate a DPO.
  • 05
    Frame international transfers — Transfers outside the EU/EEA require adequacy, SCCs or another Chapter V safeguard, with the CNPD active on transfer suspensions.
