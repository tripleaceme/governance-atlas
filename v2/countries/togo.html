The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Togo's Law No. 2019-014 of 29 October 2019 is a GDPR-inspired framework governing the collection, processing, transmission, storage and use of personal data. It rests on principles of consent and legitimacy, lawfulness, accuracy, transparency, confidentiality and security, and applies broadly to public and private controllers processing data in Togo. Outside limited exemptions, processing must be declared in advance to the supervisory authority, which issues a receipt; transfers of data abroad require the authority's authorization.

The supervisory authority, the Instance de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (IPDCP), took years to materialise: its president, Lt-Col Bédiani Béléi, was appointed by presidential decree in September 2024 and the authority was officially launched on 28 March 2025. It is now standing up its operational committees, running awareness campaigns and beginning to accompany businesses and administrations toward compliance — so active enforcement is only just starting.

Violations can attract fines up to XOF 10 million and/or prison terms of up to five years. Togo has no EU adequacy decision; practitioners should watch for IPDCP implementing decisions and compliance formalities now that the regulator is live.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Declare processing to the IPDCP — Beyond the exemptions in articles 5, 8 and 9, personal data processing is subject to a declaration with the authority, which issues a receipt.
  • 02
    Process on a lawful, consented basis — Processing must satisfy consent/legitimacy, lawfulness, accuracy, transparency, confidentiality and security principles.
  • 03
    Obtain authorization for transfers abroad — The IPDCP authorizes transfers of personal data outside Togo.
  • 04
    Secure data and respect data subject rights — Controllers must keep data confidential and secure and honour the access, rectification and objection rights defined by the law.
