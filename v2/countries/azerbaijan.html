The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Azerbaijan's Law on Personal Data (No. 998-IIIQ of 11 May 2010) governs the collection, processing and protection of personal data by state bodies and private operators. It predates the GDPR era and uses its own vocabulary — 'owners' and 'operators' of personal data rather than controllers and processors — and centres on data subject consent, with rights to be informed, access, rectify and erase data. Personal data information systems must be registered with the state, subject to exemptions.

There is no standalone data protection authority. Supervision is exercised primarily by the Ministry of Digital Development and Transport, with enforcement roles shared by other bodies including the Ministries of Internal Affairs and Justice and the State Security Service — an arrangement that leaves oversight fragmented. The law has no DPO requirement and no general breach notification duty; cross-border transfers largely turn on the data subject's written consent.

In practice, enforcement of data protection rules is limited, and commentators have criticized Azerbaijan for prioritizing cybersecurity and information-control measures over individual privacy. Penalties for violations arise under the Code of Administrative Offences and the Criminal Code but active administrative enforcement is rare, so the compliance burden for businesses is more about registration formalities and consent paperwork than regulator scrutiny.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Register information systems — Owners and operators must register personal data information systems with the state before operation, unless an exemption applies.
  • 02
    Consent-based processing — Collect and process personal data with the data subject's consent, for defined lawful purposes; written consent is the default gateway for most non-open data uses.
  • 03
    Cross-border transfers on written consent — Obtain the data subject's prior written consent before transferring personal data abroad, unless the data falls in the open category.
  • 04
    Data security and confidentiality — Protect personal data in information systems against unauthorized access, and correct or destroy unlawful or inaccurate data on demand.
