The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

South Sudan has no data protection law and no supervisory authority, but the picture is moving. The government announced that its first-ever Data Protection Bill would be introduced in 2026, to be drafted jointly by the ministries of information and justice as part of a push to have all cyberspace-governing laws in place — though as of August 2026 no tabled bill or published draft could be verified.

The country's flagship digital statute is instead the Cybercrime and Computer Misuse Act 2026, signed by President Salva Kiir on 18 February 2026 and now being enforced ahead of the December 2026 elections. It criminalises online fraud, harassment and harmful content, and includes some data-related offences, but it is a criminal statute — press-freedom groups including CPJ have warned it threatens journalists — not a data protection regime conferring rights on data subjects.

Beyond that, protections are limited to the constitutional right to privacy in the Transitional Constitution and scattered sectoral provisions. Organizations handling South Sudanese data face no domestic registration, DPO or breach duties today, but should watch for the promised Data Protection Bill later in 2026.
