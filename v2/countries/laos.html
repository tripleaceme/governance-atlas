The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Laos regulates personal data primarily through the Law on Electronic Data Protection No. 25/NA, adopted 12 May 2017. It governs the collection, processing, storage, use and transfer of data in electronic form — covering both 'general' and 'specific' (sensitive-type) data — and applies to state bodies, private entities and foreign businesses handling electronic data of Lao residents within Laos or via local infrastructure. It requires consent for collection and disclosure, imposes basic security and confidentiality duties, and gives individuals rights to access, correct and request deletion of their data.

The Ministry of Technology and Communications (MTC) is the responsible authority, but there is no dedicated, independent data protection regulator, no DPO or registration regime, and no GDPR-style breach notification deadline. The law is materially narrower than modern comprehensive statutes — it applies only to electronic data, its obligations are framed at a high level, and implementing practice is thin. Related provisions also appear in the Law on Prevention and Combating Cyber Crime and the Penal Code.

Enforcement is minimal in practice: penalties are modest (secondary sources report fines in the LAK 5–50 million range, with suspension of activities and criminal liability for serious cases), and no significant public enforcement actions are documented. Businesses should treat Laos as a consent-and-confidentiality jurisdiction with low regulatory activity but growing government interest in cyber and data controls.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Consent for collection and disclosure — Obtain the data owner's consent before collecting, using or disclosing personal electronic data, and use data only for the purpose for which it was provided.
  • 02
    Security and confidentiality — Implement measures to keep electronic data secure, accurate and confidential, and prevent unauthorized access, alteration or disclosure.
  • 03
    Cross-border transfer conditions — Send data abroad only with the data owner's consent and where the transfer does not harm national interests or public order.
  • 04
    Honor data owner rights — Allow individuals to access their data, correct inaccurate or outdated data, and request removal of data no longer necessary for its original purpose.
