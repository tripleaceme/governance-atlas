The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

North Macedonia's Law on Personal Data Protection (Official Gazette No. 42/2020, amended by 294/21) took effect on 24 February 2020 and is a close transposition of the EU GDPR, adopted as part of the country's EU accession agenda. After an 18-month transition, it has been fully applicable since August 2021. It covers controllers and processors on Macedonian territory and adopts GDPR concepts wholesale: legal bases, data subject rights, records of processing, DPIAs, and privacy by design.

The old general database registration requirement from the 2005 law was scrapped — the Agency's Central Registry survives only for high-risk databases, which controllers must notify. DPOs are mandatory for public bodies and for organisations doing large-scale systematic monitoring or extensive special-category processing. Breaches must be reported to the Agency within 72 hours unless unlikely to pose a risk to individuals.

Enforcement is handled by the Agency for Personal Data Protection (AZLP) in Skopje, an independent state body with inspection and sanctioning powers. Fines mirror the GDPR's turnover-based logic — up to 4% of a company's total annual income of the preceding financial year, with additional personal fines of EUR 300–500 for responsible officers. In practice the Agency is active on complaints and inspections, though headline fines remain far smaller than in EU member states.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    GDPR-aligned processing principles — Process personal data lawfully, fairly and transparently, for specified purposes, with data minimisation and storage limitation, and be able to demonstrate compliance.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the Agency for Personal Data Protection within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms.
  • 03
    DPO appointment — Appoint a Data Protection Officer if you are a public authority or conduct large-scale systematic monitoring or extensive processing of special categories of data.
  • 04
    High-risk database notification — Notify the Agency of databases involving high-risk data, which are recorded in the Central Registry.
  • 05
    Transfer safeguards — Transfer personal data abroad only under adequacy, appropriate safeguards, or Agency-approved mechanisms.
