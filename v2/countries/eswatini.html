The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Eswatini's Data Protection Act, 2022 (Act No. 5 of 2022) is a comprehensive, GDPR- and POPIA-influenced law that entered into force in 2022. It applies to data controllers and processors processing personal data by automated or non-automated means in Eswatini, whether or not they are domiciled or headquartered in the country. Rather than creating a standalone regulator, the Act designates the existing telecoms regulator, the Eswatini Communications Commission (ESCCOM), as the Eswatini Data Protection Authority (EDPA), with powers to investigate breaches, resolve complaints and sanction controllers.

Distinctive features include a statutory obligation for all entities that process personal data to register with the EDPA as controllers or processors, a 72-hour personal data breach notification duty, and sanctions ranging from warnings and enforcement notices to suspension of processing and fines of up to E5 million or 2% of annual turnover.

Enforcement is still maturing: ESCCOM/EDPA has been operationalising the regime through public notices and draft regulations (including on controller/processor registration and data protection officers), and there is not yet a significant published track record of fines. Companies dealing with Eswatini should treat registration and breach-notification readiness as the immediate compliance priorities.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Register with the EDPA — All entities processing personal data must register with the Eswatini Data Protection Authority as data controllers or data processors; the EDPA maintains a register of registered entities.
  • 02
    Process lawfully and fairly — Personal data may only be processed on a lawful basis, for defined purposes, and in line with data-quality and minimisation principles under the Act.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers must report personal data breaches to the EDPA within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.
  • 04
    Secure personal data — Controllers and processors must implement appropriate technical and organisational security safeguards against loss, damage and unauthorised access.
  • 05
    Respect data subject rights — Data subjects have rights of access, correction and objection, and complaints may be lodged with the EDPA, which can investigate and sanction non-compliance.
