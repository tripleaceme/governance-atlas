The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Paraguay's status has changed: the bill previously tracked has become law. Law No. 7593/2025, promulgated on 27 November 2025, is the country's first comprehensive data protection statute — the biggest privacy shift in its legislative history. It replaces a patchwork that previously regulated only credit data (Law 6534/2020) with a broad, GDPR-inspired framework covering public and private sector processing of personal data.

The law creates the Agencia Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales (ANPDP) as a decentralized unit of MITIC with functional autonomy and exclusive regulatory, supervisory and sanctioning powers. It establishes data protection principles and data subject rights, heightened protection for sensitive data and minors (parental consent below 16; joint consent for adolescents' sensitive data), breach notification duties, and a tiered sanctions regime topping out at 10,000 minimum daily wages for violations involving children's sensitive data.

Enforcement reality: the framework becomes fully enforceable over a two-year adaptation period (roughly to late 2027), and the ANPDP and implementing regulations are still being stood up as of mid-2026. Businesses are in the compliance-preparation window now. Paraguay holds no EU adequacy decision.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Establish lawful processing — Processing must rest on a lawful basis under the new framework, with prior, express and informed consent as the anchor for sensitive data.
  • 02
    Protect children's data — Obtain parental/guardian consent for under-16s; for adolescents 16 and over, sensitive data requires the adolescent's express consent jointly with parental authorization — the highest fines attach to violations here.
  • 03
    Secure data and notify breaches — Implement adequate technical and organizational security measures; breaches must be notified to the ANPDP, and failure to notify is sanctioned separately from the underlying security failure.
  • 04
    Prepare during the transition — Use the two-year adaptation period to map data flows, put transfer guarantees in place and designate a data protection officer where applicable before full enforceability.
