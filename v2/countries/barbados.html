The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Barbados has one of the more mature data protection regimes in the Eastern Caribbean. The Data Protection Act, 2019-29 is closely modelled on the GDPR and came into force on 31 March 2021 (with limited exceptions phased in). It applies to public and private sector controllers and processors handling personal data of individuals in Barbados, and imports familiar GDPR machinery: lawful bases, data subject rights, sensitive-data restrictions, 72-hour breach notification, and cross-border transfer conditions.

The regulator is the Data Protection Commission within the Ministry of Industry, Innovation, Science & Technology. Lisa Greaves was appointed the first Data Protection Commissioner in July 2021, making Barbados one of the few countries in the subregion with an actual functioning privacy regulator. Controllers and processors must register with the Commissioner, and controllers must appoint a data privacy officer. The Commissioner can investigate complaints, issue enforcement notices and pursue penalties; the Act carries criminal fines up to BBD 500,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years, with lesser fines (e.g. BBD 200,000 for false information, BBD 10,000 for failure to register).

Enforcement is still young — the Commission's early years have focused on registration and awareness rather than headline fines — but the compliance expectation is real, particularly for the island's international business and financial services sector. Companies already GDPR-compliant will find the framework familiar; the registration and DPO duties are the main Barbados-specific action items.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Register with the Commissioner — Data controllers and data processors must register with the Data Protection Commissioner before processing; failure to register is an offence (fine up to BBD 10,000 and up to 2 months' imprisonment).
  • 02
    Lawful, fair and transparent processing — Processing requires a lawful basis (consent or a statutory alternative), with GDPR-style principles of purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy and storage limitation.
  • 03
    72-hour breach notification — Notify the Commissioner of personal data breaches within 72 hours where feasible, and notify affected data subjects where the breach is likely to result in high risk.
  • 04
    Appoint a data privacy officer — Controllers must designate a data privacy officer responsible for monitoring compliance with the Act.
  • 05
    Safeguard international transfers — Personal data may only be transferred abroad where adequate protection or appropriate safeguards (or consent/derogations) are in place.
