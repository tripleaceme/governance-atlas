The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Iraq has no comprehensive data protection law in force and no data protection authority. Privacy protection rests on fragmented sources: the 2005 Constitution guarantees personal privacy (Article 17) and confidentiality of communications (Article 40), the Penal Code contains general privacy-related offences (e.g., disclosure of secrets), and the Civil Code offers general tort remedies. Sector-specific obligations exist mainly through Communications and Media Commission (CMC) directives and licensing conditions for telecom operators and digital platforms.

A draft Personal Data Protection Law — reportedly introduced to the Council of Representatives around 2021 and envisaging a national data protection authority — has been mentioned by several legal trackers, but it has not advanced visibly, has not been enacted, and reliable official documentation of its status is scarce. A long-running draft Cybercrime (Information Technology Crimes) Law and a draft Right to Information Act (2023) have drawn criticism for potential restrictions on expression and privacy rather than protections.

In practice, businesses processing Iraqi personal data face no registration, DPO, breach notification or transfer requirements under a general data law, but should observe CMC telecom/platform rules, contractual confidentiality, and Penal Code exposure for wrongful disclosure. The regulatory direction remains uncertain amid Iraq's broader legislative gridlock.
