The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Morocco's Law 09-08, promulgated in February 2009, was one of the first comprehensive data protection statutes in North Africa and is closely modeled on the pre-GDPR European Directive 95/46/EC. It applies to public and private controllers processing personal data in Morocco and operates on a prior-formalities model: no processing may begin until the controller has obtained either a declaration receipt or an authorization from the regulator, the CNDP, based in Rabat.

The CNDP is one of Africa's most active data protection authorities. It reviews declarations, handles complaints, conducts audits and issues sanctions, and continues to publish detailed deliberations — including a set of five adopted in November 2025 covering cookies, newsletters, CCTV in healthcare facilities and workplace access control — with a dedicated deliberation on AI processing in preparation as of 2026.

Enforcement relies on criminal-style fines (MAD 10,000–300,000 and up to two years imprisonment for the most serious violations) rather than GDPR-scale administrative penalties. A modernization of Law 09-08 toward closer GDPR alignment has been under study for several years but had not been enacted as of mid-2026, so the 2009 declaration/authorization regime remains the operative framework.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Complete prior formalities with the CNDP — Obtain a declaration receipt or prior authorization from the CNDP before starting any processing; sensitive data and certain categories require authorization rather than simple declaration.
  • 02
    Obtain authorization for cross-border transfers — Transfers to countries not ensuring adequate protection require prior CNDP authorization; unauthorized transfers carry criminal penalties (up to MAD 200,000 and one year imprisonment).
  • 03
    Establish a lawful basis and informed consent — Process data only on a legitimate basis, with informed consent as the default, and parental consent for children's data.
  • 04
    Implement security measures — Take all technical and organizational measures to protect personal data against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure or loss.
  • 05
    Respect data subject rights — Honor rights of access, rectification and objection, and follow CNDP deliberations on specific processing scenarios (cookies, CCTV, marketing).
