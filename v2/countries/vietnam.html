The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Vietnam replaced its decree-level regime (Decree 13/2023, the 'PDPD') with a full statute: the Law on Personal Data Protection, No. 91/2025/QH15 (PDPL), passed on 26 June 2025 and effective 1 January 2026, together with guiding Decree 356/2025/ND-CP issued 31 December 2025. The PDPL applies to domestic and foreign organizations processing personal data of individuals in Vietnam, regardless of nationality, and establishes core rights to be informed, consent, access, and rectification.

Enforcement sits with the Ministry of Public Security through its cybersecurity department A05 rather than an independent DPA — a distinctive feature that colors the whole regime. The law keeps Vietnam's hallmark dossier system: organizations must prepare processing impact assessments and cross-border transfer impact assessments and lodge them with the MPS within 60 days, and notify breaches within 72 hours. Children's data requires guardian consent plus the child's own consent from age seven.

Sanctions were significantly hardened: statutory administrative fines up to VND 3 billion (about USD 115,000) for most violations, and for cross-border transfer breaches up to 5% of the prior year's revenue — among the toughest revenue-linked penalties in ASEAN. Businesses tracking Vietnam should update anything still referencing Decree 13/PDPD 2023.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Obtain valid consent — Consent must be explicit, specific and verifiable, with data subjects able to withdraw it; sensitive data and marketing uses carry stricter conditions.
  • 02
    File impact assessment dossiers — Prepare processing impact assessments and cross-border transfer impact assessments and submit them to the Ministry of Public Security within 60 days of starting processing/transfer.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data protection violations and breaches must be reported to the MPS (A05) within 72 hours.
  • 04
    Designate data protection personnel — Organizations must appoint personnel or a department responsible for personal data protection, per the PDPL and Decree 356/2025.
  • 05
    Protect children's data — Verify age and obtain parent/guardian consent — plus the child's own consent from age 7 — before processing children's personal data.
