The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

São Tomé & Príncipe enacted Law No. 03/2016 on the Protection of Personal Data as its general data protection framework. The law is closely modeled on the EU's former Data Protection Directive (95/46/EC) and Portugal's Act 67/98, so its architecture will feel familiar to anyone who worked with pre-GDPR European law: consent or another legal ground for processing, prior notification to the regulator (with authorization for sensitive data), data quality principles, and adequacy-based rules for international transfers.

The supervisory authority, the Agência Nacional de Protecção de Dados Pessoais (ANPDP), is a genuine bright spot for a small island state: it is operational and relatively active, issuing authorisations, resolutions and opinions, operating under the National Assembly as an independent administrative entity, and participating in regional networks (NADPA and the francophone AFAPDP). Entities must generally obtain data-subject consent and give prior notice to the ANPDP before collecting and processing personal data.

Because the law predates the GDPR, it lacks modern features — there is no general breach-notification duty, no DPO requirement, and no turnover-based fines — and detailed English-language documentation of penalty levels is sparse. For most international businesses exposure is limited, but anyone processing Santomean data at scale (telecoms, banking, the growing digital-government program) should file notifications with the ANPDP and track possible modernization of the 2016 framework.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Notify the ANPDP before processing — Prior notice to the ANPDP (and authorization for sensitive data) is required to lawfully collect and process personal data, except in limited legal exceptions.
  • 02
    Obtain data subject consent — Consent — or another legal ground provided by the law — is required before collecting and processing personal data.
  • 03
    Follow Directive-style processing principles — Data must be processed fairly and lawfully, for specified purposes, kept accurate and no longer than necessary, mirroring Directive 95/46/EC and Portugal's Act 67/98.
  • 04
    Restrict international transfers — Transfers follow an adequacy-based model with ANPDP oversight for destinations lacking adequate protection.
