The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Albania replaced its 2008-era framework (Law 9887) with Law No. 124/2024 'On Personal Data Protection', approved by Parliament on 19 December 2024, published in January 2025 and in force from early 2025. The new law fully aligns Albania with the EU GDPR and the Law Enforcement Directive, a milestone in its EU accession path. It applies to controllers and processors established in Albania and, GDPR-style, to foreign entities targeting or monitoring people in Albania.

The law imports the familiar GDPR toolkit: legal bases for processing, data subject rights, records of processing (replacing the old notification-to-the-Commissioner system), data protection impact assessments, 72-hour breach notification, and mandatory DPOs for public bodies and large-scale or sensitive processing. Cross-border transfers follow an adequacy-plus-safeguards model, with Standard Contractual Clauses and Binding Corporate Rules available and Commissioner authorisation as a fallback.

Enforcement sits with the long-established Commissioner for the Right to Information and Personal Data Protection (IDP), elected by Parliament for a seven-year term. Sanctions jumped dramatically from the old regime: fines can now reach ALL 20 million or 4% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. Businesses that were compliant under the 2008 law should treat this as a fresh GDPR-grade compliance exercise; the Commissioner has signalled a shift from awareness-raising to active enforcement.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    GDPR-style accountability — Controllers must demonstrate compliance through records of processing activities, data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing, and privacy by design and by default.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach (with reasons for any delay), and inform affected individuals where the risk is high.
  • 03
    DPO appointment — Appoint a Data Protection Officer if you are a public body or your core activities involve large-scale monitoring or large-scale processing of special-category data.
  • 04
    Transfer safeguards — Transfer data abroad only to adequate jurisdictions or under appropriate safeguards such as SCCs or BCRs, with Commissioner authorisation otherwise.
  • 05
    Data subject rights handling — Honour access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection rights within statutory deadlines.
