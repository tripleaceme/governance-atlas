The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Montenegro still operates under its pre-GDPR Personal Data Protection Law of 2008 (Official Gazette 79/08 and 70/09), amended several times since — most recently in July 2024. The law follows the old EU Directive 95/46 model: it applies to controllers processing personal data in Montenegro, requires a legal basis for processing, obliges controllers to register their data filing systems with the supervisory Agency, and channels most enforcement through a misdemeanour-fine regime with maximum penalties of only EUR 20,000 for legal entities.

Supervision sits with the Agency for Personal Data Protection and Free Access to Information (AZLP), which handles both data protection and freedom-of-information. Cross-border transfers to countries lacking adequate protection require the Agency's approval, and there is no general data breach notification duty — hallmarks of the framework's age. Enforcement is real but low-stakes by European standards, and the European Commission has repeatedly flagged that the law is not aligned with the GDPR or the Law Enforcement Directive.

A comprehensive GDPR-aligned replacement law has been in the works for years as part of Montenegro's EU accession (Chapter 23). In March 2026 the Ministry of Interior submitted the draft new Law on Personal Data Protection to the European Commission for review, but as of August 2026 it has not been adopted by Parliament. Anyone doing business in Montenegro should comply with the 2008 law today while preparing for a GDPR-style overhaul in the near term. Note that some secondary trackers incorrectly report a 2023 GDPR-aligned act as already in force — official and law-firm sources confirm the 2008 law remains the one in effect.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Lawful basis and proportionality — Personal data may be processed only with the data subject's consent or another legal ground, and must be adequate and not excessive relative to the purpose.
  • 02
    Filing-system registration — Controllers must establish records of their personal data filing systems and submit them to the AZLP before processing begins.
  • 03
    Transfer approval — Transfers of personal data to countries without an adequate level of protection require prior approval from the AZLP.
  • 04
    Designated responsible person — Controllers with ten or more employees involved in processing must designate a person responsible for personal data protection.
  • 05
    Security measures — Controllers and processors must implement technical and organisational measures to protect data against loss, destruction and unauthorised access.
