The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Cameroon's status has changed: the long-pending bill became Law No. 2024/017 of 23 December 2024 relating to Personal Data Protection, the country's first comprehensive data protection statute and the 38th in Africa. It protects the data of Cameroonian nationals and residents — and, unusually, extends to individuals transiting through Cameroon. The law imports many GDPR concepts: data portability, data protection impact assessments, records of processing, breach notification, DPOs and rules on cross-border transfers.

Two features stand out. First, breach notification must be made 'immediately' to both the Authority and affected data subjects, and the duty binds processors as well as controllers — stricter on paper than GDPR's 72-hour, controller-centric rule. Second, the law creates a new supervisory body, the Personal Data Protection Authority, but that authority is still being stood up, so day-one supervision is limited. Companies were given an 18-month transition that expired on 23 June 2026, meaning compliance is now formally due.

Enforcement exposure is potentially severe — commentary points to fines reaching XAF 1 billion and personal liability, including prison, for executives. With the transition period over but the Authority and implementing regulations still maturing, the practical enforcement environment in mid-2026 remains in flux. No EU adequacy decision exists.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Establish a lawful basis and consent — Processing personal data on digital communication networks or any other medium requires the data subject's consent or another lawful basis under the law.
  • 02
    Notify breaches immediately — Both controllers and processors must inform the Authority and affected data subjects immediately upon becoming aware of a personal data breach.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO and complete formalities — The DPO serves as contact point for data subjects and the Authority and carries out the obligatory formalities on the organisation's behalf.
  • 04
    Conduct impact assessments and keep records — The law obliges controllers to carry out data protection impact assessments and maintain records of processing activities.
  • 05
    Control cross-border transfers — Transfers of personal data out of Cameroon require approval and appropriate protection.
