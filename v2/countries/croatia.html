The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Croatia is an EU member state where the GDPR applies directly, supplemented by the Act on the Implementation of the General Data Protection Regulation (Official Gazette No. 44/2018), in force since 25 May 2018. The Implementation Act keeps the GDPR default consent age of 16 for children, and adds national rules on genetic and biometric data and detailed requirements for video surveillance — a distinctive Croatian focus that generates a steady stream of enforcement.

The supervisory authority is the Personal Data Protection Agency (AZOP), an independent body with over two decades of experience. AZOP has become one of the more active enforcers in Central and Eastern Europe: it imposed a record of roughly €10.5 million in fines across 97 decisions in 2024 and nearly €7 million during 2025, with significant penalties against debt-collection, retail and telecoms companies.

Practically, compliance in Croatia is GDPR compliance plus attention to the national video-surveillance regime (signage, retention limits, access controls) and biometric data rules. Organisations targeting Croatian consumers should treat AZOP as an authority with real teeth.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    GDPR core compliance — Apply GDPR principles, lawful bases, data subject rights and accountability duties, which apply directly in Croatia.
  • 02
    Video surveillance rules — Comply with the Implementation Act's specific regime for CCTV: clear signage, purpose limitation, restricted access and limited retention.
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify AZOP within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable breach; notify individuals where the risk is high.
  • 04
    DPO appointment — Appoint a DPO where GDPR Article 37 triggers apply and publish/communicate the contact details.
  • 05
    Child consent verification — Obtain and verify parental consent for information society services offered to children under 16.
