The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Monaco comprehensively overhauled its data protection framework with Law No. 1.565 of 3 December 2024, which repeals and replaces the 1993 law (Law 1.165) and aligns the Principality with the GDPR and Council of Europe Convention 108+. The law has extraterritorial scope modelled on GDPR Article 3: it covers controllers and processors established in Monaco and foreign ones offering goods or services to, or monitoring, people in Monaco. Sovereign Ordinance No. 11.327 of 10 July 2025 set out the detailed implementing rules, and pre-existing processing benefits from a one-year transitional window.

The former Commission de Contrôle des Informations Nominatives (CCIN) has been replaced by the Autorité de Protection des Données Personnelles (APDP), which has substantially stronger powers, including administrative fines of up to €10 million or 4% of worldwide turnover. The law strengthens data subject rights (information, access, rectification, erasure, portability), introduces DPIAs for high-risk processing, mandatory records of processing, DPO designation for certain categories of organisations, and certification mechanisms.

The shift is from the old prior-formalities culture (declarations and authorisations to the CCIN) to a GDPR-style accountability model. Monaco does not hold an EU adequacy decision, so EEA exporters still need safeguards to send data to Monaco, though the 2024 reform is widely seen as positioning Monaco for a future adequacy bid.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Accountability and records — Maintain a register of processing activities and be able to demonstrate compliance in place of the old declaration regime.
  • 02
    DPIA for high-risk processing — Carry out data protection impact assessments before high-risk processing operations.
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify the APDP within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable personal data breach; inform affected individuals when risk is high.
  • 04
    DPO designation — Appoint a Data Protection Officer where the law requires it; the role may be outsourced under a service contract with named responsible individuals.
  • 05
    Transfer safeguards — Only transfer personal data abroad to adequately protective jurisdictions or with appropriate safeguards.
