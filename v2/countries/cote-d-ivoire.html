The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Côte d'Ivoire was an early West African adopter: Law No. 2013-450 of 19 June 2013 establishes a comprehensive regime for the processing and circulation of personal data, built on four pillars — legality, purpose (finalité), data minimization and consent. Data subjects have GDPR-style rights of access, objection (including to direct marketing), rectification, blocking and deletion, and a right not to be subject to purely automated decisions.

Distinctively, supervision is entrusted to the telecoms regulator: ARTCI is formally designated as the personal data protection authority, an independent administrative authority with regulatory, control and sanction powers. Most processing is subject to prior declaration, with authorizations for sensitive categories, under procedures fixed by Decree No. 2015-79. Appointing a data protection correspondent can relieve controllers of some declaration formalities.

Enforcement is real by regional standards — ARTCI runs compliance surveys, a permanent exchange framework with practitioners, and has issued significant sanction decisions; security failings can attract fines of up to XOF 10 million or up to 5% of pre-tax turnover. There is no EU adequacy decision.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Declare processing to ARTCI — Controllers must file prior declarations (or obtain authorizations for sensitive processing) with ARTCI before processing, per Decree No. 2015-79.
  • 02
    Respect the four core principles — Processing must observe legality, purpose limitation, data minimization and consent.
  • 03
    Secure personal data — Article 40 requires all necessary precautions to prevent data being altered, damaged or accessed by unauthorized third parties — breaches of this duty drive ARTCI's largest sanctions.
  • 04
    Honour data subject rights — Provide access in intelligible form, allow objection for legitimate reasons and to marketing, and correct, complete, block or delete inaccurate data.
  • 05
    Control transfers abroad — Cross-border transfers must go to destinations ensuring adequate protection and follow ARTCI formalities.
