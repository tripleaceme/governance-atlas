The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Gabon's data protection framework rests on Law No. 001/2011 of 25 September 2011, one of Central Africa's earliest comprehensive statutes, originally modeled on France's pre-GDPR Informatique et Libertés regime: declaration and authorization formalities with a national commission, processing principles, data subject rights, and restrictions on transfers to countries lacking adequate protection. Its longtime regulator was the Commission Nationale pour la Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (CNPDCP).

In July 2023 the framework was significantly modernized by Law No. 025/2023, which amends the 2011 law to align more closely with GDPR-era standards. Key changes include transforming the CNPDCP into a strengthened Autorité pour la Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel et de la Vie Privée (APDPVP) with enhanced powers, introducing specific protections for children's data, and setting financial sanctions ranging from FCFA 1 million to 100 million.

Enforcement reality is mixed: the authority has an established institutional history (it participates in the NADPA and AFAPDP regional networks) and the 2023 reform signals renewed political attention to the digital sector, but published enforcement actions are scarce and post-amendment implementing practice (breach notification mechanics, DPO expectations) is not yet well documented. Businesses in telecoms, banking and oil services should complete declaration formalities and watch for APDPVP guidance under the amended law.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Complete formalities with the authority — Processing operations are subject to declaration or prior authorization with the data protection authority under the Law 001/2011 framework.
  • 02
    Process data lawfully and proportionately — Controllers must respect the law's processing principles and data subject rights, with violations sanctioned at FCFA 1m–100m under the amended regime.
  • 03
    Restrict transfers abroad — Transfers of personal data to countries that do not ensure adequate protection require approval from the authority.
  • 04
    Protect children's data — The 2023 amendment introduces specific measures for children's personal data, reflecting GDPR-era standards.
