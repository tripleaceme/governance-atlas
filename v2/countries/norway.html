The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Norway applies the full text of the EU GDPR through the EEA Agreement, incorporated by the Personal Data Act of 15 June 2018 (personopplysningsloven), which entered into force on 20 July 2018. The Act fills the gaps the GDPR leaves to national law — for example on the digital age of consent (13), employment-related processing and exemptions — but the substantive regime is the GDPR itself, applying to any organisation established in Norway or targeting people there.

The supervisory authority, Datatilsynet, is one of Europe's more assertive enforcers relative to market size, with a record that includes the landmark Grindr fine over unlawful sharing of user data with advertisers, and firm positions on tracking, cloud services and workplace monitoring. Because Norway sits outside the EU institutional framework, Datatilsynet participates in the European Data Protection Board without voting rights, but applies EDPB guidance in practice.

As an EEA state inside the GDPR zone, Norway needs no EU adequacy decision — personal data flows freely between Norway and EU member states, and transfers out of the EEA follow the standard GDPR Chapter V toolkit.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Comply with full GDPR obligations — Lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, records of processing, DPIAs and accountability requirements apply exactly as under the EU GDPR.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches posing a risk to individuals must be reported to Datatilsynet within 72 hours.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Mandatory for public authorities and organisations whose core activities involve large-scale monitoring or large-scale special-category processing.
  • 04
    Safeguard cross-border transfers — Transfers outside the EEA require adequacy, standard contractual clauses or other Chapter V safeguards, with transfer impact assessments in line with EDPB guidance.
  • 05
    Obtain parental consent under 13 — Online services relying on consent must obtain parental authorisation for children under 13.
