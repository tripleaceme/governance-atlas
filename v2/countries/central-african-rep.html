The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Central African Republic enacted its first comprehensive data protection statute, Law No. 24.001 on the Protection of Personal Data, in January 2024 — a significant correction to older references that list the country as having no law. The text is comparable in structure to other modern francophone African laws: processing principles, data-subject rights, a mandatory in-country Data Protection Officer, breach reporting, and turnover-based administrative penalties of up to 5% of annual turnover.

Institutionally, the law provides for an independent supervisory authority to be established under the Ministry of Digital Economy, Posts and Telecommunications within 12 months of enactment; until it exists, the Ministry oversees implementation. Cross-border transfers require adequate protection, with specific arrangements for CEMAC and ECCAS member states and fallbacks (explicit consent, contractual necessity, public interest, or authorisation with guarantees) for other destinations.

Enforcement reality should be treated as embryonic: there is no operational regulator, no published guidance, and the country's security situation limits state capacity. Distinctive features to note in compliance planning are the residency requirement for the DPO and parental consent for processing minors' data.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Appoint a resident Data Protection Officer — Controllers must appoint a DPO resident in CAR who maintains the processing register, advises on new operations and acts independently, dismissible only in serious circumstances.
  • 02
    Keep a register of processing — An updated register of all processing activities must be maintained on behalf of the controller by the DPO.
  • 03
    Report breaches to the authority — Controllers must inform the data protection agency of personal data breaches (no fixed statutory deadline identified).
  • 04
    Restrict cross-border transfers — Transfers require an adequate level of protection; CEMAC/ECCAS destinations have specific conditions, and other transfers rely on explicit consent, contractual necessity, public interest or authorisation with sufficient guarantees.
  • 05
    Obtain parental consent for minors — Where the data subject is a minor, parental or guardian consent must be obtained, and minors' rights may be exercised through their representatives.
