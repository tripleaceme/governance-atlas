The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Antigua & Barbuda's Data Protection Act, 2013 (No. 10 of 2013) was one of the first comprehensive data protection statutes in the Eastern Caribbean and came into force on assent on 28 October 2013. It applies to personal data processed by both public and private bodies, requiring that processing be lawful, necessary for a specified purpose, and generally based on consent, with heightened rules for sensitive personal data and obligations to secure data against unauthorized access, loss, or disclosure.

Enforcement sits with the Information Commissioner — a role tied to the Freedom of Information Act, 2004 framework — who hears and investigates complaints, monitors controller compliance, and promotes data subject rights, with appeals lying to the courts. Offences carry criminal penalties of up to XCD 50,000 or three years' imprisonment on summary conviction, and up to XCD 100,000 or five years on indictment.

The practical reality is that the regime is largely dormant: the law predates GDPR-era concepts (no breach-notification deadline, DPO mandate, or turnover-based fines), there is no dedicated data protection office or website, and reported enforcement activity is minimal. Businesses should treat the Act as setting baseline consent, purpose-limitation, and security duties, while recognizing that active supervision is limited.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Process lawfully and with consent — Personal data may only be processed with the data subject's consent or where processing is lawful and necessary for a specified purpose, contract, legal obligation, or vital interest.
  • 02
    Purpose limitation — Collect personal data only for lawful purposes directly related to the controller's activity and do not process it in ways incompatible with those purposes.
  • 03
    Safeguard personal data — Implement security measures protecting personal data against unauthorized access, alteration, loss, disclosure, or destruction.
  • 04
    Respect data subject rights — Give individuals access to their personal data and the ability to correct it, and honor withdrawals of consent, subject to the Act's exemptions.
