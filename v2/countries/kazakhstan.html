The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Kazakhstan's Law No. 94-V 'On Personal Data and Their Protection', in force since 2013 and amended repeatedly, is a consent-centric regime: collecting and processing personal data generally requires the data subject's consent unless a statutory exception applies. It applies to state bodies and private 'owners' and 'operators' of personal data databases, and is supplemented by ministerial rules on collection and processing. The authorized state body is the Ministry of Digital Development, Innovation and Aerospace Industry, which gained explicit oversight and enforcement functions through amendments in the early 2020s.

Kazakhstan's most distinctive features are data localization and, since 1 July 2024, rapid breach reporting. Personal data of Kazakh citizens must be stored in databases physically located in Kazakhstan — copies may be transferred abroad, but the primary database stays onshore. Cross-border transfers are permitted to jurisdictions ensuring adequate protection or with consent and other statutory grounds. Data breaches must now be reported to the Ministry within one working day of discovery, one of the shortest deadlines anywhere.

Enforcement historically leaned on the Administrative Offences Code, whose MCI-indexed fines are modest by international standards, with criminal liability reserved for serious unlawful collection or dissemination; regulators have signalled tightening oversight amid a string of high-profile leaks. There is no general registration or licensing of controllers, but organisations must designate a person responsible for data processing, adopt internal documentation, and destroy or depersonalise data once the purpose is fulfilled.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Consent-based processing — Obtain the data subject's consent (often in writing or via verified electronic means) before collecting and processing personal data, unless a statutory exception applies.
  • 02
    Data localization — Store the primary database containing Kazakh personal data on servers physically located in Kazakhstan; only copies may go abroad.
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify the Ministry of Digital Development within one working day of discovering a personal data security breach (effective July 2024).
  • 04
    Responsible person and security measures — Designate a person or unit responsible for personal data processing and implement legal, organisational and technical protection measures.
  • 05
    Purpose and retention limits — Process data only for defined purposes and destroy or depersonalise it once the purpose is achieved or consent is withdrawn.
