The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Seychelles' Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act 24 of 2023) was gazetted and entered into force on 22 December 2023, replacing the Data Protection Act 2003 — a law that had been enacted but never brought into practical operation. The new Act is a modern, GDPR-influenced framework covering data controllers and processors, with duties around lawful processing, retention, security, cross-border transfers and the appointment of data protection officers for organisations of sufficient size or processing risk.

Supervision sits with the Information Commission, which also handles access to information; it is empowered to conduct audits and investigations and to impose administrative fines. Controllers must notify the Commission within 72 hours of discovering a breach that may put data subjects' rights and freedoms at risk, and children's data (under 18) may only be processed with parental or guardian consent.

All controllers and processors were given an 18-month transition from 22 December 2023, which expired in June 2025 — so the Act is now fully applicable. Enforcement history is still short, and no EU adequacy decision exists; businesses should track Information Commission guidance as its data protection practice develops.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers have 72 hours after discovering a qualifying breach to notify the Information Commission, with any delay requiring explanation.
  • 02
    Appoint a DPO where required — Organisations meeting size/processing thresholds must appoint a Data Protection Officer.
  • 03
    Safeguard cross-border transfers — Entities transferring personal data outside Seychelles must apply the Act's safeguards and mechanisms to keep data protected to an equivalent standard.
  • 04
    Obtain parental consent for children — Processing data of individuals under 18 requires consent of a parent or legal guardian, or verification of such consent where data is obtained from a third party.
  • 05
    Comply with retention and security rules — Controllers and processors must implement the Act's data protection, retention and processing requirements, subject to Commission audit.
