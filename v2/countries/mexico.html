The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Mexico replaced its 2010 private-sector data protection law with an entirely new LFPDPPP, published 20 March 2025 and effective 21 March 2025. The rewrite was driven by a constitutional 'organic simplification' reform (December 2024) that dissolved seven autonomous bodies — including INAI, the independent data protection authority operating since 2010. Supervision and enforcement of both the private-sector law and public-sector rules now sit with the executive-branch Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno (SABG).

Substantively the regime remains recognizable: controllers need a privacy notice (aviso de privacidad), a lawful basis with express consent for sensitive and financial data, designated data protection personnel to process ARCO (access, rectification, cancellation, opposition) requests, security measures, and conditions on transfers. Breach notification runs to affected data subjects — there is still no general duty to notify the regulator, though the SABG has signaled that mandatory regulator notification, formal DPO rules, and DPIAs are priorities for further legislation. Fines range from 100 to 320,000 UMA.

The critical governance point for foreign counsel is independence: moving enforcement from an autonomous institute into a government ministry runs counter to international best practice and complicates any future EU adequacy ambitions. Enforcement under the SABG is still ramping up, and implementing regulations for the new law are pending, so practical guidance largely carries over from INAI-era criteria.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Issue a compliant privacy notice — Provide an aviso de privacidad stating identity, purposes, transfers, and ARCO mechanisms before or at collection.
  • 02
    Establish consent and lawful basis — Obtain consent appropriate to the data type — express consent for financial data and express (often written) consent for sensitive data — unless a statutory exception applies.
  • 03
    Designate data protection personnel — Appoint a person or department responsible for personal data to receive and resolve ARCO requests within statutory deadlines.
  • 04
    Implement security and breach response — Adopt administrative, technical, and physical safeguards, and notify affected data subjects without delay of breaches that significantly affect their patrimonial or moral rights.
  • 05
    Control transfers — Disclose transfers in the privacy notice and obtain consent unless an exception applies; ensure recipients assume the same obligations.
