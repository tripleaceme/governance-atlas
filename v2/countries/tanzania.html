The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act, 2022 (Act No. 11 of 2022, passed 27 November 2022, in force 1 May 2023) established the Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) and, together with the May 2023 Regulations on collection/processing and complaints handling, created a complete operative regime. The framework's defining feature is gatekeeping: controllers and processors must register with the PDPC before processing any personal data, and cross-border transfers require a prior PDPC permit backed by adequacy findings, international or bilateral agreements, or contractual safeguards.

Obligations include appointing a data protection officer, notifying the Commission of security breaches without undue delay, and observing GDPR-style processing principles and data subject rights. Sanctions are layered: administrative enforcement and penalty notices up to TZS 100 million plus uncapped compensation orders, and criminal offences (such as unlawful disclosure) carrying fines up to TZS 5 billion for corporates and up to 10 years' imprisonment for individuals.

Enforcement has moved from theory to practice quickly. Registration deadlines were extended through 2025, with the PDPC signaling full enforcement against unregistered processors from April 2026; the Commission has also begun issuing substantive decisions — notably a 2025 ruling that children's images are sensitive personal data whose commercial use without parental consent is unlawful. Any business touching Tanzanian personal data in 2026 should treat registration, DPO appointment and transfer permits as immediate, actively-policed requirements.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Register with the PDPC before processing — Registration of controllers and processors is mandatory prior to any collection or processing; failure attracts fines and, from April 2026, active enforcement.
  • 02
    Obtain a permit for cross-border transfers — Personal data may leave Tanzania only with a prior PDPC permit supported by adequacy, international/bilateral agreements, or contractual safeguards, typically with data-subject consent.
  • 03
    Appoint a data protection officer — Controllers and processors must designate a DPO responsible for security controls, PDPA compliance, and handling data subject complaints.
  • 04
    Notify breaches without undue delay — Section 27(5) requires controllers to notify the Commission of any security breach affecting personal data without undue delay.
  • 05
    Protect children's and sensitive data — Sensitive data (which the PDPC has held includes children's images) demands parental consent and heightened safeguards; unlawful disclosure is a criminal offence.
