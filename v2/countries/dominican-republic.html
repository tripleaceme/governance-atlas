The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Dominican Republic's Law No. 172-13 on the Comprehensive Protection of Personal Data (December 2013) protects personal data held in public and private files, registries, and databases, with a strong emphasis on credit information — it doubles as the regulatory regime for credit bureaus (Sociedades de Información Crediticia). It establishes consent-based processing, data quality duties, and constitutional habeas data rights allowing individuals to access, rectify, and suppress their data through the courts.

Its greatest weakness is institutional: there is no national data protection authority. The Superintendencia de Bancos supervises only credit bureaus, Pro Consumidor handles consumer-facing complaints without fining power, and general enforcement runs through criminal courts (fines and six months to two years' imprisonment) and civil damages claims. There is no registration regime outside credit bureaus, no DPO requirement, and no breach notification duty. International transfers require the data subject's express authorization or necessity grounds, but with no regulator, compliance is effectively litigation-driven.

The law is widely viewed as overdue for modernization; recent legislative energy has gone to adjacent areas instead — an AI bill introduced in February 2025 and a new Penal Code effective August 2026 that criminalizes offenses against private information in automated data systems. No comprehensive replacement data protection bill had been enacted as of August 2026, so Law 172-13 remains the operative framework.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Obtain consent for processing and disclosure — Treatment and transfer of personal data generally require the data subject's consent, with express authorization for international transfers absent a necessity ground.
  • 02
    Ensure data quality and purpose limitation — Keep data accurate, current, and used only for the purposes for which it was collected, with suppression when no longer necessary.
  • 03
    Respect habeas data rights — Enable individuals to access, rectify, and suppress their data; they may enforce these rights through habeas data proceedings and damages claims.
  • 04
    Credit bureaus: comply with the special regime — Sociedades de Información Crediticia must register with and submit to inspection by the Superintendencia de Bancos and follow strict rules on credit data handling and retention.
  • 05
    Maintain security and confidentiality — Protect databases against unauthorized access and disclosure; violations can trigger criminal fines and imprisonment.
