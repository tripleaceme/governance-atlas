The short version

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Iceland applies the EU GDPR through the EEA Agreement, implemented and supplemented nationally by Act No. 90/2018 on Data Protection and the Processing of Personal Data, in force since 15 July 2018. For practical purposes Iceland operates like an EU member state on data protection: the same principles, data subject rights, breach rules and penalty ceilings apply to any organisation processing personal data of people in Iceland.

The national law makes use of GDPR opening clauses in a few distinctive ways — notably, it extends protection to data about deceased persons for five years after death, and sets the digital age of consent at 13. Maximum administrative fines are expressed in local currency at up to ISK 2.4 billion or 4% of worldwide turnover.

The supervisory authority is Persónuvernd, an active, independent regulator based in Reykjavík that handles a high volume of complaints for a small jurisdiction and participates in EU-level cooperation as an EEA authority. Because Iceland is inside the EEA/GDPR zone, personal data flows freely between Iceland and the EU without any adequacy mechanism being needed.

What you actually have to do

  • 01
    Comply with full GDPR obligations — Controllers and processors must meet GDPR requirements on lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, records of processing and accountability, as applied by Act 90/2018.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches must be reported to Persónuvernd within 72 hours where there is a risk to individuals, and to affected individuals where the risk is high.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — A Data Protection Officer is mandatory for public authorities and for organisations whose core activities involve large-scale monitoring or large-scale processing of special categories of data.
  • 04
    Safeguard cross-border transfers — Transfers outside the EEA require an adequacy decision, standard contractual clauses or other Chapter V safeguards.
  • 05
    Respect extended protections — Icelandic law protects deceased persons' data for five years after death and requires parental consent for online services offered to children under 13.
