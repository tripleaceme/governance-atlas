Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Trinidad and Tobago's Data Protection Act, 2011 (Chap. 22:04) is on the books but only fragmentarily in force — a critical caveat behind its 'has a law' label. Part I and sections 7-18, 22, 23, 25(1), 26 and 28 were proclaimed on 6 January 2012, essentially the definitional and administrative machinery including the Office of the Information Commissioner; sections 42(a) and (b) followed on 20 August 2021. The operative parts — the rules actually governing collection, protection and disclosure of personal information, and the penalties for breaking them — have never been proclaimed. In practice, there is no enforceable general obligation on private-sector controllers, no registration, no breach notification duty, and no functioning enforcement regime.

When (if) fully proclaimed, the Act would impose General Privacy Principles on public and private bodies, overseen by the Information Commissioner, with penalties of up to TTD 100,000 or five years' imprisonment for individuals and up to 10% of annual returns for companies. A 2018 government review found the Act out of step with the GDPR (weak on e-marketing, online privacy and breach notification), and stakeholder consultations in 2019 discussed amendments; parliamentary motions have since urged proclamation of the remaining sections, but as of 2025-2026 the position was unchanged.

For a meeting: treat T&T as 'law enacted, obligations mostly dormant'. Compliance exposure today comes mainly from sectoral confidentiality rules, contract, and foreign laws with extraterritorial reach — but reform or proclamation could change the picture, so the Act's GDPR-divergent text is still worth knowing.

Key obligations

  • 01
    General Privacy Principles (largely unproclaimed) — The Act's core principles governing collection, use, disclosure and safeguarding of personal information are enacted but not yet proclaimed into force.
  • 02
    Information Commissioner oversight — The proclaimed provisions establish the Office of the Information Commissioner as the body responsible for oversight, interpretation and (eventual) enforcement of the Act.
  • 03
    Sensitive personal information handling (s. 42(a)-(b)) — Limited provisions on the handling of sensitive personal information were brought into operation in August 2021.
  • 04
    Penalties once proclaimed — Full proclamation would expose individuals to fines up to TTD 100,000 or 5 years' imprisonment and companies to fines up to 10% of annual returns.
