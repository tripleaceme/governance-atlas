Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Papua New Guinea has no comprehensive data protection law in force. The Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT) completed the National Data Governance and Data Protection Policy 2024, which sets out principles and commits the government to a regulatory framework addressing data breaches, privacy violations and data misuse — but this is policy, not statute, and no dedicated Data Protection Bill had been tabled in Parliament as of August 2026. Describing PNG as 'bill in progress' therefore slightly overstates the position; it is more accurately at the policy-to-drafting stage.

Legislative energy has instead gone into the digital identity agenda: following the National Digital ID Policy (2025), DICT introduced a proposed Digital Identity and Verifiable Credentials Bill to underpin the SevisPass digital ID, SevisWallet and consent-based data exchange, with rollout targeted for 2026. That bill embeds some consent and data-handling safeguards but is not a general privacy law. In the meantime, protections rest on the constitutional right to privacy (s 49 of the Constitution), the Cybercrime Code Act 2016, and sectoral confidentiality rules.

For businesses, there is currently no registration, breach notification or transfer regime to comply with, and no supervisory authority. The practical risk horizon is the pace at which the 2024 policy is converted into a bill — organisations building data systems for the PNG market should track DICT announcements closely.
