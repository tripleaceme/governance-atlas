Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Tajikistan enacted Law No. 1537 'On the Protection of Personal Data' on 3 August 2018, a CIS-model comprehensive statute governing collection, processing and protection of personal data by state bodies, legal entities and individuals. It follows the familiar regional template: consent as the default legal basis, purpose limitation, confidentiality duties on data holders/operators, and data subject rights to access and correct their data.

The law provides for an 'authorized state body' on personal data protection, determined by the President, which is meant to implement state policy, issue implementing acts, and handle complaints — including imposing administrative penalties. However, no independent, operational data protection authority with a public enforcement record has emerged, and practical guidance on procedures such as database registration is scarce.

For businesses, the compliance reality is consent-driven paperwork rather than active regulatory scrutiny. There is no DPO requirement and no breach notification regime. Cross-border transfers follow the CIS pattern of permitting transfers to jurisdictions ensuring adequate protection, with consent as the practical fallback.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain consent — Personal data collection and processing generally requires the data subject's consent, subject to statutory exceptions for state functions.
  • 02
    Ensure confidentiality and security — Holders and operators of personal data must protect data against unauthorized access, modification, and disclosure with legal, organizational and technical measures.
  • 03
    Respect data subject rights — Individuals may know who processes their data, obtain access, and demand correction or blocking of inaccurate or unlawfully processed data.
  • 04
    Cross-border safeguards — Transfers abroad are permitted to states ensuring adequate protection; otherwise consent or other narrow grounds are needed.
