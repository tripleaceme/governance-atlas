Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Moldova is in the final weeks of a generational transition. The framework currently in force is Law No. 133 of 8 July 2011 on Personal Data Protection, a Convention 108/Directive 95/46-era law that requires controllers to register processing operations with the National Center for Personal Data Protection (CNPDP), observe lawfulness and proportionality, and respect data subject rights of access, rectification and objection. Enforcement under it operates through the Contravention Code, with modest fines — a recent published penalty was MDL 6,500 (about EUR 330).

On 25 July 2024 Parliament adopted Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection, which substantially transposes the GDPR and becomes applicable on 23 August 2026 — this month. It replaces Law 133/2011 with GDPR architecture: accountability duties for controllers and processors, expanded data subject rights, 72-hour breach notification, DPO requirements, a GDPR-style transfer regime, and far heavier sanctions, ending the old registration model. The two-year transition period given to organisations is now effectively over.

Anyone dealing with Moldova today should treat Law 195/2024 as the operative benchmark: EU candidate-country alignment is driving convergence with the GDPR, and the CNPDP is repositioning as a GDPR-style supervisory authority. Moldova does not hold an EU adequacy decision, so transfers from the EU still need safeguards.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register processing with the CNPDP — Under Law 133/2011 controllers must notify/register processing operations with the Center before starting them (a duty abolished by Law 195/2024).
  • 02
    Process lawfully and proportionately — Personal data must be processed on a legal basis, for defined purposes, and be adequate and not excessive relative to those purposes.
  • 03
    Respect data subject rights — Individuals have rights of access, rectification, objection and remedies before the CNPDP and courts.
  • 04
    Secure the data — Controllers must implement the organisational and technical security measures required by law and government decisions.
  • 05
    Prepare for the GDPR-style regime — From 23 August 2026, Law 195/2024 imposes accountability, 72-hour breach notification, DPO appointment triggers and materially higher fines.
