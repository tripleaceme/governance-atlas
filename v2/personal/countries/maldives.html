Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Maldives currently has no comprehensive data protection law in force, but the position has moved from 'nothing' to 'bill in progress': in 2026 the Attorney General's Office finalized a government-backed Personal Data Protection Bill, which was submitted to the People's Majlis (sponsored by West Henveiru MP Ali Ibrahim). The bill aims to establish a full legal framework for the collection, processing and use of personal data, define responsibilities of data processors, regulate sensitive data, and control transfers of data to foreign entities.

This follows an earlier Privacy and Personal Data Protection Bill draft released for public consultation in May 2023, which contemplated GDPR-style principles (lawful, fair, transparent processing), data subject rights to access, correct, delete and object, security obligations, breach notification to a new supervisory body, and a Privacy Commissioner with complaint-handling and enforcement powers.

Pending enactment, protections rest on the constitutional right to privacy in the 2008 Constitution and scattered sectoral rules; there is no supervisory authority, registration scheme, breach notification duty or administrative fining regime. Businesses handling Maldivian data should monitor the Majlis process, as the government has signalled a transition period before full compliance is required once the bill passes.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful and transparent processing (proposed) — The bill would require personal data to be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently, generally on the basis of clear, informed consent.
  • 02
    Data subject rights (proposed) — Individuals would gain rights to access, correct, delete and object to processing of their personal data.
  • 03
    Security and breach notification (proposed) — Organizations would need robust security measures and to notify the new supervisory authority and affected individuals of data breaches.
