Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Czechia applies the EU GDPR directly, supplemented by Act No. 110/2019 Coll. on Personal Data Processing, effective 24 April 2019, which replaced the 2000 act and also transposes the Law Enforcement Directive. The Act's notable national choices are a children's online consent age of 15, exemptions and modifications for journalistic, academic and archival processing, and — most distinctively — a full waiver of administrative fines for public authorities and public bodies.

The regulator is the Office for Personal Data Protection (ÚOOÚ) in Prague, an independent authority dating to 2000. It runs annual inspection plans, handles complaints, and publishes opinions and annual reports; its fines are typically modest by Western European standards (thousands to low millions of CZK), though activity has been increasing, including in cookie-banner and telemarketing sweeps.

For businesses, compliance is standard GDPR work with Czech-language transparency, the 15-year consent age for online services, and awareness that public-sector counterparties face no fine exposure — political accountability and corrective orders are the enforcement levers there. The ÚOOÚ also acquired new roles in whistleblowing oversight and remains the contact point for cross-border EDPB cases.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR compliance baseline — Apply the GDPR in full: lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, and accountability documentation.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the ÚOOÚ within 72 hours of a personal data breach and affected individuals where risk is high.
  • 03
    Children's consent at 15 — Obtain parental consent for information society services offered to children under 15 (Section 7, Act 110/2019).
  • 04
    DPIA for high-risk processing — Conduct impact assessments for processing on the ÚOOÚ's published high-risk list.
  • 05
    DPO where required — Appoint a data protection officer where Art. 37 GDPR thresholds are met.
