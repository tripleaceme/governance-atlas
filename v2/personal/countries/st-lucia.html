Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Saint Lucia enacted its Data Protection Act in 2011 (amended in 2015), but the law sat dormant for over a decade. In early 2023 the Government issued a proclamation order partially commencing the Act, bringing the core data protection principles — purpose limitation, accountability, adequacy of safeguards, fairness and transparency — into legal effect. So the recorded status of 'no law' is out of date: Saint Lucia now has an operative, if incomplete, data protection statute. It is a pre-GDPR-style framework covering controllers who process personal data in or from Saint Lucia, with a registration obligation and a Data Protection Commissioner as the oversight office.

Important gaps remain. Provisions granting individuals their operative rights — access, correction, and objection to processing — had not been proclaimed as of the last available reporting, and there is no public evidence of a fully staffed, operational Commissioner's office actively enforcing the law. Penalties are modest by modern standards (fines in the tens of thousands of EC dollars and short imprisonment terms, up to XCD 100,000 for companies), and there is no GDPR-style 72-hour breach notification or DPO mandate.

For practical purposes: businesses handling Saint Lucian personal data should comply with the in-force principles and prepare to register, but the enforcement risk is currently low and the regime should be monitored for the proclamation of the remaining provisions and the operationalisation of the Commissioner.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Comply with the data protection principles — The commenced provisions require fair, transparent processing for specified purposes, with adequate safeguards and accountability for personal data.
  • 02
    Register with the Commissioner — Data controllers must register with the Data Protection Commissioner, describing the categories of data processed and the purposes of processing.
  • 03
    Secure personal data — Controllers must implement appropriate safeguards against unauthorised access, use, disclosure or destruction of personal data.
  • 04
    Restrict onward and overseas disclosure — Personal data should not be disclosed or transferred inconsistently with the purposes collected or without adequate protection.
