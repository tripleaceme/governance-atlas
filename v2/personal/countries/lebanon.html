Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Lebanon's Law No. 81 of 10 October 2018 on Electronic Transactions and Personal Data (in effect since 31 March 2019) is the country's first legislation addressing personal data, but it is a hybrid statute — most of it concerns e-transactions, e-signatures, e-commerce and cybercrime, with one section devoted to personal data. It defines processing broadly (collection, recording, storage, transmission, deletion) and establishes basic conditions of lawful, consented processing along with data subject rights of access and correction.

Its most distinctive — and dated — feature is a licensing model: rather than an independent regulator, oversight sits with the Ministry of Economy and Trade, which issues permits and licenses for personal data processing where required. In practice broad exemptions (consent-based processing, employers processing employee data, businesses processing customer data for legitimate operations, public authorities, non-profits, schools) mean most routine processing needs no permit. The law contains no DPO requirement, no breach notification duty, and no dedicated cross-border transfer regime.

Enforcement has been minimal amid Lebanon's economic and institutional crisis, and civil society (notably SMEX) has criticized the law as falling well short of GDPR-style protection. Companies handling Lebanese data face light formal obligations, but should watch for reform proposals and note that sectoral rules (banking secrecy, telecom) can impose stricter duties than Law 81 itself.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Permit where required — File a declaration and obtain a permit/license from the Ministry of Economy and Trade for personal data processing that does not fall within the law's broad exemptions.
  • 02
    Lawful, consented collection — Collect and process personal data fairly and lawfully, generally on the basis of the data subject's consent or another legitimate ground.
  • 03
    Security and confidentiality — Apply encryption and protective measures when handling personal data, particularly in electronic transactions and online services.
  • 04
    Respect data subject rights — Allow individuals to access and correct personal data held about them.
