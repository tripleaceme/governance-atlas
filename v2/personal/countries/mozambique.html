Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Mozambique still has no dedicated data protection law, so the recorded 'bill in progress' status remains correct — but the bill has advanced materially. The National Institute of Information and Communication Technologies (INTIC) published a draft Personal Data Protection Bill for public consultation in September 2025 and consulted the Council of Europe in October 2025 to align it with international standards. In early 2026 the Council of Ministers approved the Bill, which now awaits debate and approval in the Assembly of the Republic.

The Bill would create Mozambique's first comprehensive regime, including an Autoridade Nacional de Protecção de Dados Pessoais (ANPD) — expected to be built on the existing telecommunications regulator — with powers to investigate complaints, issue regulations and impose sanctions, alongside GDPR-style principles, data subject rights and transfer rules.

Pending enactment, protection rests on the constitutional right to privacy, the Electronic Transactions Law and sectoral confidentiality rules (telecoms, banking, health), none of which amount to a general data protection framework. There is no supervisory authority, registration duty, breach-notification obligation or administrative fine regime today.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Proposed: national data protection authority — The Bill establishes the ANPD with investigation, regulation and sanctioning powers.
  • 02
    Proposed: GDPR-style duties for controllers — The draft sets out lawful-basis processing, data subject rights and security obligations for controllers and processors.
  • 03
    Current: constitutional and sectoral protection only — Until the Bill passes, privacy relies on the Constitution, the Electronic Transactions Law and sector-specific secrecy rules.
