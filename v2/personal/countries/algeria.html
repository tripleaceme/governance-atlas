Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Algeria enacted Law 18-07 in June 2018, but the law only became effectively applicable in August 2023, one year after its supervisory authority, the ANPDP, was installed in August 2022. The law applies to public and private controllers processing personal data in Algeria and follows the classic Francophone model of prior declarations to the authority, with authorizations required for sensitive categories.

A major amendment, Law No. 25-11 of 24 July 2025, moved Algeria significantly closer to GDPR-style accountability: it makes appointing a Data Protection Officer mandatory, requires records of processing activities, logging, data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing, prior consultation with the ANPDP, and introduces a breach-notification duty — the ANPDP must be notified within five days, and data subjects informed where the breach poses a high risk.

Cross-border transfers are permitted only to countries ensuring adequate protection, otherwise requiring ANPDP approval or a listed exception such as consent, contract performance or public interest. Enforcement combines administrative measures (warnings, formal notices, withdrawal of declarations/authorizations) with criminal penalties — fines of DZD 20,000 to 1,000,000 and imprisonment from two months to five years. Enforcement practice is still young: the authority has only been operating since 2022–2023 and is building out its supervisory activity.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Declare processing to the ANPDP — File prior declarations for processing operations and obtain authorization for sensitive data before processing begins.
  • 02
    Appoint a Data Protection Officer — Since Law 25-11 (2025), organizations conducting large-scale or sensitive processing must designate a DPO and maintain records of processing activities.
  • 03
    Notify data breaches within 5 days — Report personal data breaches to the ANPDP within five days and inform affected individuals in clear terms where the breach creates a high risk.
  • 04
    Conduct DPIAs for high-risk processing — Carry out data protection impact assessments and consult the ANPDP before high-risk processing (introduced by the 2025 amendment).
  • 05
    Restrict international transfers — Transfer data abroad only to adequate jurisdictions or under ANPDP approval, consent, or another statutory exception.
