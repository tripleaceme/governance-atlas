Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Bolivia remains one of the last countries in South America without a comprehensive data protection law. There is no data protection authority, no registration regime, no DPO requirement and no breach notification duty. Protection rests on the 2009 Constitution, which guarantees privacy, honor and one's own image and provides the 'Acción de Protección de Privacidad' (a habeas data-style constitutional action allowing individuals to access, correct or delete data held about them).

Partial, sectoral protections exist — notably confidentiality rules in the telecommunications law (Law 164 of 2011) and e-government/digital signature regulations (Supreme Decree 1793), plus banking secrecy and consumer provisions. Criminal law covers some computer-related offenses, but there is no general cybercrime or privacy statute of broad application.

Several draft bills have been introduced — including Draft Law 185/2019 and Draft Law 349/2020-2021, both of which would create an Agency for the Protection of Personal Data — but none has advanced to enactment and legislative momentum has been slow. Companies operating in Bolivia typically apply contractual and constitutional principles as their compliance baseline. Bolivia holds no EU adequacy decision.
