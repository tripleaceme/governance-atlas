Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Federated States of Micronesia has no data protection law in force, but the status has moved from 'nothing' to 'draft in progress': Congressional Bill No. 24-17, which would enact the FSM Personal Data Protection Act of 2025 as a new chapter of Title 16 of the FSM Code, was introduced in the 24th FSM Congress on 17 May 2025 and remains pending as of mid-2026.

The bill is narrow by international standards. It would apply only to core national government departments and agencies that collect, use, store, process, disclose or transfer personal data — it would not bind the four states for purely intrastate activity, nor regulate the private sector. It designates the National Statistics Office as the competent authority, sets out familiar data protection principles (legitimate purpose, minimization, accuracy, retention limits, security, accountability), and creates a private right of action without compensatory damages. If approved, it would take effect 18 months after enactment.

Beyond the bill, only fragments exist: telecommunications confidentiality provisions in Title 21 of the FSM Code, constitutional privacy protections, and a separate Cybersecurity Act 2025 initiative. There is no authority, registration regime, DPO requirement, breach duty or transfer restriction applicable to businesses today.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Proposed: principles for government data (bill) — Under pending Bill 24-17, national government agencies would have to process personal data per principles of legitimate purpose, minimization, accuracy, retention limits, security and accountability.
  • 02
    Proposed: competent authority oversight (bill) — The National Statistics Office would administer the Act, issue guidance and receive complaints for national government processing.
  • 03
    Proposed: private right of action (bill) — Individuals could sue over violations by covered agencies, though the bill excludes compensatory damages.
