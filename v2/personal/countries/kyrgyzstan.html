Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Kyrgyzstan's Law No. 58 'On Personal Information' of 14 April 2008 was one of Central Asia's earliest data protection statutes, and it has been significantly modernized by amendments in 2017, 2021 and 2022. It regulates collection, processing and protection of personal data by public and private 'holders', built on consent or another lawful basis, purpose limitation, and data subject rights of access and correction.

A dedicated regulator — the State Agency for Personal Data Protection under the Cabinet of Ministers — was established in December 2021, giving the law an enforcement arm it previously lacked. Its flagship mechanism is mandatory registration: holders of personal data arrays must register with the Agency via a three-stage online process launched in November 2022 (currently workable mainly for local entities). There is no formal DPO requirement, though a responsible person must be identified at registration, and there is no general breach notification duty to the Agency — but data subjects must be told within a week if their data was passed to third parties without consent.

Cross-border transfers rely on international treaties ensuring adequate protection, data subject consent, or narrow statutory grounds. Enforcement is young and penalties are modest; the practical compliance question for companies operating in Kyrgyzstan is completing registration and consent documentation as the new Agency ramps up activity.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register with the State Agency — Holders (owners) of personal data arrays must register with the State Agency for Personal Data Protection, identifying the person responsible for personal data work.
  • 02
    Consent or legal basis — Collect and process personal data only with the data subject's consent or another basis established by law, for specified lawful purposes.
  • 03
    Notify subjects of unauthorized disclosure — If personal data is transferred to third parties without consent, inform the data subject within one week.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer conditions — Transfer data abroad only under a treaty ensuring adequate protection, with consent, or in limited statutory circumstances.
  • 05
    Data security — Apply protective measures to personal data arrays against loss, unauthorized access and misuse.
