Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Australia's Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) is one of the world's older comprehensive privacy statutes, built around 13 Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) covering collection, use, disclosure, quality, security, access and correction. It applies to federal government agencies and private-sector organisations with annual turnover above AUD 3 million (plus smaller entities in specific categories such as health providers) — the small-business and employee-records exemptions remain distinctive carve-outs slated for review in a second reform tranche. The regulator is the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), which runs the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme: eligible breaches likely to cause serious harm must be assessed within 30 days and notified to the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable.

The Act is in the middle of its biggest overhaul in decades. The Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024 (passed 29 November 2024) delivered the first tranche of reforms: a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy (in force 10 June 2025), a tiered civil penalty regime beneath the headline maximum of AUD 50 million / 3x benefit / 30% of adjusted turnover, an anti-doxxing offence, clarified 'technical and organisational measures' security duties, transparency requirements for automated decision-making (privacy policy disclosures due by December 2026), and authorisation for an OAIC-drafted Children's Online Privacy Code required by December 2026.

Enforcement is real and escalating: the OAIC has pursued civil penalty proceedings against major companies following large-scale breaches, and the new mid-tier penalties give it more usable tools. A second reform tranche — a 'fair and reasonable' processing test, erasure rights, and removal of the small business exemption — remains under development, so compliance programs should be built with GDPR-style obligations on the horizon. Australia does not hold an EU adequacy decision.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Comply with the Australian Privacy Principles — Handle personal information per the 13 APPs: open and transparent management (privacy policy), lawful and fair collection, purpose limitation on use and disclosure, data quality, security (including technical and organisational measures), and access and correction rights.
  • 02
    Notifiable Data Breaches scheme — Assess suspected eligible data breaches within 30 days; where serious harm is likely and not remediated, notify the OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable with recommended steps.
  • 03
    Cross-border accountability (APP 8) — Before disclosing personal information overseas, take reasonable steps to ensure the recipient complies with the APPs; the discloser generally remains liable for the recipient's acts unless an exception (e.g. informed consent, substantially similar foreign law) applies.
  • 04
    Automated decision transparency — Under the 2024 amendments, privacy policies must disclose kinds of personal information used in, and decisions made by, computer programs that significantly affect individuals (compliance required by December 2026).
  • 05
    Avoid serious invasions of privacy — Since 10 June 2025, individuals can sue in tort for serious invasions of privacy (intrusion upon seclusion or misuse of information) that are intentional or reckless — an exposure beyond OAIC enforcement.
