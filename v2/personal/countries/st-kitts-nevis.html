Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

St. Kitts & Nevis occupies an unusual middle ground: the National Assembly passed the Data Protection Act, 2018 (Act No. 5 of 2018) on 4 May 2018 and it was gazetted in June 2018, but the Act only takes effect on a date fixed by the Minister for Justice by commencement order — and no such order has ever been published. As of the most recent authoritative reporting, the Act remains not in force, so there are no operative data protection obligations, no supervisory authority, and no enforcement.

The dormant Act follows the Caribbean/Commonwealth model (influenced by the OECS harmonization effort and similar to other regional Data Protection Acts), covering processing by public and private bodies, consent-based processing, security safeguards, and an Information Commissioner as regulator once commenced. Until then, privacy protection rests on the constitutional protection of privacy of home and property and on sectoral confidentiality rules (e.g., in financial services legislation).

For practical purposes the jurisdiction should be treated as having no enforceable general privacy law today, but with a ready-made statute that could be switched on quickly by ministerial order — worth monitoring for organizations in financial services and citizenship-by-investment sectors that handle large volumes of personal data.
