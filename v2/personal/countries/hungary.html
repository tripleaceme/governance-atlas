Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Hungary applies the GDPR directly, supplemented by Act CXII of 2011 on Informational Self-Determination and Freedom of Information (the "Infotv."), which was amended in 2018 to fit the GDPR. Distinctively, the Infotv. extends GDPR-style rules to processing outside the GDPR's scope (such as law enforcement and national security files) and contains notable provisions on deceased persons' data, allowing relatives to exercise rights for up to five years after death. Hungary kept the GDPR's default digital consent age of 16.

The regulator is the NAIH, which enforces both the GDPR and the domestic act and also supervises freedom of information. It is an assertive enforcer with a steady stream of fines — including high-profile cases on AI-based voice analysis, unlawful CCTV and data-subject-rights failures — and it drew international attention with its ex officio investigation into Pegasus spyware use. Fines follow the GDPR ceilings of EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide turnover.

Compliance is standard GDPR: lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs, DPOs where triggered, and Chapter V transfer mechanics, plus Hungarian specifics on employee monitoring (regulated in the Labour Code) and deceased persons' data.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and transparency — Processing requires a GDPR Art. 6 basis with clear information; the Infotv. extends equivalent duties to processing outside the GDPR's scope.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report personal data breaches to the NAIH within 72 hours and keep an internal breach register.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public bodies and organisations doing large-scale monitoring or special-category processing must designate a DPO.
  • 04
    Handle minors' and deceased persons' data properly — Under-16s need parental consent for information-society services; close relatives can exercise certain rights of deceased data subjects for five years.
  • 05
    Frame international transfers — Transfers outside the EU/EEA require adequacy, SCCs or another Chapter V safeguard.
