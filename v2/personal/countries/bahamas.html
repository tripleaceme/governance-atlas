Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Bahamas was an early Caribbean mover, enacting the Data Protection (Privacy of Personal Information) Act in 2003, modeled on OECD-era principles. It applies to data controllers established in The Bahamas (and foreign controllers using equipment there) and imposes classic fair-processing duties — fair obtaining, purpose limitation, accuracy, retention limits, security, and a subject access right. A Data Protection Commissioner (a corporation sole) enforces the Act through enforcement, information, and prohibition notices, and can prosecute summary offences.

By modern standards the Act is thin: there is no registration requirement, no DPO duty, no breach notification obligation, no specific children's rules, and penalties top out at BSD 100,000 on conviction on information. Cross-border transfers are not conditioned on adequacy — instead the Commissioner holds a discretionary power to prohibit particular transfers likely to cause damage or distress.

A full replacement is imminent. The draft Data Protection Bill, 2025 went through public consultation and was passed by the lower house of Parliament in late 2025; it introduces GDPR-style principles, expanded data subject rights, a strengthened Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, and cross-border transfer provisions, with coverage of biometrics, AI, and digital assets. As of August 2026 the 2003 Act remains the law in force, so professionals should comply with it today while preparing for the substantially stricter incoming framework.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain and process data fairly — Collect personal data fairly, for specified lawful purposes, and do not use or disclose it in ways incompatible with those purposes.
  • 02
    Keep data accurate, relevant, and time-limited — Ensure data is accurate and up to date, adequate and not excessive, and retained no longer than necessary.
  • 03
    Secure personal data — Take appropriate security measures against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure, destruction, or loss.
  • 04
    Answer subject access requests — Inform individuals whether their personal data is held and provide a copy on request within the statutory period.
  • 05
    Comply with Commissioner notices — Respond to enforcement, information, and prohibition notices — non-compliance is a criminal offence.
