Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Uganda's Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019 (now Cap. 97) is one of East Africa's established regimes, fleshed out by the Data Protection and Privacy Regulations, 2021. It applies to anyone collecting or processing personal data of Ugandan residents — including offshore entities, as the regulator clarified in 2025 guidance — and is supervised by the Personal Data Protection Office (PDPO), an office established under NITA-U that has grown into an active regulator.

The regime is registration-heavy: every data collector, processor and controller must register with the PDPO through its online portal, and registration must be renewed annually — failure to register is itself an offence. Cross-border transfers are permitted only with the data subject's consent or where the receiving jurisdiction provides protection at least equivalent to the Act, and controllers must keep records justifying each transfer for inspection.

Enforcement has moved from theory to practice, with the PDPO issuing registration decisions, its first fines, and Uganda recording criminal data protection convictions in 2025. Corporate offenders face fines up to 2% of annual gross turnover on conviction, alongside individual fines and potential imprisonment. Uganda holds no EU adequacy decision.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register and renew with the PDPO — Every data collector, processor and controller must register in the data protection register; registration is valid for one year, with renewal applied for within three months before expiry.
  • 02
    Secure data and report breaches — Personal data must be protected against unauthorized access, and breaches must be notified to the PDPO (and affected data subjects) as soon as practicable, following the procedures in the Regulations.
  • 03
    Justify cross-border transfers — Transfer or storage outside Uganda requires consent or an adequately protective destination, with records of the legal basis and safeguards kept for inspection.
  • 04
    Obtain parental consent for children — Processing personal data relating to a child requires the prior consent of a parent or guardian.
  • 05
    Designate a data protection contact — Institutions must designate a person responsible for data protection compliance under the 2021 Regulations.
