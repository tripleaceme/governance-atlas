Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Switzerland's fully revised Federal Act on Data Protection (revFADP), adopted in September 2020 and in force since 1 September 2023, modernised Swiss law to GDPR-comparable standards while keeping a distinctly Swiss design. It applies to private controllers and federal bodies, has extraterritorial effect on processing with effects in Switzerland, and requires foreign companies meeting thresholds to appoint a Swiss representative. The EU renewed Switzerland's adequacy status on 15 January 2024, so EU-Swiss data flows remain free.

The headline structural difference from the GDPR is sanctions: there are no corporate administrative fines. Instead, intentional violations of specific duties (information, disclosure, security, transfer rules) are criminal offences punishable by fines up to CHF 250,000 imposed on responsible individuals — managers, not companies — which changes personal risk calculations for executives. Breach notification runs to the FDPIC "as soon as possible" only where high risk is involved, and a DPO ("data protection advisor") is optional.

Compliance staples include records of processing (with an exemption for companies under 250 employees and low-risk processing), privacy by design and default, DPIAs for high-risk processing, and transfer checks against the Federal Council's country adequacy list. The FDPIC supervises and investigates but cannot itself levy fines; prosecution runs through cantonal criminal authorities.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Inform at collection — Controllers must proactively inform data subjects when collecting personal data, including recipient countries for transfers — a duty backed by criminal fines.
  • 02
    Keep records and build privacy in — Maintain records of processing activities (SMEs under 250 staff with low-risk processing are exempt) and apply privacy by design and default.
  • 03
    Run DPIAs for high-risk processing — A data protection impact assessment is required where processing may pose high risk; consult the FDPIC unless a data protection advisor has been appointed.
  • 04
    Report qualifying breaches promptly — Notify the FDPIC as soon as possible of breaches likely to result in high risk, and affected individuals where needed for their protection.
  • 05
    Check transfers against the adequacy list — Cross-border disclosure is permitted to countries the Federal Council deems adequate; elsewhere use SCCs or other recognised safeguards.
