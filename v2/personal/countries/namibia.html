Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Namibia still has no comprehensive data protection law, so the recorded status of 'bill in progress' remains correct. Government published a Draft Data Protection Bill in 2021, and in August 2025 the Minister of Information and Communication Technology, Emma Theofelus, said the Bill was in its final stage and would be tabled in Parliament around September–October 2025. As of early 2026, however, the Bill had still not been enacted, and reporting through February 2026 confirms no comprehensive law is in force.

The Bill would establish a supervisory authority, data subject rights and controller obligations broadly aligned with international norms; civil society groups (including the Association for Progressive Communications) have criticised later drafts for watering down rights and independence safeguards. It forms part of a wider digital-law package alongside cybercrime legislation and the operationalisation of electronic signatures under the Electronic Transactions Act 2019.

In the meantime, protection rests on Article 13 of the Namibian Constitution (privacy of home, correspondence and communications), common-law privacy actions, and sectoral confidentiality rules in areas like banking and telecommunications. There is no regulator, no registration, no breach-notification duty and no administrative fine regime until the Bill passes.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Proposed: supervisory authority and registration — The draft Bill would create a data protection supervisory authority and compliance duties for controllers and processors once enacted.
  • 02
    Proposed: data subject rights — The draft provides access, correction and related rights for data subjects, though rights groups say later drafts weakened some protections.
  • 03
    Current: constitutional and sectoral protection only — Until enactment, privacy is protected via Article 13 of the Constitution, common law and sector-specific confidentiality rules.
