Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Djibouti moved from having no data protection law to one of the region's most modern frameworks in a single step: the National Assembly adopted the country's first Digital Code (Code du Numérique, Law No. 019/AN/23/9ème L) on 30 June 2025, promulgated in July 2025. The Code runs to nearly 800 articles across eight books, with a 156-article data protection regime that is unmistakably GDPR-modelled — extraterritorial scope over foreign controllers targeting or monitoring people in Djibouti, 72-hour breach notification, DPOs, and turnover-based fines.

The law creates the Commission Nationale de Protection des Données Personnelles (CNDP) as an independent supervisory authority with administrative and financial autonomy, operating a tiered filing system: simple declarations for routine processing and prior authorisation for high-risk processing such as sensitive, genetic, biometric or criminal-record data. Administrative penalties reach DJF 70 million or 5% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher, with criminal penalties up to 10 years' imprisonment.

The critical caveat is institutional: the CNDP had not yet been operationally established as of the most recent reporting, so active enforcement has not begun and there is no regulator website or guidance yet. Local civil-society critics have also raised concerns about parts of the broader Code being used to restrict expression. Organisations touching Djiboutian data should begin aligning now, since the substantive obligations are already law.

Key obligations

  • 01
    File declarations or seek authorisation — Routine processing requires a declaration to the CNDP; high-risk processing (sensitive data, genetic/biometric data, criminal records, processing affecting contractual rights) requires prior authorisation.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers must notify the CNDP within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach and inform affected data subjects without undue delay where the risk is high.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public authorities, organisations conducting large-scale systematic monitoring, and large-scale processors of sensitive or conviction data must appoint a Data Protection Officer.
  • 04
    Transfer only to adequate destinations — Cross-border transfers are permitted only to countries or organisations offering protection equivalent to Djiboutian standards, as assessed and published by the CNDP.
  • 05
    Comply with extraterritorial scope — Foreign controllers and processors offering goods or services to, or monitoring the behaviour of, individuals in Djibouti fall within the law's scope.
