Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Nepal's Individual Privacy Act, 2075 (in force 18 September 2018) was the country's first dedicated privacy statute, giving effect to the constitutional right to privacy (Article 28 of the 2015 Constitution). Supplemented by the Individual Privacy Regulation 2020 and provisions of the National Civil and Penal Codes, it regulates the collection, retention, analysis, processing and publication of personal information by public authorities and Nepali-incorporated corporate bodies, covering identifiers such as ethnicity, religion, education, biometric data, ID numbers and criminal records.

The Act is privacy-focused rather than a full GDPR-style data protection code: it establishes consent requirements and confidentiality duties, but has no supervisory authority, no registration or DPO requirements, no breach notification regime, and no dedicated cross-border transfer mechanism. Enforcement runs through the courts — violations are criminal offences prosecuted on complaint, punishable by fines up to roughly NPR 30,000 and/or up to three years' imprisonment, alongside compensation claims.

The practical reality is thin enforcement: cases are rare, remedies are individual rather than systemic, and compliance culture is nascent. Businesses processing Nepali data should anchor on documented consent and purpose limitation. Discussion of a modern data protection law and authority continues, but no bill has been enacted.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Consent for collection and use — Personal information may only be collected, retained or used with the individual's consent and for the stated purpose.
  • 02
    Purpose limitation and confidentiality — Data collected by public bodies or corporations must not be used or disclosed for other purposes, and holders must keep it secure and confidential.
  • 03
    Protect sensitive categories — Heightened restraint applies to sensitive matters — health, ethnicity, religion, biometric data, and criminal history — whose publication without consent is an offence.
  • 04
    Guardian consent for minors — Collection and use of children's personal information requires the consent of their guardian.
