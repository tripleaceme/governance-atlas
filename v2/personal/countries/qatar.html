Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Qatar's Law No. 13 of 2016 on Personal Data Privacy Protection (PDPPL) was the first standalone national data protection law in the GCC. In force since 2017, it applies to personal data processed electronically by public and private entities in Qatar (the QFC free zone has its own separate regime). The law rests on principles of transparency, fairness and human dignity, requires consent or another lawful basis, and grants rights of access, correction and erasure.

Administration and enforcement now sit with the National Data Privacy Office (NDPO) within the National Cyber Governance and Assurance Affairs (NCGAA) division of the National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA). The NDPO has issued a substantial set of executive guidelines fleshing out the law — including a 72-hour breach notification expectation, a required 'Personal Data Management System' (incorporating DPIAs and records of processing), permits for sensitive-data processing, and penalty methodology. Distinctively, the PDPPL contains explicit protections for children online, requiring guardian consent and controls on websites directed at minors.

Enforcement has begun in earnest: the NDPO issued compliance rulings against an ICT company in December 2024 and an e-commerce company in March 2025, ordering strengthened administrative and technical safeguards. Fines can reach QAR 5 million, so companies processing Qatari data should align with the NDPO's guideline suite rather than the bare 2016 text.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Consent or legitimate purpose — Process personal data fairly and lawfully, based on consent or a legitimate purpose, and only to the extent necessary for that purpose.
  • 02
    Permit for sensitive data — Obtain a permit from the regulator before processing data of a special nature (health, ethnic origin, religious beliefs, criminal records, children's data).
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify the NDPO/NCGAA and affected individuals within 72 hours of breaches likely to cause serious harm, including the nature of the breach, data affected and mitigation taken.
  • 04
    Personal Data Management System — Implement a PDMS with appropriate technical and organizational precautions, DPIAs and records of processing, following NDPO guidelines.
  • 05
    Children's online protections — Website operators directed at children must obtain verified guardian consent, be transparent about data use, and honor guardian deletion and control rights.
