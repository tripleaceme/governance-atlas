Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Malawi's Data Protection Act, 2024 was gazetted in February 2024 and came into force on 3 June 2024, replacing the thin data-protection provisions of the Electronic Transactions and Cyber Security Act 2016 with a comprehensive, GDPR-inspired regime. It applies to automated and manual processing by controllers and processors, sets out familiar principles (lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, security) and grants data subjects rights of access, correction, erasure and objection.

The Malawi Communications Regulatory Authority (MACRA) is the designated Data Protection Authority and operates a dedicated portal (dpa.mw). Distinctive features include a tiered registration regime for 'significant' data controllers and processors (those processing data of more than 10,000 subjects or data of national importance), mandatory DPOs for large-scale processing, 72-hour breach notification, and transfer restrictions based on adequacy or safeguards such as binding corporate rules.

Enforcement is young: the framework is in place and MACRA has been standing up registration and guidance, but there is little published enforcement history yet. Penalty exposure under the Act is primarily criminal (fines and imprisonment) rather than GDPR-scale administrative fines.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register if a significant controller/processor — Entities processing data of more than 10,000 data subjects or data of national importance must register with MACRA before processing.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Controllers must notify MACRA of personal data breaches within 72 hours, and inform affected data subjects where the breach poses a high risk to their rights.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO for large-scale processing — Organisations engaged in large-scale data processing must appoint a data protection officer to oversee compliance.
  • 04
    Restrict cross-border transfers — Personal data may leave Malawi only where the destination offers adequate protection or mechanisms such as binding corporate rules are in place.
  • 05
    Obtain verified guardian consent for children — Processing minors' data requires parent or legal guardian consent, with mechanisms to verify age and guardian identity.
