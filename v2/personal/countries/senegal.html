Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Senegal was one of Africa's data protection pioneers: Law No. 2008-12 of 25 January 2008 established a comprehensive regime and created the Commission de Protection des Données Personnelles (CDP), one of the continent's first operational supervisory authorities. The law follows the pre-GDPR French/CNIL model — built around declarations and prior authorisations to the regulator rather than accountability tools like DPOs and breach notification.

Controllers must declare most processing to the CDP and obtain prior authorisation for sensitive categories such as biometric and genetic data, criminal records and national ID numbers. There is no mandatory breach-notification duty and DPO appointment is discretionary, which makes the regime feel dated next to newer African laws. Cross-border transfers are permitted only to countries offering 'sufficient protection', assessed by the CDP.

A modernising replacement bill — addressing biometrics, big data, AI and a strengthened authority — has been in preparation since 2019 under the Digital Senegal strategy, with a finalisation workshop held by the CDP and the digital ministry in October 2024, but it had not been adopted as of mid-2026, so the 2008 law remains in force. The CDP is active in awareness-raising and issues regular deliberations, though monetary enforcement has historically been light.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Declare processing to the CDP — Most processing operations must be notified to the CDP before they begin; genetic, biometric, criminal-record, interconnection and national-ID processing require prior authorisation.
  • 02
    Lawful and fair processing — Personal data must be processed for legitimate, specified purposes with a lawful basis, and data subjects must be informed and able to exercise access, rectification and objection rights.
  • 03
    Restrict international transfers — Transfers are prohibited unless the destination country ensures sufficient protection; the CDP evaluates adequacy case by case, with narrow exceptions (vital interest, public safety, legal claims).
  • 04
    Secure sensitive data — Health, biometric and other sensitive data attract heightened conditions and prior authorisation; controllers must implement security and confidentiality measures.
