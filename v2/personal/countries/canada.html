Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Canada's federal private-sector law remains PIPEDA (2000), a principles-based statute applying to organizations that collect, use, or disclose personal information in commercial activity (provinces with substantially similar laws — Quebec, Alberta, British Columbia — apply their own regimes intra-provincially, with Quebec's modernized Law 25 the strictest). PIPEDA is built on ten fair-information principles: accountability, meaningful consent, limiting collection, safeguards, openness, and individual access.

The long-planned replacement died on the order paper: Bill C-27 (the Consumer Privacy Protection Act plus the AI and Data Act) was killed when Parliament was prorogued in January 2025 and has not been reintroduced, so Canada is still governed by a 25-year-old statute with no administrative monetary penalty regime. The OPC investigates and makes findings but cannot fine — only certain offences (e.g., failing to report breaches) carry fines up to CAD 100,000 via prosecution. Mandatory breach reporting (since 2018) requires reporting to the OPC and notifying individuals as soon as feasible when there is a real risk of significant harm, and keeping breach records for 24 months.

For international business the key fact is that Canada retains its EU adequacy decision for PIPEDA-covered commercial organizations, reaffirmed in the European Commission's January 2024 adequacy review. Enforcement reality is ombudsman-style — negotiated compliance agreements and reputational pressure — which is why reform pressure continues, including federal AI-governance consultations reported in 2026.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Designate an accountable privacy officer — Appoint an individual accountable for the organization's compliance and implement policies and practices to give effect to the ten principles.
  • 02
    Obtain meaningful consent — Collect, use, and disclose personal information only with valid, meaningful consent appropriate to the sensitivity of the information, for purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate.
  • 03
    Report and record breaches — Report breaches of security safeguards posing a real risk of significant harm to the OPC and notify affected individuals as soon as feasible; keep records of all breaches for 24 months.
  • 04
    Safeguard and limit data — Protect personal information with security appropriate to its sensitivity, limit collection and retention to what is necessary, and ensure comparable protection when outsourcing (including abroad).
  • 05
    Provide access and correction — On request, tell individuals what personal information is held, how it is used and disclosed, and give access and correction rights.
