Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Angola's Law No. 22/11 of 17 June 2011 (Lei da Protecção de Dados Pessoais) is a Portuguese-inspired, pre-GDPR statute that remains the country's primary data protection law. Its central mechanism is regulatory gatekeeping: virtually all processing must be either notified to or authorized in advance by the Agência de Protecção de Dados (APD), which must decide on notifications within 30 days. Transfers abroad — including use of cloud servers outside Angola — require APD authorization and an adequacy assessment, with exceptions for express written consent, international treaties, contract performance and humanitarian purposes.

Although the law dates from 2011, the APD only became truly active in recent years and is now one of the busier enforcement stories in Lusophone Africa: it has issued fines including a USD 150,000 penalty against a telecoms provider for failing to notify multiple processing activities, and its published penalty bands run from roughly USD 7,000–15,000 for notification failures to USD 75,000–150,000 for major violations. In October 2024 it issued Instruction 02/APD/2024 addressing the duty to notify data breaches, partially filling a gap in the original law, which contains no general breach-reporting mechanism.

For companies in Angola's oil, banking and telecoms sectors, the practical priorities are inventorying processing activities and regularizing notifications/authorizations with the APD — the enforcement pattern so far punishes failure to file more than substantive processing conduct. The law has no DPO requirement and no modern accountability tooling, and reform discussions to modernize the 2011 framework are worth monitoring.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Notify or seek authorization from the APD — All processing of personal data is subject to prior notification or authorization by the APD; failure to notify is a punishable misdemeanor (≈USD 7,000–15,000) and has driven the largest fines to date.
  • 02
    Obtain authorization for international transfers — Transfers to countries without adequate protection — including offshore cloud hosting — require APD authorization, unless covered by express written consent, treaty, contract necessity, or humanitarian grounds.
  • 03
    Respect consent and processing principles — Processing requires the data subject's consent or another legal ground, with heightened rules for sensitive data requiring APD authorization.
  • 04
    Notify data breaches per APD instruction — Instruction 02/APD/2024 of 24 October 2024 sets out the duty to notify the APD in case of a data breach, supplementing the silent 2011 statute.
