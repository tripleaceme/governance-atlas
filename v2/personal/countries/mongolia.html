Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Mongolia's Law on Personal Data Protection was adopted on 17 December 2021 and took effect on 1 May 2022, replacing a 1995-era individual-secrets law. It was passed as part of a package with the Cybersecurity Law, Public Information Transparency Law and Electronic Signature Law, together forming Mongolia's first comprehensive digital-governance framework. The law applies to individuals, legal entities and organisations collecting, processing and using personal data in Mongolia, and is broadly consent-based, with heightened rules for sensitive categories including health, genetic and biometric data.

Distinctively, Mongolia has no dedicated data protection authority: compliance oversight sits with the National Human Rights Commission of Mongolia, while the Ministry of Digital Development and Communications handles digital-security aspects. There is no registration regime and no general DPO mandate. Sanctions are imposed under the general Law on Violations, with fines ranging from roughly MNT 500,000 to MNT 20,000,000 (about USD 150–5,800) depending on the offence.

Enforcement is still maturing — the NHRC's role is closer to a human-rights ombudsman than an inspecting regulator, and practice on issues like breach handling and cross-border transfers (generally consent-based) is thin. Businesses operating in Mongolia should anchor compliance in documented consent, purpose limitation, security measures for sensitive data, and the specific rules on workplace monitoring and CCTV that the law introduced.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Consent-based collection — Collect and process personal data with the data subject's consent, obtained for defined purposes, with stricter conditions for sensitive data such as health, genetic and biometric information.
  • 02
    Purpose and use limitation — Use personal data only for the purposes disclosed at collection and do not disclose it to third parties without a lawful basis.
  • 03
    Security of processing — Implement organisational and technical measures to protect personal data, aligned with the parallel Cybersecurity Law obligations.
  • 04
    Workplace monitoring rules — Follow the specific conditions for employee monitoring and CCTV, including informing affected individuals.
  • 05
    Cross-border care — Ensure a consent or treaty basis before transferring personal data outside Mongolia.
