Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Saudi Arabia's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) — issued by Royal Decree M/19 in September 2021 and substantially amended in 2023 — is the Kingdom's first comprehensive data protection law. It entered into force on 14 September 2023 with a one-year grace period that ended in September 2024. It applies to any processing of personal data of individuals residing in Saudi Arabia, including by entities outside the Kingdom, and is supplemented by Implementing Regulations and separate Data Transfer Regulations.

The regime is GDPR-influenced but distinctive: controllers must register on SDAIA's National Data Governance Platform, appoint DPOs in defined high-risk cases, notify breaches to SDAIA within 72 hours, and run risk assessments before cross-border transfers. Criminal liability attaches to unlawful disclosure of sensitive data (up to two years' imprisonment) — a notable departure from purely administrative regimes.

Enforcement is real and accelerating: SDAIA, the competent authority since September 2023, had issued roughly 48 enforcement decisions by early 2026, with failure to honor data subject rights and inadequate security controls among the most commonly cited findings. Companies serving the Saudi market should treat PDPL compliance as an active supervisory risk, not a paper exercise.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and consent — Process personal data only with the data subject's consent or another narrow statutory basis (actual interest, contract, legal obligation); consent is revocable at any time.
  • 02
    Register with SDAIA and appoint a DPO where required — Register controller profiles on the National Data Governance Platform and appoint a registered DPO for public entities, large-scale monitoring, or large-scale sensitive-data processing.
  • 03
    72-hour breach notification — Notify SDAIA within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach that may harm personal data or data subjects, and notify affected individuals where there is potential harm.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer controls — Complete a data transfer risk assessment and rely on adequacy, appropriate safeguards (e.g., standard contractual clauses, binding common rules) or exemptions before transferring data outside the Kingdom.
  • 05
    Privacy notices, RoPA and data subject rights — Maintain records of processing activities, provide clear privacy notices, and honor rights of access, correction, destruction and consent withdrawal within regulated timelines.
