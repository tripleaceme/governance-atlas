Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The recorded law needs updating: Botswana repealed the never-fully-effective Data Protection Act 32 of 2018 and replaced it with the Data Protection Act 18 of 2024, assented on 29 October 2024 and brought into force on 14 January 2025. The new Act is significantly closer to the GDPR, with detailed duties for controllers, processors and sub-processors, dedicated children's-data provisions, and expanded powers for the Information and Data Protection Commission (IDPC).

Distinctive features include GDPR-scale sanctions — fines up to BWP 50 million or 4% of global turnover — 72-hour breach notification to the IDPC, mandatory DPOs (whose details must be registered) for large-scale monitoring or sensitive processing, and a soft data-localisation rule requiring that a copy of personal data transferred abroad be retained in Botswana during processing.

Enforcement is ramping up rather than mature: the IDPC is the independent supervisor and has been building out its compliance programme, but the Act has been fully effective only since January 2025 and published enforcement actions remain limited. The 2018 Act's history (its commencement was postponed by successive ministerial orders) is a reminder to verify commencement instruments when assessing Botswana.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Data controllers must notify the IDPC within 72 hours of discovering a breach and inform affected individuals without undue delay where their rights or freedoms are at risk.
  • 02
    Appoint and register a DPO — Organisations conducting large-scale monitoring or processing sensitive data must appoint a Data Protection Officer and register the DPO's contact details with the Commission.
  • 03
    Comply with transfer and localisation rules — Cross-border transfers require adequacy or contractual safeguards, and a copy of transferred personal data must be retained in Botswana during the processing period.
  • 04
    Protect children's data — The Act contains dedicated rules for processing children's personal data, including guardian consent.
  • 05
    Meet controller/processor accountability duties — Detailed statutory obligations apply to controllers, processors and sub-processors, backed by fines of up to BWP 50m or 4% of global turnover.
