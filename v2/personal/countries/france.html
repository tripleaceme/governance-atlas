Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

France applies the GDPR directly, supplemented by the Loi Informatique et Libertés (Law 78-17 of 6 January 1978) — one of the world's oldest data protection laws, comprehensively rewritten in 2018-2019 to fit the GDPR. The national law fills the spaces the GDPR leaves to member states: it sets the digital consent age at 15 (with joint parent-child consent below that), keeps special regimes for health, biometric and criminal-record data, and preserves some prior formalities for sensitive health research processing.

Supervision belongs to the CNIL, the 1978-founded regulator that remains one of Europe's most influential authorities. The CNIL is a prolific enforcer, and notably uses the ePrivacy framework to fine cookie and tracker violations directly — bypassing the GDPR one-stop-shop — which has produced some of Europe's largest penalties against global tech platforms. It pairs enforcement with extensive practical guidance widely used across the French-speaking world.

For most organisations the compliance picture is standard GDPR: lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPOs where required, and Chapter V transfer tools. The distinctive French angles are the age-15 rule for minors, strict CNIL cookie-consent doctrine, and active sectoral rules on health data hosting (HDS certification).

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and transparency — Every processing operation needs a GDPR Art. 6 basis and clear information to data subjects; CNIL doctrine on cookies requires genuine accept/refuse choices.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches must be reported to the CNIL within 72 hours and to individuals when the risk is high.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public bodies and organisations doing large-scale monitoring or special-category processing must designate a data protection officer.
  • 04
    Handle minors' data with care — Information-society services relying on consent need joint parental consent for users under 15.
  • 05
    Frame international transfers — Transfers outside the EU/EEA require adequacy, SCCs with transfer impact assessments, or another Chapter V mechanism.
