Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Bhutan has no comprehensive data protection law and no dedicated data protection authority. The closest framework is the Information, Communications and Media Act of Bhutan 2018 (ICM Act), the omnibus statute for telecom, broadcasting and online content. It contains scattered privacy-relevant provisions — online privacy (ss. 336–343), security of payment and personal information (ss. 344–346), unsolicited e-mail (s. 347), communications with children (ss. 348–351), and unlawful disclosure of data (s. 388) — enforced by the Bhutan InfoComm and Media Authority (BICMA), a partly independent sector regulator that can investigate complaints and award compensation.

These provisions amount to a minimal, electronic-communications-focused privacy regime rather than a data protection law: there are no general data subject rights, no principles-based obligations on controllers, no registration, DPO, breach notification, or transfer rules. Government practice is evolving faster than legislation — the GovTech Agency has issued data management guidance, and the rollout of Bhutan's blockchain-based National Digital Identity (NDI) system (NDI Act 2023) has intensified debate about the need for a standalone data protection law.

For now, organizations dealing with Bhutanese data face only the ICM Act's e-privacy offences and general confidentiality expectations. Watch the digital identity program as the likely catalyst for future comprehensive legislation.
