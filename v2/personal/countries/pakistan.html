Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Pakistan still has no comprehensive data protection law. A Personal Data Protection Bill has been through repeated drafts since 2018 (2020, 2021, a Cabinet-approved 'final draft' in May 2023, and a further Personal Data Protection Act 2025 draft), but as of mid-2026 none has been passed by the National Assembly and Senate. The drafts are GDPR-inspired — consent-based processing, data subject rights, 72-hour breach notification, and a proposed National Commission for Personal Data Protection (NCPDP) as regulator — but have drawn sustained criticism for broad state exemptions and data localization requirements for 'critical' personal data.

In the meantime, partial protections apply. Article 14 of the Constitution recognizes the dignity and privacy of the home; the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act (PECA) 2016 — amended most recently in January 2025 — criminalizes unauthorized access to data and certain misuse of personal information; and sectoral rules (banking, telecom licensing by the PTA) impose confidentiality duties. None of this gives individuals general rights of access, correction or erasure, and there is no supervisory authority for personal data.

The practical read for businesses: no registration, DPO, or breach filing obligations exist today, but the direction of travel is toward a strict regime with localization and a powerful new Commission. The 2025 PECA amendments also signal a state-security-heavy approach to digital regulation, which is the central tension delaying the bill.
