Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Kuwait has no comprehensive data protection statute passed by parliament. Its main framework is regulator-issued: the Data Privacy Protection Regulation (DPPR), first adopted by the Communication and Information Technology Regulatory Authority (CITRA) as Resolution No. 42 of 2021 and replaced by Resolution No. 26 of 2024, effective 19 February 2024. The Governance Atlas entry citing 'DPPR (2021)' should be updated to the 2024 regulation. The DPPR applies to CITRA-licensed telecom and ICT service providers — a category broad enough in practice to cover operators of networks, websites, smart applications and cloud services in Kuwait — regardless of where the data is processed.

Substantively the DPPR is GDPR-flavored: transparency and accessible privacy policies, informed consent before collection, purpose limitation, security and encryption requirements, data subject rights to access, rectify, delete and withdraw consent, notice before transferring personal data outside Kuwait, and a strikingly short 24-hour breach notification window to CITRA. Sanctions run through CITRA's licensing regime — fines and suspension of operating licenses — rather than a standalone administrative fine schedule.

For meetings, the key framing is that Kuwait's regime is sectoral secondary legislation, not an economy-wide statute: entities outside CITRA licensing largely face only general confidentiality and constitutional privacy principles. Enforcement by CITRA is opaque, with little published penalty practice. Watch for movement toward a full national data protection law as neighbors (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Jordan) have enacted theirs.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Informed consent and transparency — Licensed service providers must obtain user consent before collecting personal data and publish clear, accessible privacy policies stating purposes and uses.
  • 02
    24-hour breach notification — Data breaches must be notified to CITRA within 24 hours.
  • 03
    Security and encryption — Providers must legally protect data and ensure appropriate processing, encryption, and safeguards against unauthorized access.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer notice — Users must be informed (and consent obtained) before their personal data is transferred outside Kuwait.
  • 05
    Honor data subject rights — Users can withdraw consent and request access, rectification, and deletion of their data.
