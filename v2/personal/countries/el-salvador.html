Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

El Salvador adopted its first comprehensive privacy law, the Ley de Protección de Datos Personales (LPDP), on 12 November 2024, alongside a companion Cybersecurity and Information Security Law; both were published on 15 November 2024 and entered into force in late November 2024. The LPDP applies to public- and private-sector processing of Salvadorans' personal data and grants data subjects ARCO-POL rights (access, rectification, cancellation, opposition, portability, oblivion/erasure, and limitation).

Supervision is distinctive: rather than an independent DPA, enforcement belongs to the new State Cybersecurity Agency (ACE), an executive-branch agency whose director is appointed by the President — a design human rights groups (including Human Rights Watch) have criticized as lacking independence and potentially threatening privacy and free expression. The ACE issues guidelines, supervises compliance, resolves disputes, and imposes sanctions ranging from roughly US$408.80 to US$16,352 depending on severity.

Key operational duties include designating a data protection delegate to handle ARCO-POL requests, notifying security breaches to the ACE, the Attorney General's Office, and affected individuals within 72 hours, and restricting international transfers to destinations offering adequate protection with the data subject's express consent. The regime is very young — regulations and enforcement practice are still forming — so compliance expectations are evolving through ACE guidance.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Honor ARCO-POL rights — Establish procedures and a responsible delegate to receive and resolve data subjects' requests for access, rectification, cancellation, opposition, portability, erasure, and limitation within statutory deadlines.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report security breaches (theft, leakage, loss of data) to the Agencia de Ciberseguridad del Estado, the Fiscalía General de la República, and affected data subjects within no more than 72 hours.
  • 03
    Obtain valid consent and process lawfully — Process personal data on a lawful basis — generally the data subject's informed consent — for specified purposes, with stricter rules for sensitive data.
  • 04
    Control international transfers — Transfer personal data abroad only to recipients in countries with adequate protection, with the data subject's express consent, and ensure recipients meet the same legal standards.
  • 05
    Implement security measures — Adopt technical and organizational measures appropriate to the risk, coordinated with the obligations of the companion Cybersecurity and Information Security Law.
