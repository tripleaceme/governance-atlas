Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019), fully effective since 1 June 2022, is a GDPR-inspired law covering private and most public-sector processing, with extraterritorial reach over foreign businesses offering goods or services to, or monitoring, people in Thailand. It requires a lawful basis for processing, with a strong emphasis on explicit, unbundled consent, and grants data subjects rights to access, correction, erasure, portability and objection.

The Office of the Personal Data Protection Committee (PDPC) has built out the regime through sub-regulations and guidelines on consent, notification, breach reporting, DPOs and cross-border transfers. Distinctive features include a detailed minor-consent framework tied to the Civil and Commercial Code (guardian consent below age 10, dual consent for minors 10–20) and a 72-hour breach notification rule with a formal exemption-request process for late filings.

Enforcement moved from a soft-touch phase into real sanctions: the PDPC issued its first administrative fines and in August 2025 imposed penalties totalling over THB 21.5 million, signalling the end of the grace-period approach. Administrative fines run to THB 5 million per violation, alongside criminal liability and statutory/punitive damages in civil claims.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish a lawful basis and valid consent — Processing requires a statutory basis; where consent is used it must be an explicit, informed, affirmative act — bundled or implied consent is invalid.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Notify the PDPC within 72 hours of a breach likely to pose a risk; high-risk breaches must also be notified to data subjects; late filing requires a justified exemption request within 15 days.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — A DPO is mandatory for public authorities and for organizations whose core activities involve regular large-scale monitoring or processing of sensitive data.
  • 04
    Control cross-border transfers — Under s.28, transfers require an adequate destination, appropriate safeguards such as BCRs or contractual clauses, consent, or another exception.
  • 05
    Handle minors' data carefully — Obtain guardian consent for children under 10 and dual (minor + guardian) consent for those 10–20, with age-verification measures and child-comprehensible notices.
