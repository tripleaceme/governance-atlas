Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Commonwealth of Dominica has no comprehensive data protection law in force and no dedicated bill actively progressing through Parliament as of mid-2026. Privacy protection rests on the Constitution of Dominica, which protects the privacy of the home and property, and on general common-law confidentiality principles.

A few sectoral and adjacent instruments touch on data: the Electronic Evidence Act, 2010 supports the integrity and admissibility of electronic records, electronic transactions and cybercrime-related legislation address some misuse of computer data, and the Government's own web privacy policy governs data collected via official online platforms. None of these creates general obligations for private-sector controllers, data subject rights, or a supervisory authority.

The most likely path to legislation is regional: the Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States launched an initiative in 2022 to develop harmonized data protection laws for member states including Dominica. Until such a law is adopted, organizations processing Dominican residents' data face no local registration, breach-notification, or fining regime and typically apply the standards of other jurisdictions (e.g., GDPR) contractually.
