Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Marshall Islands still lacks a comprehensive data protection law, but there is a notable new development: the Personal Data Protection Act 2025 (P.L. 2025-43), which commenced on 7 October 2025 and is codified at Title 6, Chapter 4 of the Marshall Islands Revised Code. Crucially, the Act applies only to 'core Government ministries and agencies' of the national government — it does not regulate the private sector, state-owned enterprises, or local governments, and it excludes law enforcement and national security processing.

Within its public-sector scope, the Act sets out data protection principles, designates the Economic Policy, Planning and Statistics Office (EPPSO) as competent authority (with education, coordination and reporting duties rather than fines), establishes a complaints process and a private right of action with remedies, and regulates data sharing among ministries with a safe-harbor mechanism. It is best understood as groundwork for the country's digital ID program; the government has signaled plans for a broader data protection law as that program develops (reportedly by 2028).

For businesses, the practical position is unchanged: no registration, no DPO, no breach notification and no transfer restrictions apply to private-sector processing. Protection otherwise rests on the constitutional right to privacy and fragmented provisions in sectoral statutes such as the Persons with Disabilities Act (2015) and Gender Equality Act (2019). The Marshall Islands holds no EU adequacy decision.
