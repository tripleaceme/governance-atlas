Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Nicaragua enacted Law No. 787, the Personal Data Protection Law, on 29 March 2012, with an implementing regulation (Decree 36-2012) issued the same year. On paper it is a habeas-data-style comprehensive law: it protects individuals' informational self-determination, requires consent for processing, grants rights of access, rectification, and cancellation, and mandates that data files be registered with a supervisory body, the Dirección de Protección de Datos Personales (DIPRODAP), which the law places under the Ministry of Finance (MHCP).

The regime's defining feature is that it has never been operationalized: DIPRODAP was never actually established or staffed, the data-file registry required by the law does not function, and no meaningful administrative enforcement has taken place in the fourteen years since enactment. Legal commentators consistently cite the absence of the authority as the law's central weakness — obligations exist in text but complaints have no functioning regulator to receive them, leaving judicial habeas data actions under the Constitution as the practical remedy.

For organizations handling Nicaraguan data, the law still formally applies — consent, purpose limitation, and security duties are on the books — but compliance risk is driven more by reputational and constitutional-litigation exposure than by regulatory action. The broader governance environment also means reform toward an independent, active DPA is unlikely in the near term.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain consent for processing — Personal data may generally only be processed with the data subject's consent, with heightened protection for sensitive data categories.
  • 02
    Register data files — Data file owners are required to register their files in the registry contemplated by Law 787 — though the DIPRODAP registry has never been made operational in practice.
  • 03
    Respect access, rectification and cancellation rights — Data subjects may access data held about them and demand correction or deletion, backed by the constitutional habeas data guarantee.
  • 04
    Secure personal data — Adopt technical and organizational measures to protect personal data against loss, alteration, and unauthorized access or treatment.
