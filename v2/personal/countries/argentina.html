Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Argentina was a regional pioneer: Law 25.326 of 2000 was Latin America's first comprehensive data protection law, modeled on the pre-GDPR European directive, and earned Argentina an EU adequacy decision in 2003 that was reaffirmed in the European Commission's January 2024 review. The law applies to public and private processing, is consent-centric, and requires mandatory registration of databases with the regulator, the AAIP.

Its age shows. There is no general breach notification duty, no DPO requirement, no portability right, and fines remain capped at token amounts — up to ARS 100,000 per infringement under the scales unified by AAIP Resolution 126/2024 (with a 500x multiplier cap for accumulated conduct), figures widely criticized as symbolic after years of inflation. The AAIP compensates with a more proactive posture: its 2022-2026 strategic plan, the unified sanctions regime, and guidance on AI and public-sector compliance signal tightening supervision.

Multiple reform bills to replace the 25-year-old statute — including Bill 1948-D-2025 and the AAIP's own draft — are pending in Congress, proposing GDPR-style rights (portability, protection against automated decisions), breach notification and meaningful fines, but none had been enacted as of August 2026. Companies should comply with the current registration and transfer rules while tracking the reform closely.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register databases — Public and private databases must be registered in the National Registry of Databases (RNBD); operating unregistered databases is unlawful.
  • 02
    Obtain informed consent — Express, informed consent is the default legal basis for processing; sensitive data enjoys heightened protection and generally cannot be compelled.
  • 03
    Ensure data quality — Data must be adequate, relevant, not excessive, accurate and used only for the purpose for which it was collected.
  • 04
    Apply security measures — Controllers must adopt the technical and organizational security measures recommended by the AAIP (Resolution 47/2018) to protect data confidentiality and integrity.
  • 05
    Safeguard international transfers — Transfers to non-adequate countries require AAIP-approved contractual clauses, consent or another legal exception.
