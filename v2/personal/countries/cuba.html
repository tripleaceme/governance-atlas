Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Cuba's Law 149/2022 on Personal Data Protection, published in the Official Gazette on 25 August 2022 and in force since February 2023, is the country's first comprehensive data protection statute, implementing the data protection right recognized in the 2019 Constitution. It applies to public and private processing of personal data in physical or digital registers, files, and databases, and grants data subjects rights of access, rectification, erasure once the purpose is fulfilled, opposition to harmful processing, and opposition to automated decision-making.

Its architecture diverges sharply from GDPR-style regimes: there is no independent supervisory authority. Oversight and enforcement run through existing state structures, with the Ministry of Justice playing the central role, and the law creates categories of 'responsible' and 'designated persons' inside entities rather than independent DPOs. Reported sanctions include fines up to CUP 20,000 and suspension of non-compliant databases, alongside civil liability. Critically, broad carve-outs cover national security, defense, and public order — critics note state security processing effectively escapes scrutiny.

For practical purposes, foreign engagement with Cuban personal data is limited, and there is little public evidence of enforcement practice, published guidance, or complaint mechanisms. Treat the law as in force but institutionally immature, with rights primarily exercisable against civil entities via administrative channels and the courts.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish consent or legal authorization — Process personal data only with the data subject's consent or where legally authorized, for defined purposes.
  • 02
    Honor data subject rights — Provide mechanisms for access, rectification, erasure (once purpose is fulfilled), opposition, and objection to automated decision-making.
  • 03
    Assign responsibility for processing — Identify the responsible and designated persons accountable for lawful handling of each register, file, or database.
  • 04
    Protect data security and confidentiality — Maintain security and confidentiality of personal data across physical and digital media; violations expose entities to fines, database suspension, and civil claims.
