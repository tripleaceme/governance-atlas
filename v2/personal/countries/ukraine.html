Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Ukraine's framework is still the Law on Personal Data Protection No. 2297-VI, adopted on 1 June 2010 and in force since 1 January 2011. It is a pre-GDPR, Convention 108-style law applying to controllers and processors handling personal data of individuals in Ukraine. Since 2014 supervision has sat with the Parliamentary Commissioner for Human Rights (Ombudsman) rather than a dedicated data protection authority — an unusual arrangement the pending reform would end.

The law requires a legal basis for processing, notification of high-risk processing to the Ombudsman, designation of a responsible person for such processing, and observance of data subject rights. Enforcement is real but light: the Ombudsman investigates complaints and conducts inspections, but fines under the Code of Administrative Offences top out around UAH 34,000 (under EUR 1,000), so reputational and criminal exposure matter more than administrative penalties.

The big story is reform: Draft Law No. 8153 passed its first reading on 20 November 2024 and, as of mid-2026, remains in preparation for a second reading. It would align Ukraine with the GDPR and Convention 108+, create an independent supervisory authority, add 72-hour breach notification and DPO duties, and introduce fines up to UAH 150 million or 8% of annual turnover. EU accession pressure makes eventual adoption likely, so anyone building compliance in Ukraine should plan against GDPR-grade requirements.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish a legal basis — Processing requires consent or another ground listed in the law; sensitive-data processing is restricted to "particular risk" conditions.
  • 02
    Notify high-risk processing — Controllers must notify the Ombudsman of processing that poses a particular risk to data subjects' rights and freedoms.
  • 03
    Designate a responsible person — Organisations conducting high-risk processing must designate a responsible person or structural unit for data protection.
  • 04
    Honour data subject rights — Individuals may access their data, learn the sources and purposes of processing, and demand rectification or erasure of unlawfully processed data.
  • 05
    Secure the data — Controllers must protect personal data from unlawful access, loss and destruction, following the Ombudsman's model procedures.
