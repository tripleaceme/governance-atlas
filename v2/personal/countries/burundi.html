Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Burundi's status has changed: after years with no comprehensive privacy statute, the National Assembly unanimously adopted a personal data protection bill on 15 January 2026, and Law No. 1/03 of 10 March 2026 on the protection of personal data was promulgated in March 2026. The law, structured in eight chapters, is now Burundi's primary hard law on personal data. It grants data subjects rights to information, access, correction, erasure, portability and objection, regulates automated decision-making, requires stronger controls (including impact assessments) for higher-risk processing, and covers data transfers and breach notification. The Ministry of Interior sponsored the legislation, citing the digitised voter register, expanding e-government and the planned biometric national ID as drivers.

The law creates an independent administrative authority to oversee data protection, but the authority's missions and organisation are left to a later decree, and no supervisory body is operational yet. Sanctions for breaches are provided in the law and sit alongside the Penal Code, the 2022 Cybercriminality Law and sectoral confidentiality rules (banking, health, labour).

Enforcement reality: this is a brand-new regime with no regulator constituted, no implementing decrees and no track record. Businesses operating in Burundi should monitor the establishment of the authority and treat detailed compliance parameters (deadlines, fines, registration mechanics) as not yet settled. Detailed English-language analysis of the text is still scarce.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Respect data subject rights — Controllers must honour rights of information, access, correction, erasure, portability and objection established by the law.
  • 02
    Controls on higher-risk processing — The law requires stronger safeguards, including impact assessments, for higher-risk processing and regulates automated decision-making.
  • 03
    Breach notification and transfer rules — The law addresses personal data breach notification and conditions for data transfers, with detailed mechanics expected in implementing measures.
