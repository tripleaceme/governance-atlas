Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Bangladesh has moved from draft to enacted law. After years of circulating drafts, the interim government gazetted the Personal Data Protection Ordinance, 2025 (Ordinance No. 61 of 2025) on 6 November 2025 — the country's first standalone data protection legislation — alongside a companion data governance ordinance. A February 2026 amendment ordinance softened the regime (narrowing data localization to 'restricted' and critical-information-infrastructure data and replacing imprisonment of company directors with monetary fines), and in April 2026 Parliament passed the permanent Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 (Act No. 63 of 2026), which repeals and replaces the ordinance.

The framework is consent-centric and declares individuals — not the state — owners of their data. It borrows from India's DPDP model: a tiered regime with heavier duties on 'significant' data controllers (Chief Data Officer appointment, advance approval for large-volume sensitive-data transfers), verifiable parental consent for under-18s, and cross-border transfer grounds based on consent, contract or the data subject's benefit, with sovereignty and security screens for sensitive identifiers such as NIDs and biometrics.

Enforcement reality: this is a brand-new regime with its supervisory institution (reported as the National Data Management Authority) still being organized, subordinate regulations pending, and full enforcement widely reported to activate around May 2027. Penalty figures differ across sources (fixed taka amounts in the Act versus turnover-percentage fines under the ordinance), so specifics should be verified against the final gazetted Act before relying on them.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Explicit consent before processing — Obtain explicit, informed consent before collecting, storing, using or transferring personal data, with the data subject recognized as owner of their data and able to withdraw consent.
  • 02
    Children's data safeguards — Obtain verifiable consent from a parent, legal guardian or authorized representative before processing personal data of anyone under 18.
  • 03
    Significant-controller duties — Significant data controllers must appoint a qualified Chief Data Officer as their representative before the Authority and obtain advance approval for large-volume sensitive-data transfers.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer and localization compliance — Transfer data abroad only on permitted grounds, document destination-country safeguards on request, and keep restricted and critical-information-infrastructure data within Bangladesh.
  • 05
    Security and breach notification — Maintain data protection and security measures and notify the Authority of breaches causing significant harm within the timeframe regulations will prescribe.
