Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Oman's Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL), issued by Royal Decree 6/2022, is the Sultanate's first comprehensive data protection statute and one of the stricter regimes in the Gulf: it is consent-centric, requiring a lawful basis (usually express consent) for most processing, and layering a government permit requirement on top for sensitive categories such as health, genetic and biometric data. It applies to controllers and processors handling personal data in Oman, with the Ministry of Transport, Communications and Information Technology (MTCIT) acting as regulator rather than a standalone data protection authority.

Executive Regulations issued in 2024 (Ministerial Decision 34/2024) fleshed out the law — DPO appointment, breach notification within 72 hours, permits for sensitive data, and data subject rights to access, correction, deletion and portability. The transition period ended on 5 February 2026, so the PDPL is now fully enforceable and MTCIT has moved into an active supervisory role.

Enforcement reality is two-track: relatively modest administrative fines (up to OMR 2,000 per violation, plus permit suspension or cancellation), but severe criminal penalties — up to OMR 500,000 — for serious breaches such as unlawful cross-border transfer or processing of sensitive data without a permit. Businesses operating in Oman should treat the permit and consent requirements as the distinctive compliance risks.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain express consent — Processing generally requires the data subject's express consent unless another statutory basis applies; consent for children must come from a guardian.
  • 02
    Permit for sensitive data — A permit from MTCIT is required before processing sensitive personal data (health, genetic, biometric, criminal-record data), with applications decided within 45 days.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO — Controllers must designate a personal data protection officer; the regulator prefers the DPO to be based in Oman for direct communication.
  • 04
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Breaches posing risk to data subjects' rights must be reported to MTCIT within 72 hours, including nature, impact and mitigation; high-risk breaches must also be notified to affected individuals.
  • 05
    Control cross-border transfers — Transfers abroad require data subject consent, and MTCIT approval where sensitive data will be stored or processed outside Oman; unlawful transfers attract criminal fines.
