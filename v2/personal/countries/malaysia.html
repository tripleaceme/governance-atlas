Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Malaysia's Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709) governs the processing of personal data in commercial transactions and is enforced by the Personal Data Protection Commissioner via the Personal Data Protection Department (JPDP) under the Ministry of Digital. Notably, it does not apply to the federal and state governments or to purely non-commercial processing. The Act rests on seven data protection principles, with certain classes of data users required to register with the Commissioner.

The regime was substantially modernized by the Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024, whose phases came into force through 2025: direct obligations on data processors, mandatory data breach notification to the Commissioner within 72 hours (and to individuals where significant harm is likely), mandatory DPO appointment above processing thresholds from 1 June 2025, a new data portability right, biometric data classed as sensitive, and higher penalties — up to RM 1 million and/or three years' imprisonment for breaching the principles.

Cross-border transfers were also overhauled: the old ministerial white-list was abolished on 29 April 2025 in favor of transfers to jurisdictions with substantially similar laws or with an ensured equivalent level of protection, operationalized through Transfer Impact Assessments under the CBPDT Guidelines. With new guidelines, an active registration regime and a modernized toolkit, Malaysian enforcement is expected to sharpen considerably.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Comply with the seven principles — Processing must satisfy the General, Notice & Choice, Disclosure, Security, Retention, Data Integrity and Access principles; breach now carries fines up to RM 1 million and/or 3 years' imprisonment.
  • 02
    Register where required — Data users in prescribed classes must register with the Personal Data Protection Commissioner and keep registrations current.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Since the 2024 Amendment and 2025 Guidelines, controllers must notify the Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach, and affected individuals where significant harm is likely.
  • 04
    Appoint a DPO above thresholds — From 1 June 2025, controllers and processors meeting the thresholds in the DPO Guidelines must appoint at least one Data Protection Officer.
  • 05
    Assess cross-border transfers — Conduct Transfer Impact Assessments and transfer only to jurisdictions with substantially similar laws or where an equivalent level of protection is ensured.
