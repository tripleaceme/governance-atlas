Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Palau has no comprehensive data protection or privacy law, no data protection authority, and no general obligations on companies handling personal data. Privacy protection rests on the constitutional right against unreasonable search and invasion of privacy, plus scattered sectoral confidentiality rules (e.g., for government records and the digital residency program's cybersecurity regulations issued by the Ministry of Finance).

Recent legislative activity has focused on security and online harms rather than data protection proper. A 2024 law criminalized the non-consensual distribution of sensitive and intimate images, creating criminal penalties and civil remedies. In October 2025 the Senate unanimously passed Senate Bill No. 12-9 (the Cybersecurity Act), Palau's first comprehensive national cybersecurity framework, which moved to the House of Delegates — but it is a security statute, not a GDPR-style privacy law.

Some low-quality online sources refer to a 'Palau Privacy Act 2019', but this could not be verified against any official Palauan source and should not be relied on. For a professional audience the practical position is: no registration, no DPO, no breach notification and no transfer restrictions apply, and contractual protections are the main tool when handling Palauan personal data.
