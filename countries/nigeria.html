Executive summary

6 min read · plain-English summary — not legal advice

The Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) was signed into law in June 2023, replacing the NDPR 2019 as Nigeria's primary data protection framework. It applies to anyone processing the personal data of people in Nigeria — whether or not the organisation itself is based in Nigeria.

The Act follows the broad shape of the GDPR: it establishes lawful bases for processing, rights for data subjects (access, correction, erasure, portability, objection), and an independent regulator — the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC) — with the power to investigate and fine.

The most distinctive feature is the concept of data controllers and processors of "major importance", who must register with the NDPC, appoint a Data Protection Officer, and file annual compliance audit returns through licensed DPCOs.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish a lawful basis before processing — consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public interest, or legitimate interest.
  • 02
    Register with the NDPC if you qualify as a controller or processor of major importance.
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO if you are of major importance; the DPO may be internal or outsourced.
  • 04
    Report breaches within 72 hours to the NDPC when there is a risk to data subjects, and notify affected individuals for high-risk breaches.
  • 05
    Restrict cross-border transfers to countries with adequate protection, or apply safeguards such as contractual clauses or binding corporate rules.
