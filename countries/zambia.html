Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Zambia's Data Protection Act, 2021 (Act No. 3 of 2021) is a GDPR-influenced statute with a distinctly strict local flavor: it imposes mandatory registration of controllers and processors, mandatory DPO appointment, a 24-hour breach-notification window (among the world's tightest), and hard data-localization rules — sensitive personal data must be stored and processed inside Zambia, and even non-sensitive data can leave only with data-subject consent plus Commissioner-approved contracts, intragroup schemes, ministerial prescription or specific authorization. It applies extraterritorially to anyone processing the personal data of people in Zambia.

For several years the law outpaced its institutions — a Commissioner was appointed in 2023 but the office wasn't functional. That changed decisively: the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) is now fully operational, launched an online registration portal at dataprotection.gov.zm, and set a registration deadline of 30 April 2025, with non-registration punishable by a ZMW 200,000 fine and/or up to five years' imprisonment. General penalties scale to 2% of annual turnover for bodies corporate.

Practically, 2025–2026 marks Zambia's shift from legislative intent to regulatory action. Businesses — including foreign SaaS, fintech and telecom providers serving Zambian users — should confirm ODPC registration, appoint a DPO, map where sensitive data is hosted (localization is the most commercially disruptive requirement), and build 24-hour breach response into incident plans.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register with the ODPC — Anyone processing personal data of individuals in Zambia — wherever based — must register as a controller and/or processor via the ODPC portal; the deadline was 30 April 2025 and non-registration is an offence.
  • 02
    Localize sensitive data — Sensitive personal data (which includes children's data) must be stored and processed within Zambia; other personal data requires consent plus approved safeguards to cross the border.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 24 hours — Data controllers must notify the Data Protection Commissioner of data breaches within 24 hours — one of the shortest windows globally.
  • 04
    Appoint a data protection officer — Controllers and processors are obliged to designate a DPO responsible for compliance.
  • 05
    Process on a lawful basis with consent safeguards — Processing requires a lawful basis, with parental consent for children and heightened protection for sensitive categories; violations expose corporates to fines up to 2% of annual turnover.
