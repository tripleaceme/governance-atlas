Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law No. 151 of 2020 (in force since October 2020) created the country's first comprehensive framework, but it remained largely dormant for five years pending its Executive Regulations. That changed on 1 November 2025, when Prime Ministerial Decree No. 816 of 2025 issued the Executive Regulations and operationalized the Personal Data Protection Center (PDPC), under the Ministry of Communications and Information Technology, as the licensing and enforcement authority. Compliance deadlines under the transition period ran into 2026, so 2026 is effectively year one of real enforcement.

The regime is notably permission-heavy compared with the GDPR. Controllers and processors need PDPC licenses or permits for regulated activities; cross-border transfers require a separate license or permit, an adequacy assessment of the destination, and the data subject's explicit consent. DPO appointment is mandatory and formalized — DPOs must be registered with the PDPC, meet qualification requirements and pass approved exams, and must file annual privacy reports. Breaches must be notified to the PDPC within 72 hours and to affected individuals within three working days. Children under 15 require documented written parental consent.

Sanctions combine administrative measures with criminal penalties, with fines ranging from EGP 100,000 to EGP 5,000,000 depending on the offense. Any business touching Egyptian personal data — including outsourcing, fintech and health sectors — should treat licensing, DPO registration and transfer permits as immediate 2026 priorities.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain PDPC licenses and permits — Secure the required license or permit from the Personal Data Protection Center for regulated processing, including sensitive data and any cross-border transfer of personal data.
  • 02
    Appoint and register a DPO — Designate a Data Protection Officer registered in the PDPC's DPO registry (qualifications, experience and approved exams required) who monitors compliance and reports annually to the Center.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Report personal data breaches to the PDPC within 72 hours of awareness and inform affected data subjects within three working days.
  • 04
    Control cross-border transfers — Do not transfer, store, share or allow access to personal data outside Egypt without a PDPC license/permit, an adequacy assessment and the data subject's explicit consent.
  • 05
    Protect children's data — Obtain explicit written parental consent before collecting or processing data of children under 15, verify age, and retain documented proof.
