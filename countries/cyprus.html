Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Cyprus, as an EU member state, applies the GDPR directly, supplemented by Law 125(I)/2018 on the Protection of Natural Persons with regard to the Processing of Personal Data, enacted on 31 July 2018. The national law exercises the GDPR's member-state options and applies to controllers and processors established in Cyprus as well as foreign entities targeting or monitoring people there.

Cyprus's main national choices include setting the digital age of consent at 14 — below 14, processing based on a child's consent for online services is lawful only with parental authorisation — plus specific rules on combining genetic and biometric data and on processing for journalistic purposes. The standard GDPR obligations apply: 72-hour breach notification, DPOs where Article 37 triggers are met, records of processing, and Chapter V transfer mechanisms.

Enforcement rests with the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection in Nicosia, who can impose the full GDPR fine tiers on private organisations (up to EUR 20 million or 4% of worldwide turnover) and has issued a steady stream of decisions against banks, employers and marketers. As a small jurisdiction with a large financial and corporate-services sector, Cyprus sees particular regulatory attention on employee monitoring, direct marketing and international data flows.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR compliance baseline — Maintain lawful bases, transparency, records of processing, DPIAs for high-risk processing, and respond to data subject rights requests within one month.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach, and affected data subjects where the risk to them is high.
  • 03
    DPO appointment — Appoint a DPO if you are a public authority or your core activities involve large-scale monitoring or large-scale special-category data processing.
  • 04
    Children's consent — Obtain parental authorisation before processing based on the consent of a child under 14 for information-society services.
  • 05
    Transfer safeguards — Use GDPR Chapter V mechanisms (adequacy, SCCs, BCRs) for transfers outside the EEA.
