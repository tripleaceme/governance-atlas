Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) is the country's first comprehensive data protection law, applying to digital personal data processed in India and to processing abroad connected with offering goods or services to individuals in India. After a two-year wait, the DPDP Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025, bringing the regime to life: the Data Protection Board of India (DPBI) was established the same day as a fully-online adjudicatory body, and MeitY notified a phased commencement calendar (14 November 2025, 14 November 2026, and 14 May 2027 tranches).

The Act is deliberately leaner than the GDPR: it uses 'Data Fiduciary'/'Data Principal' terminology, recognizes only consent and defined 'legitimate uses' as bases, and layers heavier duties (DPO, annual DPIAs and audits, algorithmic due diligence) on government-designated Significant Data Fiduciaries. Distinctive features include verifiable parental consent for everyone under 18 with a ban on tracking and targeted ads at children, registered Consent Managers as an interoperability layer, and a negative-list (blacklist) approach to cross-border transfers rather than adequacy assessments.

Enforcement reality: the Board exists and breach reporting machinery is being stood up, but the substantive obligations and the penalty regime — headline fines up to INR 250 crore per incident — bite fully from mid-May 2027. Businesses operating in India should use the 2025–2027 runway to build consent, breach-response and children's-data workflows now.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Consent or legitimate use — Process digital personal data only with free, specific, informed consent given via clear notice, or for enumerated 'legitimate uses'; consent must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
  • 02
    Security safeguards and breach reporting — Implement reasonable security safeguards (encryption, access control, logging, backups) and, on breach, inform affected Data Principals and file a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours — penalties up to INR 250 crore for safeguard failures.
  • 03
    Children's data protections — Obtain verifiable parental consent (via identity or Digital Locker-based verification) before processing data of under-18s, and do not track, behaviorally monitor or target ads at children.
  • 04
    Significant Data Fiduciary duties — If designated an SDF, appoint an India-based DPO, conduct annual DPIAs and independent audits, and perform algorithmic and transfer due diligence.
  • 05
    Data Principal rights and erasure — Honor rights of access, correction, erasure and grievance redressal, and erase personal data once its purpose is served or consent withdrawn, subject to prescribed retention windows.
