Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Republic of Congo (Brazzaville) adopted Law No. 29-2019 on the Protection of Personal Data on 10 October 2019, a comprehensive statute in the classic Francophone mold: lawful-basis requirements (consent, legal obligation, contract or other legitimate ground), prior declaration of processing to a supervisory authority, data subject rights, and tiered sanctions running from FCFA 8 million to 200 million, rising to 5% of annual turnover for the most serious harm. Private-sector entities were given one year and public bodies two years to come into compliance.

For years the law's main weakness was the absence of its regulator. That changed recently: after Council of Ministers approval in July 2024, Law No. 5-2025 of 29 March 2025 formally created the Commission Nationale pour la Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (CNPD), an independent administrative authority charged with authorizing and controlling processing, handling complaints, and sanctioning violations. The Commission is newly created and its operational machinery (registration portals, guidance, enforcement practice) is still in its infancy.

Practically, businesses operating in Congo should treat 2026 as the start of the enforcement era: the substantive obligations have been binding since 2020-2021, and a regulator now exists to enforce them. Companies handling sensitive or large-scale data should appoint a DPO and prepare declaration filings as the CNPD publishes its procedures.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish a lawful basis — Personal data must be collected with consent, a legal obligation, contractual necessity, or another legitimate basis, and processed fairly and transparently.
  • 02
    Declare processing to the authority — The law follows the Francophone model of prior notification/declaration of processing operations to the supervisory authority (now the CNPD).
  • 03
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public entities and private companies engaged in large-scale or sensitive data processing must designate a data protection officer.
  • 04
    Respect data subject rights and security — Controllers must safeguard privacy rights and secure data; violations carry fines of FCFA 8m–200m, up to 5% of turnover in the gravest cases.
