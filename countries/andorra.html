Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Andorra's Qualified Law 29/2021 on Personal Data Protection (LQPD), adopted 28 October 2021 and in force since 17 May 2022, replaced the 2003 law and deliberately mirrors the GDPR in structure and substance: same principles, data subject rights, accountability tools (records of processing, DPIAs, DPOs) and 72-hour breach notification. Implementing regulations for the law and for the supervisory agency were approved in 2022.

The supervisory authority is the Andorran Data Protection Agency (APDA), an independent body that also issues guidance on cookies and emerging topics such as AI. A key commercial point: Andorra has held an EU adequacy decision since 2010, reaffirmed in the European Commission's January 2024 review of pre-GDPR adequacy decisions, so personal data can flow freely from the EEA to Andorra.

The main divergence from the GDPR is the sanctions regime: fines are far lower than GDPR levels, topping out at €100,000 for very serious infringements. The LQPD treats EEA states, EU-adequate jurisdictions and Convention 108+ parties as safe destinations for onward transfers, and requires parental authorisation for consent-based processing of data of minors under 16.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR-style accountability — Maintain records of processing, implement privacy by design/default and carry out impact assessments for high-risk processing.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the APDA without undue delay and where feasible within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable breach.
  • 03
    DPO appointment — Designate a Data Protection Officer in the GDPR-mirroring mandatory cases, including public entities and large-scale processing.
  • 04
    Transfer safeguards — Transfer data outside Andorra only to EEA/adequate/Convention 108+ destinations or with appropriate safeguards.
  • 05
    Parental consent for minors — Obtain and verify legal-representative consent before consent-based processing of data of children under 16.
