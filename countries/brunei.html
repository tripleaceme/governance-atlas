Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Brunei enacted its first comprehensive data protection law, the Personal Data Protection Order, 2025 (PDPO), on 8 January 2025, with most substantive provisions commencing on 1 January 2026 after a phased transition — so the recorded 'consultation/draft' status is out of date: the law is now in force. Closely modeled on Singapore's PDPA, it applies to private-sector organizations of all kinds, whether or not they are incorporated or physically present in Brunei; the public sector is outside its scope.

The Authority for Info-communications Technology Industry (AITI) is the responsible authority. Core duties include consent-based collection and use, purpose limitation, security arrangements, appointment of a compliance officer (DPO), a comparable-protection rule for overseas transfers, and Singapore-style breach notification: assess the breach, then notify AITI as soon as practicable and no later than three calendar days where significant harm is likely, with affected individuals also informed.

Penalties mirror Singapore's modern regime — up to BND 1 million or 10% of the organization's annual turnover in Brunei. As enforcement only began with the 2026 commencement, there is little enforcement track record yet; expect AITI guidance and a pragmatic early posture while organizations complete compliance programs.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain consent and limit purposes — Organizations may collect, use and disclose personal data only with consent (or a statutory exception) and for purposes a reasonable person would consider appropriate.
  • 02
    Appoint a compliance officer — Each organization must designate at least one officer responsible for ensuring PDPO compliance.
  • 03
    Protect and notify — Reasonable security arrangements are required; notifiable breaches (significant harm) must be reported to AITI within 3 calendar days of assessment and to affected individuals.
  • 04
    Restrict overseas transfers — Personal data may only be transferred outside Brunei where a comparable standard of protection is ensured.
