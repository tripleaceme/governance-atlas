Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Guatemala still has no comprehensive data protection law in force as of mid-2026. The main vehicle is Bill (Iniciativa) No. 6105, the Personal Data Protection Law, introduced on 23 June 2022. In 2025-2026 it advanced through the Congressional Transparency and Probity Commission and was returned to the plenary of Congress, where it must survive three debates (or a two-thirds 'national emergency' vote for fast-track approval). A newer initiative, No. 6572 on personal data protection and digital rights, has also been presented, so the legislative picture is active but unresolved.

In the meantime, privacy protection rests on partial instruments: the 1985 Constitution (which recognizes privacy and habeas data-style guarantees), and Decree 57-2008, the Law on Access to Public Information, which contains limited rules on sensitive personal data held by public bodies. There is no supervisory authority, no registration or breach-notification regime, and no general private-sector obligations.

For businesses, this means Guatemala is currently a low-regulation environment on paper, but multinational groups typically apply regional (GDPR-style) standards voluntarily. Anyone processing Guatemalan data should track Bill 6105/6572, since an approved law would introduce a supervisory authority and GDPR-inspired obligations.
