Executive summary

6 min read · plain-English summary — not legal advice

The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) was signed in 2013 but only came fully into force on 1 July 2021 after a one-year grace period. It gives effect to the constitutional right to privacy through eight processing conditions — accountability, processing limitation, purpose specification, further-processing limitation, information quality, openness, security safeguards, and data subject participation.

Two features make POPIA unusual. First, it protects juristic persons — companies' information is covered, not just individuals'. Second, instead of a DPO, every organisation's head is by default its Information Officer, who must be registered with the Information Regulator and can delegate to deputies.

POPIA also regulates direct marketing (opt-in for electronic channels), automated decision-making, and special personal information — with prior authorisation from the Regulator required for some processing categories.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Meet the eight processing conditions for all personal information — of natural and juristic persons alike.
  • 02
    Register your Information Officer with the Information Regulator before processing begins, and train deputies where delegated.
  • 03
    Compile a PAIA manual and maintain documentation of processing operations.
  • 04
    Report breaches as soon as reasonably possible to the Regulator and affected data subjects, unless an exemption applies.
  • 05
    Justify cross-border transfers — adequate protection in the recipient country, binding corporate rules, contract, consent, or necessity.
