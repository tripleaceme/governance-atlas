Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Contrary to a common assumption that Equatorial Guinea has no data protection law, the country does have one on the books: Law No. 1/2016 on the Protection of Personal Data, a comprehensive statute available only in Spanish. It grants citizens the right to consent to the use of their personal data and to be informed and notified of any use of it, establishes general data protection requirements and data subject rights, and provides for penalties of up to FCFA 15 million for violations. The previously recorded status of 'no law' should therefore be corrected.

The critical caveat is enforcement: the law creates a supervisory authority — the Órgano Rector de Protección de Datos Personales — but that body has never been made operational. There is no functioning registration system, no published guidance, no enforcement track record, and very limited English-language analysis of the law's detailed provisions (transfer rules, breach duties and children's provisions are not clearly documented in accessible sources). In practical terms, compliance risk is low today but the legal obligations formally exist.

For a professional audience: treat Equatorial Guinea as a jurisdiction with a dormant law rather than a legal vacuum. Multinationals typically apply group-level standards, monitor for any move to stand up the authority, and note that the country participates in regional (CEMAC/African Union) discussions that could eventually revive implementation.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Obtain consent for data use — The law grants individuals the exclusive right to consent to the use of their personal data; processing without consent or another legal basis breaches the law.
  • 02
    Inform data subjects — Individuals must be informed and notified of uses made of their personal data.
  • 03
    Avoid unlawful processing — Violations of the law's data protection provisions expose organizations to fines of up to FCFA 15,000,000, even though enforcement machinery is not yet in place.
