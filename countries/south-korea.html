Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

South Korea's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), enacted in 2011 and heavily amended in 2020 and 2023, is one of Asia's strictest and most actively enforced privacy regimes. It applies to virtually all public and private data controllers, with extraterritorial reach over foreign companies serving Korean users. The independent Personal Information Protection Commission (PIPC) consolidates rulemaking, investigation and sanctioning powers, and has fined global tech companies tens of billions of won.

The 2023 amendment (effective from September 2023, with provisions phasing in through 2025) unified the previously separate online/offline rules, standardized breach notification at 72 hours, expanded cross-border transfer mechanisms beyond consent (adequacy designations, certifications, contractual disclosure), introduced rights around automated decision-making, and moved penalties to a turnover basis — up to 3% of total annual revenue, with the burden on the company to show revenue unrelated to the violation. In February 2026 the National Assembly went further, authorizing fines of up to 10% of total revenue for repeat, intentional, or mass-scale (10m+ subjects) breach cases, and tying accountability to senior management.

Distinctive features include mandatory designation of a privacy officer (CPO) for all controllers, guardian consent for children under 14, a domestic representative requirement for large foreign providers, and separate stringent rules for resident registration numbers and pseudonymized data. Korea has held an EU adequacy decision since December 2021, making it a reliable hub for EU data flows. Enforcement is real and frequent — this is a jurisdiction to treat with GDPR-level seriousness.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and consent granularity — Processing requires consent or another statutory basis, with separate consent for sensitive data, marketing, and third-party provision; consent requests must be itemized.
  • 02
    72-hour breach notification — Breaches meeting thresholds must be reported to the PIPC and affected individuals within 72 hours of awareness.
  • 03
    Designate a privacy officer (CPO) — Every controller must designate a Chief Privacy Officer; large controllers face qualification/independence requirements, and large foreign providers need a domestic representative.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer safeguards — Overseas transfers require consent, contractual disclosure, PIPC-recognized adequacy, or certification — and PIPC can order transfer suspension.
  • 05
    Safeguards and minimization — Controllers must implement mandated technical/organizational security measures, minimize collection, and destroy data once the purpose is achieved.
