Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Eritrea has no data protection legislation, no draft bill in progress, and no supervisory authority — it is one of the few African states with essentially no movement toward a data protection framework. There are no statutory duties around lawful basis, transparency, individual rights or breach reporting, and no complaint mechanism for individuals.

The only relevant texts are the unimplemented 1997 Constitution, whose Article 18 guarantees privacy of communications and correspondence but has never been brought into operative force, and the Communications Proclamation No. 102/1998, which regulates telecommunications, broadcasting and post under the Ministry of Transport and Communications rather than protecting personal data as such.

In practice, organizations processing data of Eritrean residents face no domestic compliance obligations, but also operate in an environment with pervasive state control of telecommunications and extremely low internet penetration. Any counterpart obligations will come from foreign law (e.g. GDPR extraterritoriality) rather than Eritrean law.
