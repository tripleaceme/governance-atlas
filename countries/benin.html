Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Benin's data protection regime sits inside its Digital Code — Law No. 2017-20 of 20 April 2018, amended by Law No. 2020-35 of 2021 — one of Africa's most sophisticated unified technology statutes, covering data protection alongside e-commerce, e-signatures, electronic communications and cybercrime. The personal data book of the Code is heavily GDPR-inspired, with accountability duties, processing registers, DPOs and breach notification.

The supervisory authority is the Autorité de Protection des Données Personnelles (APDP), created by Article 462 of the Code to replace the former CNIL-Bénin, governed by an 11-member board. Controllers must notify the APDP of most processing (small and medium enterprises are exempt unless processing is risky or sensitive), and DPO appointment — mandatory for state bodies and large-scale or monitoring-based processing — exempts an organisation from the notification duty, an unusual incentive design.

Penalties reach XOF 50 million for a first offence and XOF 100 million or 5% of annual sales for repeat offences within five years. The APDP is among the more active West African regulators, publishing communiqués, deliberations and compliance deadlines; foreign providers serving the Beninese market should take notification/DPO obligations and transfer adequacy requirements seriously.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Notify the APDP or appoint a DPO — Most processing must be notified to the APDP before it begins; appointing a data protection officer exempts the controller from notification. State bodies and large-scale/sensitive processors must appoint a DPO.
  • 02
    Maintain processing registers — Controllers must keep registers of processing activities and be able to demonstrate compliance with the Digital Code's principles.
  • 03
    Notify breaches without delay — Security breaches must be reported to the APDP without delay, describing the breach, affected data and individuals, consequences and remedial measures.
  • 04
    Ensure adequate protection for transfers — Transfers require the recipient country to ensure an adequate level of protection; contractual clauses or binding internal rules can substitute for non-adequate destinations.
  • 05
    Respect escalating sanctions — Fines run to XOF 50m for a first offence and XOF 100m or 5% of annual sales for repeat offences within five years.
