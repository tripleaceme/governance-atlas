Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Tuvalu has no data protection or privacy legislation. UNCTAD's assessments state plainly that no legal framework exists for electronic transactions, data protection or online consumer protection in Tuvalu; a cybercrime law is the main piece of digital-era legislation. No supervisory authority exists and no bill could be verified as under parliamentary consideration as of August 2026.

The direction of travel is set by UNCTAD's eTrade readiness assessment (launched December 2019), which proposed a roadmap for a digital economy development agenda including foundational e-commerce laws — of which data protection would be one pillar. Tuvalu participates in regional Pacific digital-policy and cybersecurity discussions, but with a population of around 11,000 and severe capacity constraints, legislative progress has been slow.

Note that some low-quality secondary websites claim Tuvalu has a 'Data Protection and Privacy Act 2020' and a 'Tuvalu Data Protection Authority'; no official or authoritative source corroborates this, and UNCTAD's own reporting contradicts it. Treat such claims as erroneous.
