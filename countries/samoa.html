Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Samoa has no comprehensive data protection or privacy law and no data protection authority. Several AI-generated legal content sites assert that Samoa has a 'Privacy Act 2013' enforced by an 'Office of the Privacy Commissioner'; no official Samoan government or authoritative international source corroborates this, and it should be treated as unverified and likely erroneous. Actual protections are fragmentary: confidentiality obligations on telecommunications providers under the Telecommunications Act 2005, computer-misuse and privacy-adjacent offences in the Crimes Act 2013, and data safeguards embedded in the National Digital Identification Act 2024 governing the new digital ID system.

Policy momentum is building. The Ministry of Communications and Information Technology's National Communications & ICT Policy 2025–2030 and National Cybersecurity Strategy 2025/26–2030/31 bring connectivity, digital ID, responsible data use and cybersecurity under one framework, and the Ministry has stated it is proposing new legislation to address current gaps including data protection, alongside a national data governance framework for the public sector.

Until such legislation materialises, organisations handling Samoan personal data face no registration, breach notification, DPO or transfer requirements; the main legal exposures are telecom confidentiality duties, digital-ID-specific rules, and criminal provisions in the Crimes Act.
