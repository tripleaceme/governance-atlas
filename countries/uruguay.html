Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Uruguay's Law 18.331 of 2008 is one of Latin America's most European-style data protection laws, and it earned Uruguay an EU adequacy decision in August 2012 (Decision 2012/484/EU) — reaffirmed by the European Commission on 15 January 2024. The law covers public and private processing, treats data protection as an inherent human right, and provides the habeas data action alongside administrative enforcement.

The regime has been actively modernized without a wholesale rewrite: Law 19.670 (2018) and its implementing Decree 64/020 (2020) grafted GDPR-style accountability onto the 2008 statute — mandatory data protection officers for public bodies and for private entities that process sensitive data as their core business or handle large volumes, 72-hour breach notification to the URCDP, proactive responsibility (privacy by design, impact assessments), and extended territorial scope.

The URCDP, a technically autonomous unit operating within the e-government agency AGESIC, is a credible and consultative regulator: it maintains database, DPO and breach registers, issues opinions and rulings, and sanctions range from warnings to fines of up to 500,000 indexed units plus database closure. For a small market, Uruguay punches above its weight as a compliant hub for regional data operations.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register databases — Controllers must register their databases with the URCDP through its management system, which also records DPOs and breach notifications.
  • 02
    Obtain prior informed consent — Processing generally requires the data subject's prior, informed consent, with statutory exceptions; sensitive data has heightened requirements.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Security incidents must be reported to the URCDP within 72 hours of awareness and affected individuals informed (Decree 64/020).
  • 04
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public bodies and private entities processing sensitive data as a core business or large data volumes must designate a data protection officer registered with the URCDP.
  • 05
    Build in accountability — Privacy by design/default and data protection impact assessments are required for higher-risk processing under the 2020 accountability decree.
