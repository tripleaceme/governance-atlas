Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Brazil's LGPD (Law 13.709/2018, effective September 2020 with sanctions from August 2021) is Latin America's most consequential data protection law and closely mirrors the GDPR. It applies to any processing carried out in Brazil or aimed at individuals located in Brazil, regardless of where the processor is based, and recognizes ten legal bases for processing — so consent is not always required, unlike older Latin American laws.

The regulator, the ANPD, has matured quickly: it has issued regulations on sanctions dosimetry, small-agent exemptions, breach notification (Resolution 15/2024, setting the 3-business-day deadline) and international transfers (Resolution 19/2024, including Brazilian standard contractual clauses). In September 2025 Provisional Measure 1.317/2025 elevated the body from an 'authority' to a full regulatory agency with greater autonomy, keeping the ANPD acronym.

Enforcement is real: the ANPD has imposed tens of millions of reais in fines since 2023 and has taken high-profile actions against large tech companies over AI training and data scraping. Maximum fines reach 2% of Brazilian revenue capped at R$50 million per violation, and the agency can also order processing bans. Brazil does not yet hold an EU adequacy decision, though alignment is high.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish a legal basis — Every processing operation must map to one of the LGPD's ten legal bases (consent, legitimate interest, contract, legal obligation, etc.) and be documented.
  • 02
    Appoint an encarregado (DPO) — Controllers must appoint and publicly identify a data protection officer; only small-scale processing agents are exempt under ANPD Resolution 2/2022.
  • 03
    Notify breaches in 3 business days — Incidents likely to cause relevant risk or harm must be reported to the ANPD and affected data subjects within 3 business days (Resolution 15/2024).
  • 04
    Honor data subject rights — Respond to access, deletion, portability, correction and information requests within statutory deadlines (simplified format immediately or full response within 15 days).
  • 05
    Use approved transfer mechanisms — International transfers must rely on adequacy, the ANPD's standard contractual clauses, or approved BCRs under Resolution 19/2024.
