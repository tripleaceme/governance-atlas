Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Israel's Protection of Privacy Law (PPL) dates to 1981, but Amendment 13 — passed in August 2024 and effective 14 August 2025 — transformed it into a modern, GDPR-influenced regime. The amendment modernized core definitions (personal information, sensitive ('specially sensitive') data, processing), introduced mandatory Data Protection Officers for public bodies, data brokers and large-scale/sensitive-data processors, drastically narrowed the old database registration duty, and armed the Privacy Protection Authority (PPA) with real teeth: administrative inquiries, cease-and-desist orders, and formula-based monetary penalties that can reach millions of shekels for large or sensitive databases.

The framework is complemented by the Privacy Protection (Information Security) Regulations 2017 — which impose tiered security requirements and require immediate reporting of serious security incidents to the PPA — and the 2001 Transfer Regulations plus dedicated 2023 regulations governing data received from the EEA. Israel has held an EU adequacy decision since 2011, reconfirmed by the European Commission in January 2024, so EU-Israel data flows remain free, though civil society has pressed the Commission to revisit the decision.

Enforcement reality is shifting fast: pre-Amendment 13 the PPA relied mainly on audits and modest sanctions; since August 2025 it operates with substantially expanded penalty and supervision powers, and individuals can claim statutory damages without proving harm. Companies handling Israeli data should confirm DPO applicability, security tiering, and breach playbooks against the new regime.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Appoint a DPO where required — Public bodies, data brokers, and controllers processing large-scale or specially sensitive data must appoint a qualified, conflict-free Data Protection Officer.
  • 02
    Tiered information security — The 2017 Information Security Regulations impose security controls by database risk level, with immediate reporting of serious security incidents to the PPA.
  • 03
    Notification and transparency — When collecting personal data, controllers must inform individuals of the legal duty or voluntariness, purposes, and recipients; Amendment 13 expands these notice duties.
  • 04
    Registration/notification of qualifying databases — Large sensitive databases, public-body databases, and data brokers remain subject to registration or notification with the Registrar of Databases.
  • 05
    Lawful cross-border transfers — Transfers abroad must satisfy the 2001 Transfer Regulations (adequate protection or gateways such as consent/contract), with extra onward-transfer rules for EEA-origin data.
