Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

St. Vincent and the Grenadines has no comprehensive data protection law in force. The country enacted a Privacy Act in 2003, intended to regulate the collection, use, correction and disclosure of personal data held by public authorities, but the commencement order needed to bring it into operation has never been issued, so the Act remains unenforceable on paper more than two decades after passage. Even if commenced, its scope is largely limited to the public sector and it falls well short of a modern, GDPR-style framework.

There is no data protection authority, no registration regime, no breach notification duty and no administrative fining power. Practical privacy protection rests on general constitutional and common-law principles, and on sectoral confidentiality rules (for example in banking and the offshore financial services sector, which is significant to the local economy). Some organisations — notably financial institutions serving international clients — apply GDPR-derived policies voluntarily because of extraterritorial exposure.

For a meeting: treat SVG as a jurisdiction with a legal vacuum rather than a light-touch regime. Companies operating there are effectively governed by contractual commitments and any foreign laws (GDPR, etc.) that reach them, not by local statute. No active bill was identified as progressing through Parliament as of mid-2026.
