Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Honduras has no comprehensive data protection law in force as of mid-2026. A Law for the Protection of Confidential Personal Data (Ley de Protección de Datos Personales) has been under discussion in the National Congress for years — an earlier bill stalled around 2018, and a renewed project has seen Congress approve initial chapters, but the law as a whole remains unenacted. If passed, it would create Honduras's first dedicated data protection authority and a rights framework for data subjects.

Existing protection is fragmentary: the 1982 Constitution recognizes the right to privacy and habeas data (a constitutional action allowing individuals to access and correct data held about them), and the Transparency and Access to Public Information Law (Decree 170-2006) imposes confidentiality duties on public bodies handling personal data. The Institute for Access to Public Information (IAIP) oversees that transparency regime but is not a general data protection regulator.

Practically, there is no registration, breach-notification, or fining regime for private-sector data processing. Companies operating in Honduras should monitor the congressional bill, since chapter-by-chapter approval suggests eventual passage, and should rely on constitutional habeas data and sectoral confidentiality rules in the interim.
