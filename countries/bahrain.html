Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Bahrain was an early Gulf mover on data protection: Law No. 30 of 2018 (the Personal Data Protection Law) was issued in July 2018 and has been in force since 1 August 2019. It is broadly European-inspired, applying to individuals and businesses processing personal data in Bahrain (and to foreign entities processing via means located in Bahrain), with rules on lawful bases, sensitive data, data subject rights, and cross-border transfers based on an approved-country list.

Supervision sits with the Personal Data Protection Authority (PDPA), whose functions were entrusted to the Ministry of Justice, Islamic Affairs and Waqf by Decree No. 78 of 2019 — so in practice a ministry unit acts as regulator. A package of ten ministerial resolutions and orders issued in 2022 operationalized the law, including a 72-hour breach notification rule (Resolution 44/2022) and a register-based 'Data Protection Guardian' regime (Orders 46 and 47 of 2022) — Bahrain's version of the DPO, mandatory for prescribed controllers and empowered to report uncorrected violations directly to the Authority.

Distinctive features include criminal penalties (up to one year imprisonment) for certain violations alongside modest administrative fines, and notification/authorization requirements for certain processing operations. Public enforcement activity has been limited, but the criminal exposure and the 2022 implementing rules make formal compliance worth taking seriously for entities operating in Bahrain.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis for processing — Process personal data only with the data subject's consent or another basis under the law (contract necessity, legal obligation, protection of vital interests, legitimate interests within limits); sensitive data faces stricter rules.
  • 02
    Notify or seek authorization from the Authority — File prior notifications for automated and sensitive processing operations and obtain prior written authorization for specified high-risk operations.
  • 03
    72-hour breach notification — Inform the Personal Data Protection Authority within 72 hours of discovering a personal data breach (Resolution No. 44 of 2022).
  • 04
    Appoint a Data Protection Guardian where required — Prescribed controllers must appoint a registered Data Protection Guardian who monitors compliance and escalates uncorrected violations to the Authority within set timeframes.
  • 05
    Cross-border transfer discipline — Transfer personal data abroad only to jurisdictions on the approved adequacy list, or with the Authority's authorization or a statutory exemption such as consent.
