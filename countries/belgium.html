Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Belgium applies the EU GDPR directly, supplemented by the Law of 30 July 2018 on the protection of natural persons with regard to the processing of personal data, in force since 5 September 2018, which replaced the 1992 Privacy Act. The 2018 law exercises the GDPR's opening clauses — most notably lowering the children's online consent age to 13, regulating processing of sensitive and judicial data, and transposing the Law Enforcement Directive — while a separate Law of 3 December 2017 created the supervisory authority.

That authority, the Autorité de protection des données / Gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit (APD/GBA), succeeded the former Privacy Commission on 25 May 2018 and operates from Brussels with distinct inspection and litigation chambers. It has produced influential EU-level case law, including major decisions on the IAB Europe TCF consent framework used in online advertising, and its decisions are appealable to the Brussels Market Court, which regularly refines them.

Compliance is standard GDPR practice with Belgian specifics: multilingual transparency (Dutch/French/German), the 13-year consent age, camera-surveillance rules interacting with the separate Camera Act, and sectoral committees governing access to government databases. Enforcement is complaint-driven and moderately active, with fines typically in the tens to hundreds of thousands of euros.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR compliance baseline — Apply the GDPR in full: lawful basis, transparency in the region's official languages, data subject rights, and accountability.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the APD/GBA within 72 hours of a personal data breach and affected individuals where risk is high.
  • 03
    Children's consent at 13 — Obtain parental consent for information society services offered to children under 13.
  • 04
    Extra safeguards for sensitive data — Maintain the list of persons authorised to access special-category and judicial data as required by the 2018 Law.
  • 05
    DPIA and DPO where required — Conduct DPIAs for high-risk processing and appoint a DPO where GDPR or Belgian extensions require one.
