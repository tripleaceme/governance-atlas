Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Panama's Law No. 81 of 26 March 2019 on Personal Data Protection is a GDPR-influenced comprehensive law that entered into force on 29 March 2021 and is fleshed out by Executive Decree 285 of 2021. It applies to databases located in Panama or whose controller is established there, sets out processing principles (loyalty, purpose limitation, proportionality, security), and grants data subjects ARCO-style rights of access, rectification, cancellation, opposition, and portability.

The regulator is ANTAI, the National Authority for Transparency and Access to Information, which handles complaints (including through its online portal), investigates violations, and sanctions offenders. Breaches must be reported to ANTAI without undue delay and at the latest within 72 hours, with affected individuals notified where risk is high. Fines are modest by global standards — US$1,000 to US$10,000 for grave infractions — but very grave infractions can result in suspension or permanent closure of the offending database or activity, and ANTAI has publicly imposed data protection sanctions. Minors' data requires verified parental or guardian authorization, and cross-border transfers depend on ANTAI-recognized adequacy or appropriate safeguards.

Compared with GDPR, Panama's regime is lighter-touch: no mandatory DPO, no general registration, and low monetary ceilings, but the 72-hour breach rule and transfer restrictions mean multinationals typically fold Panama into their regional GDPR-style compliance programs. ANTAI has been increasingly active with awareness campaigns and enforcement since 2022.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Process on a valid legal basis with consent as the rule — Obtain the data subject's consent (prior, express and irrefutable for sensitive data such as health) or rely on another basis permitted by Law 81, respecting purpose limitation and proportionality.
  • 02
    Report breaches within 72 hours — Notify ANTAI of security breaches without undue delay and no later than 72 hours after awareness, and inform affected data subjects when the breach poses a high risk to their rights.
  • 03
    Honor ARCO and portability rights — Provide mechanisms for data subjects to exercise access, rectification, cancellation, opposition, and portability within the deadlines set by Decree 285 of 2021.
  • 04
    Verify guardian consent for minors — Process minors' data only with prior authorization from the parent, tutor, or guardian, making reasonable technology-appropriate efforts to verify that authorization.
  • 05
    Restrict international transfers — Transfer personal data abroad only to jurisdictions or organizations providing adequate protection recognized by ANTAI, or subject to appropriate safeguards approved by the authority.
