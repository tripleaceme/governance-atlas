Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Timor-Leste has no comprehensive data protection law and no data protection authority. The main protections are constitutional: Article 38 of the Constitution gives citizens the right to access their personal data held in manual or electronic records, to demand rectification, and to know the purpose of collection, while related articles protect privacy of the home and correspondence. There is no statute operationalizing these rights against private-sector processing.

Movement is finally visible, but through a cybersecurity route rather than a standalone privacy bill. In July 2026 the Government submitted to the National Parliament a request for legislative authorisation to approve a cybersecurity legislative package — covering cybercrime, digital evidence, a national cybersecurity authority, and rules on the protection of personal data and privacy. Civil society has criticized earlier drafts of the cyber law over online-freedom and data protection concerns, and the package had not been enacted as of August 2026.

Practically, organizations operating in Timor-Leste face no registration, breach notification, or transfer restrictions today, but should track the cybersecurity package, since its personal data provisions would create the country's first enforceable data rules.
