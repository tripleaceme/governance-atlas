Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Vanuatu now has a comprehensive data protection law in force — a status change from 'bill in progress'. The Data Protection and Privacy Act No. 13 of 2024 received assent on 5 December 2024 and commenced on 2 January 2025. It applies to public and private sectors, to processing within Vanuatu or of data generated/collected in Vanuatu wherever processed, and extraterritorially to foreign controllers offering goods or services to, or monitoring, people in Vanuatu. It follows GDPR architecture: processing principles (fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, security), lawful bases including consent and legitimate interests, prohibitions on special-category and children's data with narrow exceptions, and data subject rights to access (free, within one month), restriction, rectification/erasure, objection, and freedom from solely automated decisions.

Two features stand out. First, the cross-border regime is unusually strict on paper: personal data generated or collected in Vanuatu must not be used elsewhere without prior authorisation from the Minister on the recommendation of the Deputy Commissioner of Data Protection and Privacy, unless the destination is on a Ministerial whitelist of adequate countries — a government-permission model rather than the GDPR's self-assessed safeguards. Second, enforcement is criminal rather than administrative: non-compliance with any provision is an offence punishable by fines up to VT 10 million (~USD 80,000), with no GDPR-style turnover-based administrative penalties.

Enforcement reality lags the text: the Deputy Commissioner sits within the Digital Safety Authority created by a companion Act, and that authority was not yet operational as of the latest available reporting; key operational details — breach notification duties and deadlines, processor obligations, records of processing — are delegated to Regulations that had not been issued. Businesses touching Vanuatu data should implement the principles and transfer controls now, and watch for the Regulations and the authority standing up.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Process per the statutory principles — Controllers and processors must process personal data lawfully, fairly and transparently, for explicit specified purposes, keeping data adequate, proportional, accurate and secure, and must delete data once the purpose is achieved (s 4).
  • 02
    Establish a lawful purpose — Processing is permitted only on a s 5 basis: consent (demonstrable, freely given, withdrawable), contract, protection of rights/legitimate interests, legal obligation, public interest, or legitimate interests not overridden by the data subject's rights — with special care where the data subject is a child.
  • 03
    Special categories and children's data prohibited by default — Special-category data (health, beliefs, biometrics, genetics, offences) and children's data may only be processed under narrow exceptions — for children, mainly parental/guardian consent or the child's legitimate interests, with age-verification mechanisms and plain-language notices (ss 6–7).
  • 04
    Honour data subject rights — Provide access, copies and processing information in writing, in plain language, free of charge and within one month; support restriction, rectification, erasure, objection, and the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions (ss 9–14).
  • 05
    Control cross-border transfers — Do not use Vanuatu-sourced personal data abroad without prior Ministerial authorisation or a whitelisted destination; specific-situation transfers require explicit informed consent or other s 17 grounds.
