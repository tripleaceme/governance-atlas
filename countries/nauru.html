Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Nauru has no comprehensive data protection or privacy law, no supervisory authority, and no draft bill that could be verified as of August 2026. The Constitution's Part II (Protection of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms, Articles 3–15) provides general fundamental-rights protection that implicitly supports privacy, but there is no dedicated statutory privacy right or personal-data regime.

Partial, sector-specific protections exist. The Cybercrime Act 2015 criminalises unauthorised access to computers, illegal interception of communications and data interference — addressing data protection only from a confidentiality angle. The Communications and Broadcasting Act 2018 adds safeguards for subscriber information held by telecom providers. The government has signalled interest in modernising cyber and electronic-evidence legislation, which could eventually address data protection more directly, but nothing concrete has been published.

In practice, organisations handling Nauruan personal data face no local registration, breach notification, transfer or DPO requirements; the main exposure is criminal liability for unauthorised access or interception under the Cybercrime Act.
