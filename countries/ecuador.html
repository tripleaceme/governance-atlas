Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Ecuador's Ley Orgánica de Protección de Datos Personales (LOPDP), enacted in May 2021 after the massive Novaestrat breach, is a comprehensive GDPR-modelled statute covering public and private processing with extraterritorial reach. Its sanctioning regime became applicable on 26 May 2023 following a two-year transition, and the framework has since been completed by a General Regulation (2023) and a wave of SPDP technical norms and resolutions through 2024-2025, including a risk-management and impact-assessment guide, DPO training rules, an annual audit plan, a fine-calculation methodology (SPDP-SPD-2025-0022-R), and a General Norm on national and international data transfers.

The supervisory authority, the Superintendencia de Protección de Datos Personales (SPDP), is operational and increasingly assertive — publishing sanction resolutions, requiring registration of DPO appointments, and standing up a National Data Protection Registry. Key obligations: a lawful basis (consent or alternatives) for processing; data subject rights including access, rectification, erasure and portability; DPO designation for public bodies and for large-scale or sensitive processing; breach notification to the SPDP within five days; impact assessments for high-risk processing; and transfer restrictions requiring adequacy, safeguards such as contractual clauses or BCRs, or consent. Fines for private entities run from 0.1%-0.7% of prior-year turnover for minor infringements to 0.7%-1% for serious ones, alongside corrective measures up to processing bans.

Enforcement reality: 2023-2025 were largely about norm-building and first resolutions; from 2026 the SPDP signalled a shift to active audit sweeps and effective sanctions, making Ecuador one of the more consequential compliance jurisdictions in the Andean region. GDPR-compliant organisations will find the structure familiar but must localize: DPO registration with the SPDP, the 5-day breach clock, and the new transfer norm's contract requirements.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and consent — Processing requires consent or another legal basis under the LOPDP, with heightened conditions for sensitive data and purpose limitation throughout.
  • 02
    Notify breaches within 5 days — Security incidents affecting personal data must be reported to the SPDP within five days of knowledge, and to affected data subjects where risk warrants.
  • 03
    Designate and register a DPO — Public bodies and organisations engaged in large-scale or sensitive processing must appoint a data protection delegate and register the appointment with the SPDP.
  • 04
    Honour expanded data subject rights — Controllers must operationalise access, rectification, erasure, opposition, portability and rights around automated decisions within statutory deadlines.
  • 05
    Control international transfers — Transfers abroad require an adequate destination, sufficient safeguards (standard clauses, BCRs) or the data subject's express consent, per the SPDP's General Transfer Norm.
