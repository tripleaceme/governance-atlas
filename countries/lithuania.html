Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Lithuania applies the EU GDPR directly, supplemented by the amended Law on Legal Protection of Personal Data (original law No I-1374 of 1996, comprehensively amended by Law No XIII-1426 of 30 June 2018, in force 16 July 2018). The national law lowers the children's online consent age to 14, sets rules for processing personal identification codes, employee data and video surveillance, caps fines on public authorities, and splits supervision between two bodies.

The main regulator is the State Data Protection Inspectorate (VDAI) in Vilnius, which handles complaints, breach notifications, inspections and fines; the Inspector of Journalist Ethics supervises processing for journalistic purposes. The VDAI issued one of the earlier notable GDPR fines (against payment company MisterTango in 2019) and remains active in inspections and guidance, though fine levels are generally moderate.

Compliance is standard GDPR practice with Lithuanian specifics: Lithuanian-language transparency, the 14-year consent age, restrictions on using the personal identification code as a public identifier, and prior-notice rules for workplace monitoring. Cross-border work runs through the EDPB one-stop-shop with the VDAI as national contact.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR compliance baseline — Apply the GDPR in full: lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, and accountability documentation.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the VDAI within 72 hours of a personal data breach and affected individuals where risk is high.
  • 03
    Children's consent at 14 — Obtain parental consent for information society services offered to children under 14.
  • 04
    Protect personal identification codes — Process the national personal code only under statutory conditions; it may not be made public.
  • 05
    DPIA and DPO where required — Conduct impact assessments against the VDAI's high-risk list and appoint a DPO where Art. 37 GDPR applies.
