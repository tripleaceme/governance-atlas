Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Burkina Faso replaced its 2004 data protection law with Law No. 001-2021/AN of 30 March 2021, a substantially more modern and more coercive statute. The reform expanded definitions, created new data-subject rights, tightened rules on health data and international transfers, institutionalised data protection officers in public and private organisations, and significantly increased penalties — with administrative fines now calculated at 1% to 7% of the offender's turnover.

The supervisory authority is the Commission de l'Informatique et des Libertés (CIL), an independent administrative authority operational since 2007 and strengthened by the 2021 law, including provisions on cooperation with other data protection authorities. Controllers must file prior declarations with the CIL before processing, and obstruction of the CIL is itself punishable with fines of FCFA 5–10 million.

Enforcement reality: the CIL is genuinely active — running declaration campaigns, training DPOs and publicising the law — but operates in a difficult security and political context following the 2022 coups. Businesses operating in Burkina Faso should treat the declaration duty and turnover-based fine exposure as real, while expecting guidance to continue developing.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Declare processing to the CIL — Controllers must file a prior declaration with the CIL before carrying out personal data processing; certain sensitive operations require heightened formalities.
  • 02
    Respect data-subject rights — The 2021 law expands rights of information, access, rectification, objection and erasure, and strengthens protection of health data.
  • 03
    Appoint a data protection officer — The law institutionalises DPOs in public and private organisations to steer compliance and liaise with the CIL.
  • 04
    Control cross-border transfers — Transfers of personal data abroad are subject to reinforced oversight and must ensure adequate protection.
  • 05
    Avoid obstruction and violations — Sanctions are turnover-based (1%–7%) and obstruction of CIL action attracts fines of FCFA 5–10 million, making cooperation with the regulator essential.
