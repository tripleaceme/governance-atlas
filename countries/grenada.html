Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Grenada passed a comprehensive Data Protection Act (No. 1 of 2023), assented on 9 May 2023 and published in the Official Gazette on 10 May 2023 — but the Act is not in force. It commences only on a date appointed by ministerial Order in the Gazette (different dates may be set for different provisions), and no commencement order had been identified as of mid-2026. The recorded status of 'no law' therefore understates progress: Grenada has a fully enacted modern statute sitting dormant, awaiting political action that could come at any time.

The Act establishes seven data protection principles, lawful bases for processing personal and sensitive data, data subject rights including access and rectification, and an Information Commission empowered to investigate complaints, issue enforcement notices and impose penalties. Sanctions are criminal-style: individuals face up to EC$50,000/3 years on summary conviction or EC$100,000/5 years on indictment, while bodies corporate face up to EC$250,000 (summary) or EC$500,000 (indictable), with accountability extending to senior corporate officers. The bill attracted some local controversy during passage over its scope and press-freedom implications.

Local commentary during 2025 (e.g. warnings to financial institutions to prepare for banking data protection demands) suggests commencement is anticipated. Businesses in Grenada should treat this as a compliance runway: map data flows and governance now so that commencement — which requires only a minister's signature — does not catch them unprepared.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Data protection principles (pending) — Once commenced, controllers must process personal data in line with seven principles covering fairness, purpose limitation, data integrity and disclosure limits.
  • 02
    Sensitive data restrictions (pending) — The Act imposes heightened conditions and restrictions on processing sensitive categories of personal data.
  • 03
    Honour data subject rights (pending) — Individuals will have rights of access and rectification, backed by complaint procedures before the Information Commission.
  • 04
    Corporate accountability (pending) — Senior officers of companies can be held personally liable for corporate non-compliance, with fines up to EC$500,000 for bodies corporate.
