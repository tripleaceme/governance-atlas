Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

China's Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), in force since 1 November 2021, is a GDPR-inspired but distinctly Chinese regime that applies to processing of personal information inside China and extraterritorially to handlers targeting or analyzing individuals in China. It sits atop a broader stack — the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, and the Network Data Security Management Regulation effective 1 January 2025 — and is enforced principally by the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) alongside MIIT, public security and market regulators. Consent (often 'separate consent' for sensitive data, transfers and disclosures) dominates the legal bases, and data of minors under 14 is treated as sensitive.

Its most distinctive feature is the cross-border transfer regime: exports of personal information must go through a CAC security assessment, a filed Chinese standard contract, or third-party certification — the last route completed by certification measures effective 1 January 2026 — with exemptions for low-volume, non-sensitive transfers (up to 100,000 individuals a year). Handlers above thresholds must appoint a personal information protection officer, conduct personal information protection impact assessments for high-risk processing, and foreign handlers caught extraterritorially must designate a local representative.

Enforcement is real and escalating: fines reach RMB 50 million or 5% of the prior year's turnover (the DiDi case exceeded RMB 8 billion), and a joint four-agency national enforcement campaign launched in March 2025 has specifically targeted cross-border transfer compliance, with published penalty cases through 2025-2026. Companies operating in or selling into China should treat PIPL compliance — especially transfer mechanisms and consent hygiene — as a board-level issue.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and separate consent — Establish a legal basis for each processing activity; obtain separate consent for sensitive personal information, cross-border transfers, public disclosure and provision to third parties.
  • 02
    Cross-border transfer mechanism — Before exporting personal information, complete a CAC security assessment, file China standard contractual clauses, or obtain certification — unless an exemption threshold applies.
  • 03
    Impact assessments (PIPIA) — Conduct and retain personal information protection impact assessments for sensitive data, automated decision-making, transfers and other high-risk processing.
  • 04
    Appoint PIPO / local representative — Designate a personal information protection officer above CAC-set volume thresholds; overseas handlers must establish a dedicated entity or representative in China.
  • 05
    Incident response and notification — On a breach, take immediate remedial measures and notify the regulator and affected individuals as required, per PIPL and the 2025 Network Data Security Regulation.
