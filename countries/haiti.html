Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Haiti has no comprehensive data protection law and no data protection authority. Privacy protection rests on general sources: the 1987 Constitution's protections for private life and correspondence, civil-law personality rights, and scattered sectoral provisions. Commentators on Haitian digital law describe a significant regulatory vacuum, with services like the national identification card and digitized exam results collecting personal data without a dedicated statutory framework, fueling identity-theft concerns.

Legislative attempts have stalled. A draft law instituting the single national identification card and addressing personal data protection was published by the government (circa 2017) but never enacted, and a draft personal data protection law text has circulated via the civil-service reform office (OMRH) without advancing. Industry association AHTIC has publicly called for data protection legislation. DLA Piper's country guide (updated January 2025) confirms there is still no dedicated data protection entity and references only a ministerial arrêté touching on personal data rules rather than any comprehensive law.

Given the ongoing political and security crisis, near-term enactment is unlikely. Organizations handling Haitian personal data should apply contractual safeguards and best practices voluntarily, as there is no local registration, breach notification, or transfer regime to comply with.
