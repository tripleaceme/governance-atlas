Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Post-Brexit, the UK runs a retained-EU-law regime: the UK GDPR (the EU GDPR as domesticated) together with the Data Protection Act 2018 and PECR. This framework is now being amended — not replaced — by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (DUAA), which received Royal Assent on 19 June 2025 and is being commenced in phases through June 2026, with a major tranche of data protection provisions in force from 5 February 2026. DUAA introduces 'recognised legitimate interests', relaxed rules on some cookies and on automated decision-making, a new statutory complaints-handling duty on controllers (from June 2026), and restructures the ICO into an 'Information Commission'.

The regime applies to any organisation established in the UK or targeting people there. The ICO is a globally prominent regulator that supervises via audits, enforcement notices and fines of up to £17.5 million or 4% of worldwide turnover; most controllers must pay an annual data protection fee, which funds the office. The digital age of consent is 13, reinforced by the ICO's influential Age Appropriate Design (Children's) Code.

Crucially for international business, the European Commission renewed the UK's two adequacy decisions on 19 December 2025 — after concluding the DUAA keeps UK protection 'essentially equivalent' to EU standards — so personal data continues to flow freely between the EU/EEA and the UK until 27 December 2031.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Comply with UK GDPR obligations — Lawful basis (including new recognised legitimate interests under DUAA), transparency, data subject rights, records, DPIAs and accountability apply to processing of UK residents' data.
  • 02
    Pay the ICO data protection fee — Most controllers must register for and pay the annual data protection fee to the ICO unless exempt; non-payment is enforceable by penalty.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches posing a risk to individuals must be reported to the ICO within 72 hours.
  • 04
    Implement a complaints process by June 2026 — Under the DUAA, controllers must have a statutory data protection complaints-handling process (acknowledge within 30 days and respond without undue delay) from 19 June 2026.
  • 05
    Protect children online — Parental consent is required under 13 where consent is the basis, and services likely accessed by children must conform to the ICO Children's Code (age-appropriate design).
