Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Tunisia was the first Arab country to adopt comprehensive data protection legislation: Organic Act No. 2004-63 of 27 July 2004, which establishes general principles of lawful processing, data subject rights, and the independent supervisory authority, the INPDP. The regime rests on prior formalities — declarations to the INPDP before processing, with authorizations needed for certain categories — and treats children's data with unusual care, involving guardian consent and judicial oversight.

The 2004 Act is now widely regarded as outdated, and a full replacement is in progress. A GDPR-inspired organic bill of 123 articles — covering processing principles, data subject rights (including portability and erasure), a strengthened supervisory authority, high-risk processing rules relevant to AI, and deterrent sanctions — was under examination by the Rights and Freedoms Committee of the Assembly of People's Representatives as of early 2026, but had not been reported as finally adopted.

In practice, enforcement under the 2004 Act relies on criminal-style penalties rather than large administrative fines, and the INPDP has historically been more visible in advocacy and opinions than in heavy sanctions. Businesses meeting professionals should assume the 2004 declaration regime applies today while planning for a significantly stricter GDPR-style law once the pending bill passes.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Declare processing to the INPDP — Submit a prior declaration before processing personal data; certain processing (e.g. sensitive and health data) requires INPDP authorization.
  • 02
    Obtain authorization for international transfers — Transfers of personal data abroad require INPDP approval and are restricted where the destination does not ensure adequate protection.
  • 03
    Protect minors' data with heightened safeguards — Processing data of minors requires guardian consent with judicial oversight — a distinctive feature of the Tunisian regime.
  • 04
    Respect consent and purpose limitation — Process data fairly for declared purposes with the data subject's consent unless a statutory exception applies.
  • 05
    Ensure confidentiality and security — Implement measures protecting personal data against unauthorized access, alteration or disclosure.
