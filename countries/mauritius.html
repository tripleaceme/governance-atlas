Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Mauritius's Data Protection Act 2017 (Act 20 of 2017), in force since early 2018, replaced the 2004 Act specifically to align with the GDPR and modernise one of Africa's oldest data protection regimes. It applies to controllers and processors established in Mauritius or using equipment there, covers the full set of GDPR-style principles and data subject rights, requires 72-hour breach notification to the Data Protection Commissioner, and sets a parental-consent age of 16 for children's data. Mauritius was the first African state to sign and ratify Council of Europe Convention 108 and its modernised 108+ protocol, underlining its position as a regional privacy leader.

Two features stand out for foreign businesses. First, registration is genuinely mandatory: every controller and processor must register with the Data Protection Office and renew its certificate every three years, and processing without registration is a criminal offence. Second, every controller and processor must designate a data protection officer. Cross-border transfers require Commissioner-approved safeguards, explicit consent or another statutory ground.

Enforcement is real but criminal-law flavoured: the Data Protection Office, an established and active regulator, investigates complaints and issues enforcement notices, with offences punishable by fines up to MUR 200,000 and imprisonment up to five years rather than turnover-based administrative fines. Mauritius does not hold an EU adequacy decision, though its Convention 108+ status and GDPR alignment ease practical transfer arrangements.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Register with the Data Protection Office — Controllers and processors must register with the Commissioner and renew the three-year certificate; unregistered processing is an offence carrying up to MUR 200,000 in fines and 5 years' imprisonment.
  • 02
    Designate a data protection officer — All controllers and processors must appoint a DPO with professional expertise; one DPO may serve a corporate group absent conflicts of interest.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches must be notified to the Commissioner without undue delay and, where feasible, within 72 hours, with reasons required for any delay; high-risk breaches must also be communicated to data subjects.
  • 04
    Use lawful transfer mechanisms — Transfers abroad require Commissioner-approved safeguards, explicit informed consent, contractual necessity or another ground under section 36.
  • 05
    Obtain parental consent for under-16s — Processing personal data of a child below 16 requires consent from a parent or guardian.
