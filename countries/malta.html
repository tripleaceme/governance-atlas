Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Malta is an EU member state where the GDPR applies directly, supplemented by the Data Protection Act (Act XX of 2018, Chapter 586 of the Laws of Malta), in force since 28 May 2018, which repealed the previous Chapter 440 regime. Malta chose the lowest permitted child consent age — 13 — for information society services, and subsidiary legislation under Cap. 586 covers ePrivacy in electronic communications (S.L. 586.01), the Law Enforcement Directive (S.L. 586.08) and sector-specific processing rules.

The supervisory authority is the Information and Data Protection Commissioner (IDPC), appointed under Article 11 of the Act, which enforces both the GDPR and the national Act and also administers freedom-of-information functions. The national Act moderates fines against public authorities and bodies with reduced statutory caps, while private-sector exposure follows the full GDPR maxima of €20 million or 4% of worldwide turnover.

Given Malta's outsized iGaming, financial services and remote-services sectors, the IDPC's caseload skews toward complaints about online operators, employee monitoring and direct marketing. Compliance in Malta is standard GDPR compliance, with attention to the age-13 consent threshold and the local subsidiary legislation.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR core compliance — Apply GDPR principles, lawful bases, data subject rights and accountability duties, which apply directly in Malta.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the IDPC within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable breach; notify individuals when risk is high.
  • 03
    Child consent verification — Obtain and verify parental consent for information society services offered to children under 13.
  • 04
    DPO appointment — Appoint a DPO where GDPR Article 37 triggers apply.
  • 05
    Sectoral subsidiary legislation — Comply with Cap. 586 subsidiary legislation, including the ePrivacy rules for electronic communications (S.L. 586.01).
