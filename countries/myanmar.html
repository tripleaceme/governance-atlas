Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Myanmar has no comprehensive data protection law and no data protection authority. Privacy-related provisions are scattered across sectoral statutes: the Law Protecting the Privacy and Security of Citizens (2017), the Electronic Transactions Law (2004, amended 2021 to add limited personal-data provisions and offenses), the Telecommunications Law (2013), the Financial Institutions Law (2016), and the Penal Code. None of these creates a general regime of data subject rights, controller obligations, or independent supervision.

The environment has deteriorated since the February 2021 military coup. Key protective provisions of the 2017 privacy law (including warrant requirements for surveillance and searches) were suspended by the State Administration Council, and the junta enacted a sweeping Cybersecurity Law (Law No. 1/2025) on 1 January 2025, effective 30 July 2025. That law regulates digital platform service providers (registration required at 100,000+ users), restricts VPN use, and extends jurisdiction extraterritorially — but it is oriented toward state control and surveillance rather than personal data protection.

For businesses, the practical picture is a legal vacuum on privacy paired with expanding state access to data. Any data handling connected to Myanmar carries political and human-rights risk considerations that outweigh conventional compliance analysis, and there is no realistic prospect of a GDPR-style law under the current administration.
