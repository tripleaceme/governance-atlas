Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Fiji has no comprehensive data protection statute, but it is the Pacific state with the strongest constitutional foundation for one: section 24 of the 2013 Constitution guarantees the right to personal privacy, including confidentiality of personal information and communications. In November 2025, Prime Minister Rabuka announced Cabinet's endorsement of a National Privacy and Personal Data Protection Policy, which explicitly lays the foundation for developing national privacy and personal data protection legislation. No bill had been published or tabled in Parliament as of August 2026, so Fiji sits at the policy-endorsed, pre-bill stage.

Meaningful sectoral protections fill some gaps. The Cybercrime Act 2021 covers offences against the confidentiality, integrity and availability of computer data and systems. The Reserve Bank of Fiji's Protection of Consumer Data & Privacy Guideline (February 2024) imposes data-handling standards on licensed financial institutions. Confidentiality duties also arise under the Banking Act 1995, the Fiji Revenue and Customs Service Act 1998, health practitioner and legal profession rules. The National Digital ID program approved in 2024 is also driving codification of data rules around identity systems.

For organisations operating in Fiji today there is no registration, breach notification or cross-border regime and no data protection authority — but constitutional privacy litigation is possible, financial institutions face RBF supervision, and a full statute is now a stated government commitment worth tracking.
