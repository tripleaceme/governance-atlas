Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Mauritania enacted Law No. 2017-020 on the Protection of Personal Data on 22 July 2017, a comprehensive statute in the Francophone declaration/authorization tradition establishing lawful-processing principles, data subject rights (access, rectification, erasure) and a supervisory authority. Implementation lagged badly: the decree organizing the Autorité de Protection des Données à Caractère Personnel (APD) was only adopted in February 2022, and its members were sworn in later that year.

The APD is now functioning: it published a 2023–2026 strategic plan framed around bringing Law 2017-020 into effective application, signed a cooperation agreement with Algeria's ANPDP in October 2024, and joined the Global Privacy Assembly in November 2024. However, its enforcement track record is thin, and some secondary sources still describe the law as not yet fully effective in practice — the gap between the statute and day-to-day enforcement is the defining feature of the Mauritanian regime.

Companies operating in Mauritania should expect prior-formalities obligations with the APD and honor core data subject rights, but should verify current APD practice directly, as guidance, penalty practice, and detailed rules (breach notification, transfers, DPO expectations) are not yet well documented publicly.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Complete prior formalities with the APD — Declare processing operations to the Autorité de Protection des Données before processing, with stricter formalities for sensitive categories.
  • 02
    Respect data subject rights — Provide access to personal data held, rectify inaccurate or incomplete data, and erase data that is no longer necessary.
  • 03
    Process data lawfully and securely — Follow the law's lawfulness, purpose and security principles when collecting and managing personal data in Mauritania.
