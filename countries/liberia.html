Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Liberia's status has moved from 'no law, no bill' to an active legislative process. The country still has no specific data protection law, but a draft Personal Data Protection and Privacy Act — 'An Act for the Collection, Processing, Transmission, Storage, Protection, and Use of Personal Information in Liberia' — was developed by the Ministry of Posts and Telecommunications with Internews support under the EU-funded Liberia Media Empowerment Project.

Over 50 stakeholders from government, media, civil society, education and the diplomatic corps validated the draft in Monrovia in December 2024, and after Law Reform Commission review, President Joseph Boakai submitted the bill to the Legislature, where the House of Representatives took up review during 2025. As of August 2026 there is no confirmation of enactment, so the bill remains in progress.

In the interim, protections are fragmentary: constitutional privacy guarantees, plus sectoral instruments (telecommunications and cybercrime-related provisions). A 2025 UN review highlighted the gaps in Liberia's online rights framework, adding international pressure to complete the legislation. Organisations handling Liberian data should track the bill, which would create the country's first structured framework and supervisory arrangements for personal data.
