Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Georgia's Law on Personal Data Protection (No. 3144, adopted 14 June 2023) replaced the 2011 law, with its main provisions in force since 1 March 2024. It is closely modelled on the GDPR and was a deliverable of Georgia's EU association agenda. The law applies to processing in Georgia and introduces GDPR-familiar machinery: legal bases, data subject rights, records of processing, mandatory DPOs for public institutions and organisations doing large-scale or sensitive-data processing, direct-marketing rules, and video surveillance provisions.

Breaches likely to cause significant harm must be reported to the supervisory authority within 72 hours. Cross-border transfers follow an adequacy-based model, with safeguards or authority permission required for other destinations. Administrative fines are modest by European standards — GEL 1,000 to 10,000 depending on the offence, adjusted for the violator's turnover and aggravating or mitigating circumstances — though reputational and inspection risk is real, as the regulator has been an active inspector since 2024.

The big 2026 development is institutional: under a December 2025 amendment, the independent Personal Data Protection Service (PDPS) was abolished on 2 March 2026 and its supervisory functions transferred to the State Audit Office, as part of a broader restructuring that also dissolved the Anti-Corruption Bureau. The government frames the SAO as a constitutionally independent home for the function; critics see a weakening of dedicated data protection oversight. Organisations should watch how SAO enforcement practice develops and update any references to the PDPS in their documentation.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and GDPR-style principles — Process personal data only on an enumerated legal basis, with purpose limitation, minimisation and demonstrable accountability.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Report incidents likely to cause significant harm to the supervisory authority within 72 hours, including details of the incident, affected data and mitigation steps.
  • 03
    DPO appointment — Appoint a Data Protection Officer if you are a public institution or conduct large-scale or special-category processing.
  • 04
    Direct marketing and CCTV rules — Follow the specific opt-out and signage regimes for direct marketing and video/audio monitoring, which are enforcement priorities.
  • 05
    Transfer safeguards — Transfer data abroad only to countries with adequate protection or under appropriate safeguards or authority permission.
