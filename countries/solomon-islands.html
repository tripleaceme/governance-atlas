Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Solomon Islands has no data protection law in force, but drafting is genuinely underway. UNCTAD is supporting the Ministry of Communication and Aviation to draft national Data Protection and Privacy legislation under the Pacific E-commerce Initiative, in a project listed as active from July 2025 with no fixed end date. This builds on a national Data Protection and Privacy policy developed with UNCDF support and involves stakeholder consultations aimed at aligning with international standards while fitting local needs. Reported design features of the draft framework include an Office of the Privacy Commissioner and 72-hour breach notification, though no bill text has been published for verification.

In the meantime, protections are thin: there is no national framework for digital identity or data privacy, and the main adjacent instruments are the National Cybersecurity Policy launched in August 2024 and general confidentiality rules in sectoral legislation. There is no registration, breach notification or transfer regime, and no regulator to deal with.

For professionals, the practical takeaway is that the Solomon Islands is at drafting/consultation stage — earlier than a bill before Parliament — so any compliance obligations are at least one legislative cycle away. The 'consultation' label previously recorded remains accurate.
