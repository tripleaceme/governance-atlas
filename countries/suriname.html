Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Suriname has no data protection law in force, but — unlike a pure 'nothing' jurisdiction — it has a draft bill formally before parliament, so its recorded status of 'none' undersells the position slightly. The Wet Bescherming Privacy en Persoonsgegevens (Privacy and Personal Data Protection Bill, circulated around 2020-2021) has been under consideration in De Nationale Assemblée, where it has been handled by the Commission of Rapporteurs, with stakeholder consultation rounds (including the ICT Association of Suriname) feeding written comments into the process. The bill would establish an independent data protection authority, data subject rights, and controller/processor obligations broadly in the modern mould.

Progress has been very slow: the bill has sat in the legislative process for years with no enacted outcome and no clear timeline as of 2025-2026. In the meantime, protection rests on general constitutional privacy principles and scattered sectoral confidentiality rules; there is no regulator, no registration, no breach duty and no fines regime. (Note that the Caribbean Netherlands 'BES' islands' privacy rules sometimes surfaced in searches are Dutch law and irrelevant to Suriname.)

For a meeting: treat Suriname as an unregulated jurisdiction with a long-stalled draft. Businesses handling Surinamese personal data are effectively bound only by contract and any extraterritorial foreign law, but should track the DNA's agenda since enactment would create a new authority and compliance regime from scratch.
