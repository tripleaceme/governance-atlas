Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Armenia's Law on Protection of Personal Data (HO-49-N), adopted 18 May 2015, is a comprehensive, Convention 108-inspired framework covering both public and private sector processing. It rests on familiar principles — lawfulness, purpose limitation, proportionality and consent as the default legal basis — and grants data subjects rights of access, rectification and erasure. Supervision sits with the Personal Data Protection Agency (PDPA), a unit of the Ministry of Justice rather than a fully independent regulator, which maintains a register of processors and must be notified before biometric or special-category data are processed.

The regime is lighter than the GDPR: there is no data protection officer requirement, registration is largely voluntary, and there is no general obligation to report breaches to the Agency (though data subjects must be informed of violations within three working days). Cross-border transfers are free to countries deemed adequate; transfers elsewhere need the Agency's prior written permission based on contractual safeguards it approves.

Enforcement has historically been modest, with administrative fines under the Code of Administrative Offences that are small by international standards. The PDPA became noticeably more active from 2024, launching a public-facing website with guidance, templates and training. Armenia has been discussing modernization — including a draft Cybersecurity Law pending in 2025 and moves toward closer GDPR alignment — so material change is plausible in the near term.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Lawful basis and consent — Process personal data only with the data subject's consent or another basis provided by law, for declared and legitimate purposes, and no longer than necessary.
  • 02
    Notify the Agency for sensitive processing — Notify the PDPA before processing biometric or special-category personal data; the Agency enters processors into its register within 30 days of notification.
  • 03
    Cross-border transfer authorization — Transfers to countries without adequate protection require the PDPA's prior written permission based on contractual safeguards.
  • 04
    Inform data subjects of violations — Eliminate violations and inform affected data subjects within three working days; publicize data leaks and notify the police and the Agency.
  • 05
    Data security — Apply organizational and technical measures to protect personal data against unlawful access, alteration and disclosure.
