Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Guinea regulates personal data through Law No. L/2016/037/AN of 28 July 2016, a hybrid statute whose first part deals with cybercrime and whose second part sets out a personal data protection regime. The data protection portion follows the francophone West African model inspired by the ECOWAS Supplementary Act: processing rests on express prior consent or another legitimate basis, data must be collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes, and most processing is subject to a prior declaration filed with the supervisory authority before it can begin.

The law's biggest practical weakness is institutional. It provides for the creation of a national data protection authority, but a decade after enactment that authority has still not been effectively established, so the declaration regime, complaint handling and sanctions exist largely on paper. Businesses operating in Guinea should treat the substantive principles (consent, purpose limitation, security) as binding law, while recognising that active administrative supervision and enforcement are minimal in practice.

There is no EU adequacy decision, no operational registration portal, and little published enforcement history. Companies typically benchmark against the text of the 2016 law plus the ECOWAS framework, and monitor for the eventual establishment of the authority and implementing decrees.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Prior declaration of processing — Most personal data processing must be declared to the competent authority in advance, and may only be carried out after a receipt is issued.
  • 02
    Consent and lawful basis — Processing is legitimate where the data subject has given express prior consent, subject to statutory exceptions.
  • 03
    Purpose limitation — Personal data must be collected for specific, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed incompatibly with those purposes.
  • 04
    Security of processing — Controllers must take precautions to preserve the security and confidentiality of personal data, including sound and image data covered by the law's broad definition.
