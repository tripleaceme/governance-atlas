Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Ireland is governed directly by the GDPR, given further effect by the Data Protection Act 2018, in force since 25 May 2018. The Act establishes the Data Protection Commission (DPC), sets the digital age of consent at 16 — the highest level the GDPR permits — and adds child-specific protections: a specific right to erasure for data collected from children online, and criminal offences for companies that process children's data for direct marketing, profiling or micro-targeting.

Ireland's outsized importance comes from the GDPR's one-stop-shop: because Meta, Google, Apple, TikTok, Microsoft/LinkedIn and many other multinationals have their EU headquarters in Dublin, the DPC is lead supervisory authority for much of Big Tech's European processing. Its decisions — often shaped by EDPB dispute-resolution — include some of the largest fines in GDPR history, most famously the €1.2 billion Meta transfer decision in 2023, giving the DPC an enforcement docket of global significance despite long-standing criticism from privacy advocates about pace.

For ordinary organisations, compliance in Ireland is standard GDPR compliance — lawful basis, transparency, 72-hour breach notification, DPIAs and Chapter V transfer safeguards — with particular care around children's data given the 16-year consent threshold and the DPC's Children's Fundamentals guidance.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Comply with full GDPR obligations — Lawful basis, transparency, data subject rights, records of processing, DPIAs and accountability apply directly under the GDPR as supplemented by the Data Protection Act 2018.
  • 02
    Obtain parental consent under 16 — Online services relying on consent must verify parental authorisation for users under 16, making reasonable efforts given available technology.
  • 03
    Notify breaches within 72 hours — Personal data breaches posing a risk to individuals must be reported to the DPC within 72 hours.
  • 04
    Do not profile or market to children unlawfully — Processing a child's personal data for direct marketing, profiling or micro-targeting is a criminal offence for companies under the 2018 Act.
  • 05
    Appoint a DPO where required — Mandatory for public authorities and organisations with large-scale monitoring or special-category processing as core activities.
