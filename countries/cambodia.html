Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Cambodia still has no comprehensive data protection law, but its first one is well advanced: the Ministry of Post and Telecommunications (MPTC) finalized a draft Law on Personal Data Protection (LPDP) on 23 June 2025 and released it publicly in July 2025 after several consultation workshops. The draft establishes principles for processing personal data, data subject rights, and institutional oversight, and designates the MPTC itself as the regulator with powers to regulate, audit, monitor and enforce.

As of early-to-mid 2026 the law had not yet been enacted; promulgation was expected around late 2025 or during 2026, followed by a roughly two-year implementation period before the obligations bite. Multinationals should expect a compliance runway but plan for GDPR-familiar concepts — consent, data subject rights, breach handling and cross-border rules — based on the published draft.

In the meantime, protections are fragmentary: constitutional privacy of correspondence, general Civil Code provisions, and sector rules such as the E-Commerce Law 2019's consumer data provisions and telecom regulations. There is no supervisory authority, registration duty, breach notification requirement or administrative fining regime for personal data today.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Consent-based processing (proposed) — The draft LPDP builds processing around consent and defined lawful grounds, with stricter treatment of sensitive personal data.
  • 02
    Data subject rights (proposed) — The draft creates mechanisms for individuals to exercise rights over their data, including access and correction, with complaint routes to the regulator.
  • 03
    MPTC oversight (proposed) — The MPTC would act as regulator with auditing, monitoring and enforcement powers over controllers and processors once the law and its two-year transition take effect.
