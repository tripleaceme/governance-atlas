Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

San Marino's Law No. 171 of 21 December 2018 is a comprehensive data protection statute closely modelled on the GDPR — it reproduces the Regulation's principles, lawful bases, data subject rights, accountability obligations and breach-notification regime. A consolidated text incorporating amendments through 2023 is published by the Great and General Council and by the supervisory authority.

The law established an independent supervisory authority, the Autorità Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali, a collegial body of three members supported by an office. It holds investigative, corrective, authorisation and advisory powers, including the distinctive ability to grant an infringer a deadline of up to six months to come into compliance before or alongside sanctions (Article 73). Controllers must notify personal data breaches presenting risk to individuals without undue delay and, where possible, within 72 hours.

Despite its GDPR-inspired framework, San Marino does not hold an EU adequacy decision, so EEA organisations transferring data to San Marino still need Chapter V safeguards. Enforcement activity is modest and mostly guidance-oriented, consistent with the microstate's size.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR-mirrored processing principles — Process personal data lawfully, fairly and transparently, with purpose limitation, minimisation and defined retention, and honour GDPR-style data subject rights.
  • 02
    Breach notification — Notify the Garante of risk-presenting breaches without undue delay and where possible within 72 hours; inform individuals when risk is high.
  • 03
    Accountability documentation — Maintain records of processing and conduct impact assessments for high-risk processing, in line with the law's GDPR-mirroring provisions.
  • 04
    DPO appointment — Designate a Data Protection Officer in the mandatory cases mirroring GDPR Article 37.
