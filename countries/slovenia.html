Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Slovenia is an EU member state, so the GDPR has applied directly since May 2018. However, Slovenia was famously the last EU country to pass a national GDPR implementing act: the Personal Data Protection Act (ZVOP-2) was adopted in December 2022 and entered into force on 26 January 2023. Until then, the Information Commissioner could not impose GDPR administrative fines because Slovenia lacked the domestic procedural machinery; full and systematic enforcement has only been possible since ZVOP-2 took effect.

ZVOP-2 supplements the GDPR with national specifics: a consent age of 15 for children in information society services, detailed data-security duties including traceability logs for certain processing, rules on video surveillance and biometrics, and localised fine provisions enforced through the fast-track misdemeanour procedure. The Information Commissioner (IP-RS) is a well-regarded, active authority that also handles freedom-of-information matters.

For anyone doing business in Slovenia, compliance is essentially GDPR compliance plus the ZVOP-2 national add-ons. Enforcement has stepped up markedly since 2023, with the IP-RS now conducting audits and imposing fines directly.

Key obligations

  • 01
    GDPR core compliance — Apply all GDPR principles, lawful bases, data subject rights and accountability duties, which apply directly in Slovenia.
  • 02
    Data security and traceability — ZVOP-2 imposes additional security requirements, including processing (traceability) logs for certain automated processing so access and disclosure can be audited.
  • 03
    Breach notification — Notify the Information Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of a reportable personal data breach; inform affected individuals when risk is high.
  • 04
    DPO appointment — Appoint a Data Protection Officer where GDPR Article 37 triggers apply (public sector, large-scale monitoring or special-category data).
  • 05
    Child consent verification — Obtain parental consent for information society services offered to children under 15.
