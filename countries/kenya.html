Executive summary

6 min read · plain-English summary — not legal advice

The Data Protection Act, 2019 is Kenya's comprehensive privacy law, giving effect to the constitutional right to privacy under Article 31. It applies to controllers and processors handling personal data of people in Kenya, whether established in Kenya or abroad.

The Act closely tracks the GDPR: lawful bases for processing, data subject rights (access, correction, deletion, portability, objection), data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing, and an independent regulator — the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) — with enforcement and penalty powers.

Kenya's most distinctive feature is mandatory registration: controllers and processors above turnover and employee thresholds — and anyone in sensitive sectors like health, education, or financial services — must register with the ODPC and renew periodically. The ODPC has been notably active, issuing multi-million-shilling penalties since 2023.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Establish a lawful basis before processing — consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interest, public task, or legitimate interest.
  • 02
    Register with the ODPC if you exceed the thresholds or operate in a designated sector, and renew on schedule.
  • 03
    Conduct DPIAs for processing likely to result in high risk to data subjects, and consult the ODPC where risk remains.
  • 04
    Report breaches within 72 hours to the ODPC where there is a real risk of harm, and notify affected data subjects.
  • 05
    Justify cross-border transfers — prove appropriate safeguards or data subject consent to the ODPC before transferring personal data outside Kenya.
