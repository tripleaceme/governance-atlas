Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information (APPI), originally enacted in 2003 and substantially rewritten by amendments effective April 2022, is a mature, comprehensive privacy law applying to virtually all private-sector businesses handling personal information, with extraterritorial reach over foreign operators serving people in Japan. It is enforced by the independent Personal Information Protection Commission (PPC). Japan holds an EU adequacy decision (2019, reaffirmed after review), operating a mutual adequacy arrangement with the EU — a distinctive 'data free flow with trust' posture Japan has championed globally.

The APPI is less prescriptive than the GDPR: no DPO mandate, no general registration, and a purpose-notification model rather than strict legal bases. Its sharpest teeth are the mandatory breach regime (report qualifying incidents to the PPC promptly, with a final report within 30 days, or 60 for malicious acts, and notify affected individuals), rules on third-party provision (traceability records and the opt-out filing system), pseudonymized/anonymized data categories, and cross-border transfer conditions requiring consent, an adequate destination, or safeguards plus detailed disclosures about the destination country's regime. Corporate fines reach JPY 100 million for order violations and database theft.

A significant amendment was enacted in 2026 as part of the APPI's triennial review: it introduces protections for minors under 16, extends improper-use and improper-acquisition prohibitions to 'contactable personally referable information,' and moves individual breach notification to a more risk-based approach; provisions will phase in over the coming years. The PPC enforces mainly through guidance, investigations and binding orders rather than headline fines, but compliance expectations — especially around breach reporting and transfers — are taken seriously by Japanese business.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Purpose specification and use limitation — Specify the purpose of use, notify or publish it, and do not process personal information beyond that purpose without consent.
  • 02
    Breach reporting — Report qualifying breaches (sensitive data, financial harm risk, malicious acts, or 1,000+ affected persons) to the PPC — preliminary report promptly, final within 30/60 days — and notify affected individuals.
  • 03
    Third-party provision controls — Obtain consent or use the opt-out filing regime before providing personal data to third parties, and keep transfer/receipt records; sensitive data always requires consent.
  • 04
    Cross-border transfer conditions — Transfer abroad only with consent, to an adequate country, or with safeguards — accompanied by required information to data subjects about the destination regime.
  • 05
    Security management — Implement organizational, human, physical and technical security measures and supervise employees and contractors handling personal data.
