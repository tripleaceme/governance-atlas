Executive summary

Compiled from public sources · not legal advice

The Democratic Republic of Congo regulates personal data through its Digital Code, Ordinance-Law No. 23/010 of 13 March 2023 (published 11 April 2023) — an omnibus statute covering digital services, e-commerce, cybersecurity and, in Title III, a full personal data protection regime. This replaced a previously fragmented landscape and gives the DRC, for the first time, GDPR-inspired rules on lawful processing, data subject rights, and supervision, with the authority empowered to issue formal notices requiring violations to be remedied within as little as eight days.

The institutional side is the weak point. Articles 262–270 of the Code provide for a dedicated Autorité de Protection des Données (APD), but the Prime Ministerial decree needed to establish it has not been issued. Since a ministerial decree of August 2024, the APD's missions have been provisionally exercised by ARPTIC, the posts and telecoms regulator. The DRC also ratified the African Union (Malabo) Convention on cybersecurity and personal data protection, reinforcing its commitments.

For businesses this means the substantive obligations are law, but enforcement is transitional and guidance sparse: filings and interactions currently run through ARPTIC, penalty practice is undeveloped, and details such as breach deadlines and DPO thresholds are not yet well documented in accessible secondary sources. Companies in telecoms, fintech and mining services — the sectors most exposed — should monitor for the decree that will finally stand up the APD.

Key obligations

  • 01
    Process data lawfully under the Digital Code — Title III of Ordinance-Law 23/010 requires a lawful basis, fair and transparent processing, and respect for data subject rights across all digital activities.
  • 02
    Deal with the interim regulator — Declarations and data protection matters are currently handled by ARPTIC, which provisionally exercises the powers of the yet-to-be-created APD.
  • 03
    Comply with formal notices promptly — The authority can issue formal notice to end a breach with a compliance deadline not exceeding eight days.
  • 04
    Secure personal data — The Code imposes cybersecurity and data security duties alongside data protection obligations, reflecting its omnibus digital-law design.
